\section{Purpose and scope}

This note collects in a unified form a number of related observations concerning the definition of
exclusive event samples in GENEVA when several nested resolution variables are used.
The main aim is to distinguish clearly between
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item genuine leading-power (LP) approximations in the lower-multiplicity NNLO contribution,
\item kinematic effects induced by phase-space projections in the MC event definition,
\item locality requirements on the projection maps for distributions differential in lower resolution variables,
\item fiducial power corrections that can be restored at fixed order through projection-to-Born constructions,
\item and additional constraints induced by optional Born-level fiducial or generation cuts.
\end{enumerate}

The discussion is formulated for a lowest explicitly generated multiplicity \(N\), together with three
nested resolution variables,
\begin{equation}
\rnm(\Ph_N),\qquad
\rn(\Ph_{N+1}),\qquad
\rnp(\Ph_{N+2}),
\label{eq:nested-variables}
\end{equation}
with the unresolved limits
\begin{equation}
\rnm\to0:\ N\to N-1,\qquad
\rn\to0:\ N+1\to N,\qquad
\rnp\to0:\ N+2\to N+1.
\label{eq:unresolved-limits}
\end{equation}

The Born process at multiplicity \(N\) may be either finite or singular.
If it is finite, no lower generation cut is required.
If it is singular, or if one deliberately restricts the Born-like phase space, one may impose
\begin{equation}
\rnm(\Ph_N)>\rnm^{\cut}.
\label{eq:born-cut}
\end{equation}
It is useful to encode this optional restriction in a generic Born-level acceptance factor,
\begin{equation}
\ThetaB(\Ph_M)=
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{no lower Born-level restriction},\\[2mm]
\Theta\!\left(\rnm^{(M)}(\Ph_M)-\rnm^{\cut}\right),
& \text{if the restriction \eqref{eq:born-cut} is imposed},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:thetaB}
\end{equation}
where \(\rnm^{(M)}\) denotes the extension of the lower resolution variable to multiplicity \(M\).

At NNLO one then introduces the technical resolution cuts
\begin{equation}
\rn^{\cut},\qquad \rnp^{\cut}.
\label{eq:technical-cuts}
\end{equation}
The roles of the three cuts, when all are present, are conceptually different:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
cut & role \\
\midrule
\(\rnm^{\cut}\) & optional Born-level fiducial/generation cut \\
\(\rn^{\cut}\) & NNLO slicing/resolution cut between \(N\) and \(N+1\) \\
\(\rnp^{\cut}\) & higher-multiplicity separation inside the NLO \(N+1\) contribution \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{Measurement functions and projection maps}

For a generic observable we write
\begin{equation}
\Mcal(\Ph_M)
=
\ThetaB(\Ph_M)\,
\Mcal_{\rm phys}(\Ph_M),
\label{eq:measurement-full}
\end{equation}
with, for example,
\begin{equation}
\Mcal_{\rm phys}(\Ph_M)
=
\prod_{a=1}^{n_X}
\delta\!\left(X_a-X_a(\Ph_M)\right)
\,
\Theta_{\rm fid}^{\rm phys}(\Ph_M).
\label{eq:measurement-physical}
\end{equation}
The variables \(X_a\), collected into the vector
\(\mathbf X=(X_1,\ldots,X_{n_X})\), may be Born-like and completely inclusive over additional QCD radiation,
while still being changed by the projection maps.

We shall use
\begin{equation}
\Pi_N:\Ph_{N+1}\to\widehat\Ph_N,
\qquad
\Pi_{N+1}:\Ph_{N+2}\to\widehat\Ph_{N+1},
\label{eq:projection-maps}
\end{equation}
for the two successive projections.

Two logically distinct notions of map consistency will be important:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{differential locality}: preservation, at least at LP, of variables in which the singular lower-multiplicity prediction is kept differential;
\item \emph{fiducial-boundary preservation}: avoidance of migration across any lower-multiplicity cut that is part of the process definition.
\end{enumerate}
The first requirement can be relevant even when no generation cut exists; the second is conditional on such a boundary being present.

\section{Exact NLO higher-multiplicity contribution}

For \(\rn>\rn^{\cut}\), the NNLO construction contains the NLO prediction for multiplicity \(N+1\).
With a local subtraction term \(C_{N+2}\),
\begin{align}
\sigma_{N+1}^{\rm NLO}[\Mcal]
={}&
\int d\Ph_{N+1}\,
\left[
B_{N+1}+V_{N+1}+I_{N+1}
\right](\Ph_{N+1})
\Mcal(\Ph_{N+1})
\nonumber\\
&+
\int d\Ph_{N+2}\,
\Bigl[
R_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})\Mcal(\Ph_{N+2})
-
C_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})\Mcal(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})
\Bigr].
\label{eq:nlo-nplus1}
\end{align}

The important point is that this is a dynamically exact NLO calculation.
There is no replacement
\begin{equation}
R_{N+2}\longrightarrow R_{N+2}^{\rm LP}(\rnp)
\label{eq:no-lp-rnp}
\end{equation}
in this contribution.
Hence, in the setup considered here, there is no genuine dynamical LP truncation associated with
\(\rnp^{\cut}\).

\section{Fixed-order analysis versus LHE event generation dependence on  \texorpdfstring{\(r_{N+1}^{\rm cut}\)}{rN+1cut}}

The phase-space identity
\begin{equation}
1=
\Theta\!\left(\rnp<\rnp^{\cut}\right)
+
\Theta\!\left(\rnp>\rnp^{\cut}\right)
\label{eq:rnp-partition}
\end{equation}
is exact.
If both regions are analyzed with exact \(\Ph_{N+2}\) kinematics, the final NLO result is independent of
\(\rnp^{\cut}\).
In GENEVA this is precisely what happens in a fixed-order calculation with the
\emph{internal analyzer}: every contribution is filled using its exact partonic kinematics.
Equation~\eqref{eq:rnp-partition} is then only an internal partition of phase space and the dependence on
\(\rnp^{\cut}\) cancels, up to numerical integration errors. No fiducial-power-correction restoration is
needed for the \(N+2\to N+1\) step in this setup, because no kinematic information has been discarded.
This is the appropriate setup for validating the underlying fixed-order calculation.

The situation relevant for MC event generation is different. When events are generated for output to
the LHEF file, configurations below \(\rnp^{\cut}\) are represented as \((N+1)\)-parton events rather
than as resolved \((N+2)\)-parton configurations. Their kinematics are therefore projected according to
\begin{equation}
\Ph_{N+2}\to\widehat\Ph_{N+1}=\Pi_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+2}),
\label{eq:rnp-projection}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
\Mcal(\Ph_{N+2})\to\Mcal(\widehat\Ph_{N+1}).
\label{eq:rnp-measurement-replacement}
\end{equation}
The difference between the LHE event definition and the exact fixed-order result is therefore
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}[\Mcal]
=
\int d\Ph_{N+2}\,
R_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})
\Theta\!\left(\rnp(\Ph_{N+2})<\rnp^{\cut}\right)
\left[
\Mcal(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})-\Mcal(\Ph_{N+2})
\right].
\label{eq:rnp-proj-correction}
\end{equation}
This is an observable-dependent kinematic effect induced by the LHE projection. It should not be
confused with the fiducial power corrections appearing in a projection-to-Born construction for the
lower \(N+1\to N\) projection at fixed-order NNLO.

\section{Observables preserved and violated by the  NLO projection}

Suppose a particular Born-like variable \(X_i\) is exactly preserved by \(\Pi_{N+1}\),
\begin{equation}
X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})=X_i(\Ph_{N+2}).
\label{eq:x-preserved}
\end{equation}
Then for a distribution depending only on preserved variables and on variables integrated over their full ranges,
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{dX_i}=0.
\label{eq:x-preserved-zero}
\end{equation}

In general, however, preserving one Born-like variable does not imply preservation of all the others.
When focusing on one specific component \(X_i\) of \(\mathbf X\), we denote the remaining Born-like variables
collectively by \(\mathbf X_{\neq i}\). Consider, for example,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i},
\label{eq:rn-x-spectrum}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\rn(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})=\rn(\Ph_{N+2}),
\qquad
X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})\neq X_i(\Ph_{N+2}).
\label{eq:rn-preserved-x-not}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
={}&
\int d\Ph_{N+2}\,
R_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})
\Theta\!\left(\rnp<\rnp^{\cut}\right)
\delta\!\left(\rn-\rn(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\nonumber\\
&\times
\left[
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})\right)
-
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\right].
\label{eq:rn-x-proj}
\end{align}

The cancellation after integrating over the \emph{full} range of \(X_i\) is immediate. If the integration
range contains both the exact and projected values of \(X_i\) for every phase-space point, then
\begin{equation}
\int_{\rm full} dX_i\,
\left[
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})\right)
-
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\right]
=1-1=0.
\label{eq:x-full-range-cancellation}
\end{equation}
Hence, provided \(\rn\) is itself preserved by the projection,
\begin{equation}
\int_{\rm full} dX_i\,
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
=0.
\label{eq:x-integrated-zero}
\end{equation}
The interpretation is simple: the projection can move an event from one value of \(X_i\) to another, but
it does not change the total weight. Once all values of \(X_i\) are summed inclusively, this migration is
invisible.

This cancellation is lost as soon as the integration over \(X_i\) is not fully inclusive. To make this
explicit, introduce an acceptance function \(A_{X_i}(X_i)\), equal to one in the accepted fiducial region and
zero outside. Integrating Eq.~\eqref{eq:rn-x-proj} with this acceptance gives
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma^{\rm fid}}{d\rn}
={}&
\int d\Ph_{N+2}\,
R_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})
\Theta\!\left(\rnp<\rnp^{\cut}\right)
\delta\!\left(\rn-\rn(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\nonumber\\
&\times
\left[
A_{X_i}\!\left(X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})\right)
-
A_{X_i}\!\left(X_i(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\right].
\label{eq:x-fiducial-migration}
\end{align}
Thus a fiducial cut does not merely reduce the integration range: it converts the trivial \(1-1\)
cancellation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:x-full-range-cancellation} into a difference of two event classifications.
The contribution is nonzero precisely for configurations for which the exact and projected kinematics
lie on opposite sides of a fiducial boundary.

For a single lower cut,
\begin{equation}
A_{X_i}(X_i)=\Theta(X_i-X_{i,\rm cut}),
\label{eq:x-lower-acceptance}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:x-fiducial-migration} contains the factor
\begin{equation}
\Theta\!\left(X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})-X_{i,\rm cut}\right)
-
\Theta\!\left(X_i(\Ph_{N+2})-X_{i,\rm cut}\right).
\label{eq:x-cut-classification-difference}
\end{equation}
Define the migration in \(X_i\) as
\begin{equation}
\Delta X_i
=
X_i(\Ph_{N+2})-X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1}),
\label{eq:delta-x-fiducial}
\end{equation}
for a small migration one obtains
\begin{equation}
\Theta\!\left(X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})-X_{i,\rm cut}\right)
-
\Theta\!\left(X_i(\Ph_{N+2})-X_{i,\rm cut}\right)
\simeq
-\Delta X_i\,
\delta\!\left(X_i(\Ph_{N+2})-X_{i,\rm cut}\right).
\label{eq:x-boundary-localization}
\end{equation}
Therefore the projection effect from a hard fiducial cut is localized at the cut boundary. Its size is
controlled by the amount by which the projection moves \(X_i\), multiplied by the event density at the
boundary. This makes the effect potentially important even when \(\Delta X_i\) is parametrically small:
a steep spectrum or a large cross section near the fiducial boundary can enhance the resulting migration.
For a two-sided window the same reasoning applies independently at both boundaries, with opposite signs.

More generally, if several components of \(\mathbf X\) are changed by the projection, integrating over the
full range of one of them removes only the migration associated with that component. Any remaining differential
measurement or fiducial cut on another non-preserved component can still produce a nonzero projection
effect. The actual criterion for cancellation is therefore invariance of the \emph{full measurement
function}, not merely inclusiveness with respect to QCD radiation. Likewise, if \(\rn\) itself is not
preserved, integrating over \(X_i\) does not eliminate migration in the \(\rn\) distribution.

\section{How a higher cut can contaminate a lower singular spectrum}
\label{sec:higher-singular-spectrum}

Throughout this section the lower resolution variable $\rn$ is kept differential.  The object of interest is
therefore the projection-induced modification of the singular spectrum
$d\sigma/(d\rn\,dX_i)$ at fixed $\rn$.  No integration over $\rn$ is used here to infer the size of a final
NNLO distribution differential only in $X_i$.  That separate step, and the resulting dependence on the
technical cuts after the $\rn$ integration, are discussed only in
Sec.~\ref{sec:nnlo-differential-predictions}.


The purpose of this section is to understand a potentially important feature of the LHE projection discussed
above. Although the difference between the exact \((N+2)\)-parton kinematics and the projected
\((N+1)\)-parton kinematics vanishes in the \(\rnp\to0\) limit, this does not by itself imply that the
resulting correction is nonsingular as a function of the lower resolution variable \(\rn\).

\subsection{Strongly ordered nested limit}
\label{subsec:strongly-ordered-higher-projection}

The relevant region for the analysis in this subsection is the nested unresolved limit
\begin{equation}
\rnp\ll\rn\ll Q,
\label{eq:strong-ordering}
\end{equation}
where the two emissions are strongly ordered in resolution scale. Here \(Q\) denotes a generic hard scale
characterizing the underlying \((N-1)\)-parton process and remaining finite in the nested unresolved limits.
Depending on the process, it may be an invariant mass, a hard transverse momentum, or another characteristic
short-distance scale. Its precise definition is immaterial for the argument below; it simply provides the
hard reference scale with respect to which \(\rn\) is parametrically small.

In this region the singular part of the \((N+2)\)-parton cross section can be viewed, at leading power, as
the lower-multiplicity hard spectrum, differential in the Born-like variables \(\mathbf X\), multiplied by
two successive unresolved-emission factors: one associated with the \(N+1\to N\) limit and one with the
further \(N+2\to N+1\) limit.

For resolution variables with the usual soft/collinear scaling, a single unresolved emission produces a
logarithmic measure of the form \(d r_i/r_i\), together with splitting kernels, angular variables, colour
factors and couplings. Equivalently, at fixed order the singular terms generated by one unresolved limit
have the familiar schematic form \(\ln^m(r_i/Q_i)/r_i\). Iterating two strongly ordered unresolved limits
therefore produces a leading singular structure containing one inverse power of each resolution variable.
We focus on the migration of one particular component \(X_i\) of \(\mathbf X\), while
\(\mathbf X_{\neq i}\) denotes all the remaining Born-like variables. We use \(\Ph_{\rm rad}\) collectively
for the remaining radiation variables associated with the unresolved emissions, including energy fractions,
angles, recoil variables, and any other coordinates needed to parametrize the corresponding splittings.
This gives schematically
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma_{N+2}}
{d\rn\,d\rnp\,dX_i\,d\mathbf X_{\neq i}\,d\Ph_{\rm rad}}
\sim
\frac{1}{\rn}\frac{1}{\rnp}
F\!\left(
\mathbf X,\Ph_{\rm rad},
\ln\frac{\rn}{Q},
\ln\frac{\rnp}{\rn}
\right).
\label{eq:double-singular}
\end{equation}
The first factor \(1/\rn\) is associated with resolving the \((N+1)\)-parton configuration relative to the
underlying \(N\)-parton state, while \(1/\rnp\) is the additional singularity associated with the more
unresolved \(N+2\to N+1\) emission. The logarithms have been written in terms of the two hierarchical ratios
\(\rn/Q\) and \(\rnp/\rn\), which directly expose the nested ordering. One could equivalently use
\(\rnp/Q\), since its logarithm is a linear combination of these two logarithms.

Equation~\eqref{eq:double-singular} is meant as a schematic leading-power form, not as a universal statement
for arbitrary definitions of the resolution variables. The precise powers and logarithms depend on their
canonical scaling and on the detailed factorization theorem. What matters for the argument below is that the
higher unresolved singular measure in \(\rnp\) multiplies a spectrum that is already singular in the lower
resolution variable \(\rn\).

The projection mismatch \(\Delta X_i\) has already been defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta-x-fiducial}. To
characterize its size, introduce a nonsingular characteristic scale \(X_{i,\rm char}\) associated with the
particular Born-like variable \(X_i\) whose migration is being studied. Its role is only to render the relative
shift \(\Delta X_i/X_{i,\rm char}\) dimensionless. The scale \(X_{i,\rm char}\) may depend on the underlying
Born kinematics \(\mathbf X\), but it should remain finite and nonzero in the region in which the scaling is
considered. In particular, it should not generically be identified with \(X_i\) itself, since \(X_i\) may
vanish in an otherwise regular region of phase space.

If the map approaches the exact kinematics smoothly, the relative migration can then have a generic nested
scaling
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta X_i}{X_{i,\rm char}}
\sim
c_i(\mathbf X,\Ph_{\rm rad})
\left(\frac{\rnp}{\rn}\right)^p,
\qquad p>0,
\label{eq:delta-x-scaling}
\end{equation}
where \(c_i\) is a nonsingular dimensionless coefficient and \(p\) is nonuniversal and depends on the
resolution variables and recoil prescription. Equation~\eqref{eq:delta-x-scaling} should therefore be
understood only as a parametrization of how rapidly the projection becomes local in \(X_i\) as the second
emission becomes unresolved relative to the first one; it does not assume that the absolute shift
\(\Delta X_i\) is set by \(\rnp\) itself. In particular, Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta-x-scaling} isolates the dependence
on the strong-ordering ratio \(\rnp/\rn\); it does not by itself determine whether the coefficient multiplying
this ratio receives an additional suppression when \(\rn/Q\to0\). That separate question will be addressed
in Sec.~\ref{subsec:uniform-nested-locality}.

The origin of the migration derivative can be seen directly by expanding the measurement delta. Since
\(X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})=X_i(\Ph_{N+2})-\Delta X_i\),
\[
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})\right)
-
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\simeq
\Delta X_i\,\partial_{X_i}
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\Ph_{N+2})\right).
\]
Combining this expansion with Eqs.~\eqref{eq:double-singular} and \eqref{eq:delta-x-scaling}, and integrating
the higher unresolved region up to \(\rnp^{\cut}\), gives parametrically
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
\sim
\frac{1}{\rn}
\left(\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}\right)^p
\sum_{m,k}
\partial_{X_i}
\left[
X_{i,\rm char}\,
C_{mk}(\mathbf X)
\ln^m\!\frac{\rn}{Q}
\ln^k\!\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}
\right].
\label{eq:rnp-contamination-rn}
\end{equation}
Here the coefficients \(C_{mk}(\mathbf X)\) contain the remaining nonsingular Born-kinematic dependence,
the coefficient \(c_i\) of the projection mismatch, and the integrations over the radiation variables.
The characteristic migration scale \(X_{i,\rm char}\) and the derivative generated by the displacement in
\(X_i\) are instead kept explicit. The logarithms display separately the two hierarchies \(\rn/Q\) and
\(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn\).

\medskip
\noindent
\fbox{%
\parbox{0.94\linewidth}{%
\textbf{Key point.}
Power suppression with respect to the \emph{higher} unresolved limit does not imply that the
corresponding projection effect is nonsingular in the \emph{lower} resolution variable.
The reason is that the projection mismatch multiplies a spectrum that is already singular in
\(\rn\). Thus, even if the relative mismatch vanishes as
\((\rnp/\rn)^p\) for \(\rnp/\rn\to0\), after integrating the higher unresolved region up to
\(\rnp^{\cut}\) it produces an absolute correction scaling parametrically as
\(\rn^{-1}(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn)^p\), multiplied by the explicit migration operator
\(X_{i,\rm char}\partial_{X_i}\), logarithms, and nonsingular coefficients.
The correction is therefore suppressed relative to the leading singular spectrum at fixed
\(\rn\), but its convergence is nonuniform as \(\rn\) becomes small.
}%
}
\medskip

For \(p=1\), the origin of the additional inverse power of \(\rn\) can be seen directly. Suppressing only
the logarithmic variation and the Born-dependent migration factor for this intermediate power-counting step,
\[
\frac{1}{\rn}
\int_0^{\rnp^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rnp}{\rnp}
\left(\frac{\rnp}{\rn}\right)
=
\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn^2}.
\]
Restoring all factors, the \(p=1\) specialization of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rnp-contamination-rn} is therefore
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
&\sim
\frac{1}{\rn}
\left(\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}\right)
\sum_{m,k}
\partial_{X_i}
\left[
X_{i,\rm char}\,
C_{mk}(\mathbf X)
\ln^m\!\frac{\rn}{Q}
\ln^k\!\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}
\right]
\nonumber\\
&=
\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn^2}
\sum_{m,k}
\partial_{X_i}
\left[
X_{i,\rm char}\,
C_{mk}(\mathbf X)
\ln^m\!\frac{\rn}{Q}
\ln^k\!\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}
\right].
\label{eq:p1-scaling}
\end{align}
The factor \(X_{i,\rm char}\partial_{X_i}\) originates directly from expanding the measurement function
under the displacement \(\Delta X_i\). More precisely, the derivative acts on the full Born-dependent bracket:
\(X_{i,\rm char}\), \(C_{mk}\), and, when the chosen hard scale depends on the Born kinematics, \(Q\) may
all carry \(X_i\) dependence. The coefficients \(C_{mk}\) contain the remaining nonsingular dependence,
including the coefficient \(c_i\) and the integrations over the radiation variables; they do not absorb the
characteristic migration scale or the migration derivative.

Equation~\eqref{eq:p1-scaling} should not be interpreted as a genuine \(1/\rn^2\) singularity that can be
extrapolated to \(\rn\to0\) at fixed \(\rnp^{\cut}\). It is an asymptotic estimate valid only in the strongly
ordered region \mbox{$Q\gg\rn\gg\rnp^{\cut}$}.
In that region the underlying lower-resolution spectrum supplies
the factor \(1/\rn\), while the integration over the higher unresolved emission together with the linear
projection mismatch supplies the additional relative factor \(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn\). Thus at any fixed
\(\rn>0\) the correction vanishes when \(\rnp^{\cut}\to0\), but the convergence is nonuniform in \(\rn\):
the relevant expansion parameter is \(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn\), not \(\rnp^{\cut}/Q\). Once
\(\rn\) becomes parametrically comparable to \(\rnp^{\cut}\), the strong-ordering expansion used to derive
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rnp-contamination-rn}--\eqref{eq:p1-scaling} breaks down.

The hard scale \(Q\) therefore remains important through the logarithmic structure of the underlying
\(\rn\) spectrum, while the additional power suppression induced by the projection is controlled by the ratio
of the two neighboring resolution scales, \(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn\).


\subsection{Comparable or inverted cuts and uniform nested infrared locality}
\label{subsec:uniform-nested-locality}

The strong-ordering estimate of Sec.~\ref{subsec:strongly-ordered-higher-projection} is a statement at
fixed $\rn$ and cannot be extrapolated to configurations with a finite neighboring ratio
$z=\rnp/\rn$.  At fixed $\rn$, if $\rn\gg\rnp^{\cut}$ the higher technical cut enforces
$z\ll1$, whereas for $\rn$ comparable to or smaller than $\rnp^{\cut}$ it need not do so.  Particular
resolution variables can impose additional kinematic restrictions on the finite allowed range of $z$.
The singular spectrum in this region must therefore be analyzed directly in the simultaneous unresolved
limit, independently of the later integration over $\rn$.

\subsubsection{Uniform nested infrared locality of the map}

To formulate the map property generically, introduce
\begin{equation}
\epsilon=\frac{\rn}{Q},
\qquad
z=\frac{\rnp}{\rn},
\label{eq:epsilon-z-def}
\end{equation}
and consider a family of \((N+2)\)-parton configurations
\(\Ph_{N+2}(\epsilon,z,\omega)\), where the underlying \(N\)-body coordinates are held fixed and
\(\omega\) denotes dimensionless splitting, angular and recoil variables. The simultaneous unresolved
limit is
\begin{equation}
\epsilon\to0,
\qquad
z=\mathcal O(1),
\label{eq:simultaneous-unresolved}
\end{equation}
where \(z\) is understood to remain finite inside the kinematically allowed domain of the particular
resolution variables. Thus \(\rn\) and \(\rnp\) approach zero together rather than hierarchically.

It is useful to distinguish clearly a property of the projection map from a property of the measured
observable. Ordinary one-step infrared locality of \(\Pi_{N+1}\) requires that, at fixed nonzero \(\rn\),
removing the additional unresolved emission sends the projected configuration to the same \((N+1)\)-body
infrared limit as the exact configuration. This controls \(\rnp\to0\) at fixed \(\rn\), but does not by
itself guarantee uniform behavior when the projected \((N+1)\)-body state simultaneously approaches its own
\(N\)-body unresolved limit.

We shall say that \(\Pi_{N+1}\) has \emph{uniform nested infrared locality} if, whenever an exact
\((N+2)\)-parton configuration approaches an underlying \(N\)-parton configuration in the simultaneous
limit of Eq.~\eqref{eq:simultaneous-unresolved}, the projected \((N+1)\)-parton configuration approaches the
\emph{same} underlying \(N\)-parton point, uniformly for finite kinematically allowed values of \(z\).
Equivalently, for every finite set of smooth IRC-safe coordinates
\(\mathbf Y=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_s)\) that characterize the relevant lower-multiplicity kinematics, with finite
nonsingular characteristic scales \(Y_{a,\rm char}\), the map must satisfy
\begin{equation}
\lim_{\epsilon\to0}
\sup_{0\le z\le z_*}
\max_a
\left|
\frac{
Y_a(\Ph_{N+2}(\epsilon,z,\omega))
-Y_a(\Pi_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+2}(\epsilon,z,\omega)))
}{Y_{a,\rm char}}
\right|
=0,
\label{eq:uniform-nested-locality-definition}
\end{equation}
for every fixed \(z_*\) whose interval lies inside the kinematically allowed simultaneous-unresolved region,
and for fixed \(\omega\) away from additional singular boundaries. No universal numerical value of
\(z_*\) is assumed here. The quantification over lower-multiplicity coordinates is what makes
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform-nested-locality-definition} a property of the map rather than of one chosen observable.
The uniformity in \(z\) is essential: a map that is local only for \(z\to0\) but approaches a different
lower-multiplicity point along some allowed path with finite \(z\) does not satisfy uniform nested infrared
locality.

The qualifier \emph{nested} refers to the fact that the \(N+2\to N+1\) projection must remain local not only
with respect to its own unresolved emission, but also while the projected \((N+1)\)-parton state itself
approaches the next lower \(N\)-parton limit. The qualifier \emph{uniform} stresses that this agreement must
persist for finite allowed values of \(z\), rather than only after taking \(z\to0\) first. Operationally, the
two operations ``remove the higher unresolved emission'' and ``approach the lower unresolved configuration''
must lead to the same underlying \(N\)-body kinematics.

Once the map has uniform nested infrared locality, ordinary IRC safety of a particular observable \(X_i\) is
enough to imply
\begin{equation}
\Delta X_i
=
X_i(\Ph_{N+2})-X_i(\Pi_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+2}))
\longrightarrow0
\qquad
(\epsilon\to0,\ z\text{ finite and allowed}).
\label{eq:irc-safe-consequence-uniform-map}
\end{equation}
Thus the vanishing of \(\Delta X_i\) in the simultaneous limit should not be regarded as an additional
independent requirement on every IRC-safe observable. What IRC safety alone does \emph{not} guarantee is
that an arbitrary projection map approaches the same lower-multiplicity point, nor does it determine the
rate at which the difference in Eq.~\eqref{eq:irc-safe-consequence-uniform-map} vanishes.

When this approach is power-like, the latter rate can be parametrized as
\begin{equation}
H_i(\epsilon,z,\omega)
\equiv
\frac{\Delta X_i}{X_{i,\rm char}}
=
\epsilon^{q_i}
 h_i(z,\omega)
+\mathcal O(\epsilon^{q_i+\delta_i}),
\qquad q_i>0,
\label{eq:uniform-nested-locality-power}
\end{equation}
with \(h_i\) finite for finite allowed \(z\). The exponent \(q_i\) is not fixed by IRC safety. It depends on
the recoil prescription, on the resolution variables and on how the particular observable responds to the
small kinematic displacement. The approach need not even be a pure power; Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform-nested-locality-power}
is a convenient parametrization when a power expansion exists. In the overlap with the strongly ordered
limit one expects
\begin{equation}
h_i(z,\omega)\sim z^{p_i}
\qquad (z\to0),
\label{eq:uniform-nested-strong-overlap}
\end{equation}
up to logarithms and nonsingular coefficients. Equations~\eqref{eq:uniform-nested-locality-power} and
\eqref{eq:uniform-nested-strong-overlap} make explicit a feature that was not fixed by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta-x-scaling}: the coefficient of the strong-ordering suppression may itself vanish when
the lower resolution scale becomes unresolved. The strongly ordered estimate therefore describes the
\(z\)-dependence but need not give the complete power counting in the common unresolved scale.

A convenient schematic form of the singular measure in the simultaneous region is
\begin{equation}
d\sigma_{N+2}^{\rm sing}
\sim
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}\,
d\mu(z,\omega)\,
\mathcal F\!\left(
\mathbf X,\epsilon,z,\omega,
\ln\epsilon
\right),
\label{eq:simultaneous-singular-measure}
\end{equation}
where \(d\mu(z,\omega)\) contains the remaining unresolved-emission measure and reduces to a logarithmic
\(dz/z\)-type measure in the strongly ordered \(z\to0\) limit. No expansion in \(z\) is made when \(z\) is
finite. Combining Eq.~\eqref{eq:simultaneous-singular-measure} with the power-like realization of uniform
nested infrared locality gives schematically
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
\sim
\frac{1}{\rn}
\left(\frac{\rn}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
\partial_{X_i}
\left[
X_{i,\rm char}\,
G_i\!\left(
\mathbf X,
\ln\frac{\rn}{Q};
 z_{\max}
\right)
\right],
\label{eq:uniform-nested-locality-spectrum}
\end{equation}
where \(G_i\) denotes the integral over \(z\) and the remaining radiation variables, including \(h_i\), and
\(z_{\max}\) is set jointly by the higher technical cut and the kinematic range. Uniform nested infrared
locality requires the underlying map mismatch to remain uniformly small over any finite allowed range of
\(z\); under the power-like assumption above, \(G_i\) remains parametrically finite and the suppression is
supplied by the common lower scale \((\rn/Q)^{q_i}\), not by requiring \(z\) itself to be small.


\subsubsection{Why fixed-order NNLO slicing does not require uniform nested locality}
\label{subsec:fixed-order-does-not-require-uniform-nested-locality}

Uniform nested infrared locality is stronger than what is required of an auxiliary map used only inside the NLO part above the cut for a
fixed-order NNLO slicing calculation. A concrete counterexample can be constructed by starting from any ordinary one-step
IR-local map \(\Pi_{N+1}^{(0)}\), and composing it with a smooth recoil transformation
\(\mathcal R_\alpha\) acting on the projected \((N+1)\)-body state,
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{N+1}^{\rm bad}
=
\mathcal R_{\alpha(x,y)}\circ\Pi_{N+1}^{(0)},
\qquad
x=\frac{\rn}{Q},
\qquad
y=\frac{\rnp}{Q},
\qquad
\alpha(x,y)=\frac{y}{x+y}.
\label{eq:bad-map-definition}
\end{equation}
Assume that \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) preserves the on-shell and momentum-conservation constraints of the
\((N+1)\)-body phase space and changes one smooth lower-multiplicity coordinate \(Y\) according to
\begin{equation}
Y\!\left(\mathcal R_\alpha(\Ph_{N+1})\right)
=
Y(\Ph_{N+1})
+
\alpha\,Y_{\rm char}
+
\mathcal O(\alpha^2),
\label{eq:bad-map-recoil}
\end{equation}
with a finite nonsingular scale \(Y_{\rm char}\). At fixed nonzero \(x\),
\begin{equation}
y\to0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha(x,y)\to0,
\label{eq:bad-map-one-step-local}
\end{equation}
so \(\Pi_{N+1}^{\rm bad}\) has exactly the same one-step \(N+2\to N+1\) infrared limit as
\(\Pi_{N+1}^{(0)}\). It is therefore perfectly admissible from the point of view of local NLO subtraction,
provided the usual phase-space factorization and invertibility requirements are satisfied.

In the simultaneous unresolved limit, however, taking \(y=z x\) gives
\begin{equation}
\alpha(x,zx)=\frac{z}{1+z},
\label{eq:bad-map-simultaneous-alpha}
\end{equation}
which remains finite as \(x\to0\) for any fixed nonzero \(z\). Hence
\begin{equation}
\frac{
Y(\Ph_{N+2})
-Y\!\left(\Pi_{N+1}^{\rm bad}(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
}{Y_{\rm char}}
\longrightarrow
-\frac{z}{1+z}
\label{eq:bad-map-violates-nested-locality}
\end{equation}
up to the convention for the sign of the recoil. The map is one-step IR local but violates uniform nested
infrared locality maximally: exact and projected configurations approach different points of the limiting
\(N\)-body phase space along a finite-\(z\) path.

This does not spoil the fixed-order NLO calculation of the \((N+1)\)-body contribution that enters an NNLO
calculation. Schematically, for \(\rn>\rn^{\cut}\), one has
\begin{align}
\sigma_{N+1}^{\rm NLO}[\Ocal]
={}&
\int_{\rn>\rn^{\cut}}d\Ph_{N+2}
\Bigl[
R_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})\Ocal(\Ph_{N+2})
-
C_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})
\Ocal\!\left(\Pi_{N+1}^{\rm bad}(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\Bigr]
\nonumber\\
&+
\int_{\rn>\rn^{\cut}}d\Ph_{N+1}
\Bigl[V_{N+1}+I_{N+1}^{\rm bad}\Bigr](\Ph_{N+1})
\Ocal(\Ph_{N+1}),
\label{eq:bad-map-subtracted-nlo}
\end{align}
where \(I_{N+1}^{\rm bad}\) is obtained by integrating the same counterterm with the same map. The counterterm
has only been added and subtracted. For every fixed nonzero \(\rn^{\cut}\), ordinary one-step locality is
sufficient to make the real-minus-counterterm contribution locally integrable in the
\(\rnp\to0\) limit. The map dependence cancels between the subtracted real contribution and its integrated
counterpart, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:bad-map-subtracted-nlo} is the exact NLO result independently of the bad
simultaneous-limit behavior of \(\Pi_{N+1}^{\rm bad}\).

Embedding this exact NLO contribution in a sliced NNLO calculation therefore does not require the higher map
to be uniformly nested-local. The lower region \(\rn<\rn^{\cut}\) is supplied by the separate
lower-multiplicity NNLO contribution, while the higher-multiplicity NLO calculation only requires the
ordinary \(\rnp\to0\) cancellation at fixed resolved \(\rn>\rn^{\cut}\). Individual subtraction pieces may
develop increasingly large and numerically delicate cancellations as \(\rn^{\cut}\) is reduced, but this is
a subtraction-scheme issue rather than a loss of formal NNLO accuracy.

The situation changes when the projected \((N+1)\)-body configuration is used as the physical representative
of the event below \(\rnp^{\cut}\). In that case the replacement
\begin{equation}
\Ocal(\Ph_{N+2})
\longrightarrow
\Ocal\!\left(\Pi_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\label{eq:physical-projection-replacement}
\end{equation}
is part of the event definition. There is no compensating integrated counterterm that restores the discarded
measurement on the exact \(\Ph_{N+2}\) kinematics. A failure of uniform nested locality therefore becomes a
genuine projection effect in the LHE event sample even though the same map could be entirely legitimate as
an auxiliary fixed-order subtraction map. This is the precise sense in which the mapping requirements for
GENEVA event generation are stronger than those required for formal fixed-order NNLO accuracy.

\subsubsection{Kinematic restriction for minimized \(N\)-jettiness}

For an axis-minimized definition of \(N\)-jettiness, increasing the number of final-state jet axes enlarges
the space over which the observable is minimized. At fixed final-state parton multiplicity \(M\), this
immediately implies the monotonicity relation
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_{K+1}(\Ph_M)
\le
\mathcal T_K(\Ph_M),
\label{eq:njettiness-monotonicity}
\end{equation}
for every admissible \(K\). A stronger quantitative bound follows from the same minimization property.
Let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_K(\Ph_M)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{M} t_i^{(K)},
\qquad
t_i^{(K)}\ge0,
\label{eq:njettiness-parton-contributions}
\end{equation}
be the decomposition into the nonnegative parton contributions evaluated for a set of axes that minimizes
\(\mathcal T_K\). At least one contribution satisfies
\(t_{\max}^{(K)}\ge \mathcal T_K/M\). In the \((K+1)\)-axis minimization one may keep all the minimizing
\(K\) axes and add one extra axis aligned with the parton that gives \(t_{\max}^{(K)}\). Its contribution
then vanishes, while the minimization over an enlarged set of directions cannot increase the contributions
of the other partons. Hence, provided the axis minimization admits this alignment,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal T_{K+1}(\Ph_M)
\le
\frac{M-1}{M}\,
\mathcal T_K(\Ph_M)
}.
\label{eq:njettiness-adjacent-bound}
\end{equation}
More generally, adding \(s\) axes aligned with the \(s\) largest contributions to the minimizing
\(K\)-axis configuration gives
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_{K+s}(\Ph_M)
\le
\frac{M-s}{M}\,
\mathcal T_K(\Ph_M),
\label{eq:njettiness-multiaxis-bound}
\end{equation}
whenever the corresponding \((K+s)\)-axis minimization is admissible.

Equation~(61) of arXiv:2312.06496 is the \(K=0\), \(s=1\) specialization,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathcal T_1(\Ph_M)}{\mathcal T_0(\Ph_M)}
\le
c_M,
\qquad
c_M\equiv\frac{M-1}{M}.
\label{eq:t1-t0-multiplicity-bound}
\end{equation}
Thus \(c_2=1/2\) on a two-parton configuration and \(c_3=2/3\) on a three-parton configuration. For the
neighboring pair relevant to a generic nested step on \(\Ph_{N+2}\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:njettiness-adjacent-bound}
corresponds to \(K=N\), \(M=N+2\), and therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathcal T_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+2})}
{\mathcal T_N(\Ph_{N+2})}
\le
\frac{N+1}{N+2}.
\label{eq:njettiness-nested-pair-bound}
\end{equation}
These quantitative bounds rely on the axis-minimized definition of \(N\)-jettiness and are not assumed for
generic resolution variables.

At fixed multiplicity \(M\), the region below a technical \(\mathcal T_1\) cut is therefore
\begin{equation}
0\le\mathcal T_1
<
\min\!\left\{
\mathcal T_1^{\cut},\,
c_M\,\mathcal T_0
\right\},
\label{eq:t1-t0-effective-upper-bound}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
z_{\max}
=
\min\!\left\{
\frac{\mathcal T_1^{\cut}}{\mathcal T_0},\,
c_M
\right\},
\qquad
z\equiv\frac{\mathcal T_1}{\mathcal T_0}.
\label{eq:t1-t0-zmax}
\end{equation}
The higher technical cut is kinematically inactive whenever
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_0
<
\frac{\mathcal T_1^{\cut}}{c_M},
\label{eq:t1-cut-inactive}
\end{equation}
and becomes the limiting upper bound only above this value.


For Drell--Yan double-real kinematics, the relevant configuration is \(\Ph_2\), and therefore
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_1(\Ph_2)
\le\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal T_0(\Ph_2),
\qquad
0\le z=\frac{\mathcal T_1}{\mathcal T_0}\le\frac12.
\label{eq:dy-t1-t0-bound}
\end{equation}
The higher-cut transition consequently occurs at
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_0=2\,\mathcal T_1^{\cut}.
\label{eq:dy-t1cut-transition}
\end{equation}
For a three-parton configuration, relevant for the next multiplicity in the colour-singlet-plus-jet NNLO
calculation, Eq.~\eqref{eq:t1-t0-multiplicity-bound} instead gives
\(\mathcal T_1/\mathcal T_0\le2/3\).

\subsection{Observable response to projection-induced migrations}
\label{subsec:observable-response-projection}

The size of the migration in phase space is only one part of the problem. The response of the observable can
introduce a second, logically distinct nonuniformity. At fixed $\rn$, let
\begin{equation}
S_i(\rn,X_i)=\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
\label{eq:observable-spectrum-S}
\end{equation}
denote the relevant smooth singular spectrum in a region where a first-order migration expansion is meaningful,
and define its local variation scale in $X_i$ by
\begin{equation}
L_i(\rn,X_i)
=
\left|
\partial_{X_i}\ln S_i(\rn,X_i)
\right|^{-1}
=
\left|
\frac{S_i(\rn,X_i)}{\partial_{X_i}S_i(\rn,X_i)}
\right|.
\label{eq:local-variation-scale}
\end{equation}
Then a small displacement produces parametrically
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta S_i}{S_i}
\sim
\frac{\Delta X_i}{L_i(\rn,X_i)}
\label{eq:relative-observable-sensitivity}
\end{equation}
when \(|\Delta X_i|\ll L_i\). In a smooth region \(L_i\) is of the order of a hard or characteristic
kinematic scale, so uniform nested infrared locality of the map together with IRC safety of the observable
translates into a small projection effect. Near a kinematic boundary, Jacobian feature or Sudakov shoulder,
however, \(L_i\) can become parametrically much smaller than the hard scale. A displacement that is small in
absolute terms can then generate a large local change in the distribution. When \(|\Delta X_i|\) becomes
comparable to the distance from the nonanalytic point, the derivative expansion itself is no longer uniform
and the effect is better viewed as migration across the feature rather than as a small derivative correction.

\subsubsection{Drell--Yan illustration}
\label{subsubsec:dy-illustration}

Drell--Yan production provides a useful illustration. Taking
\begin{equation}
\rn=\mathcal T_0,
\qquad
\rnp=\mathcal T_1,
\qquad
Q\sim M_{\ell\ell},
\label{eq:dy-resolution-identification}
\end{equation}
the vector-boson rapidity is preserved by the projection in both the FKS and $\mathcal T_0^{\mathrm FR}$-preserving mappings and therefore has no migration of the type discussed
here. On the other end, the transverse momenta of the decay leptons are IRC safe but are not preserved by either mapping. For the local singular spectrum at fixed \(\mathcal T_0\), two features are important. First,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dy-t1-t0-bound} guarantees that the physical ratio \(\mathcal T_1/\mathcal T_0\) never exceeds
\(1/2\) on the \(\Ph_2\) configurations relevant to the higher projection, independently of the ratio of the
two technical cuts. Second, since both maps have uniform nested infrared locality, the exact and projected states
approach the same Born configuration as \(\mathcal T_0\to0\) for all finite allowed values
\(0\le\mathcal T_1/\mathcal T_0\le1/2\). IRC safety then implies that the absolute displacement of the lepton
transverse momentum vanishes in this common unresolved limit.


The important exception is the region
\begin{equation}
p_{T,\ell}\simeq \frac{M_{\ell\ell}}{2},
\label{eq:dy-sudakov-shoulder}
\end{equation}
which corresponds to the Born kinematic endpoint and develops a Sudakov shoulder once QCD radiation is
included. There the spectrum varies rapidly and its fixed-order derivatives are enhanced. Consequently the
local variation scale in Eq.~\eqref{eq:local-variation-scale} becomes small. A projection-induced shift
\(\Delta p_{T,\ell}\) can therefore be much smaller than \(M_{\ell\ell}\) and still be comparable to the
scale over which the spectrum changes. Large local discrepancies near the shoulder are thus compatible with
a map satisfying uniform nested infrared locality: they need not signal a failure of the nested unresolved
limit, but rather a nonuniform response of the measurement to a small kinematic migration. Very close to the
shoulder, where
\begin{equation}
\left|p_{T,\ell}-\frac{M_{\ell\ell}}{2}\right|
\lesssim
|\Delta p_{T,\ell}|,
\label{eq:shoulder-migration-region}
\end{equation}
one should not expect the linearized measurement expansion to be quantitatively reliable; the exact
migration across the shoulder must be retained.

In summary, these two sources of nonuniformity should therefore be kept conceptually separate. Strong ordering can fail
because neighboring resolution scales remain at a finite ratio inside their allowed kinematic domain, in
which case uniform nested infrared locality controls the simultaneous unresolved limit. Independently, even
an IRC-safe observable can have a nonuniform differential response to a uniformly vanishing displacement when
its spectrum contains a sharp kinematic feature. For the \(\mathcal T_1/\mathcal T_0\) pair there is in
addition the multiplicity-dependent kinematic bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:t1-t0-multiplicity-bound}. The
Drell--Yan behavior described above is consistent with the combination of this kinematic restriction and
nested locality away from the shoulder, while the largest local projection differences in the lepton
transverse-momentum distribution can still arise from the nonuniform response near the shoulder.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{$Z+1$-jet}{Z+1-jet} illustration}
\label{subsubsec:z1jet-illustration}

The colour-singlet-plus-jet case provides a more stringent test of the higher projection than Drell--Yan.
For the NNLO $Z+1$-jet calculation the relevant multiplicities and neighboring resolution variables are
\begin{equation}
\Ph_1\leftrightarrow Z+1\text{ parton},\qquad
\Ph_2\leftrightarrow Z+2\text{ partons},\qquad
\Ph_3\leftrightarrow Z+3\text{ partons},
\label{eq:z1j-multiplicities}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\rnm=\mathcal T_0\ \mathrm{or}\ q_T ,\qquad
\rn=\mathcal T_1,\qquad
\rnp=\mathcal T_2.
\label{eq:z1j-resolution-identification}
\end{equation}
The LHE replacement relevant here is therefore
\begin{equation}
\Ph_3\longrightarrow
\widehat\Ph_2=\Pi_2(\Ph_3)
\qquad
\text{for }\mathcal T_2<\mathcal T_2^{\cut}.
\label{eq:z1j-higher-projection}
\end{equation}
The simultaneous lower unresolved limit is conveniently described by
\begin{equation}
\epsilon=\frac{\mathcal T_1}{Q}\to0,
\qquad
z=\frac{\mathcal T_2}{\mathcal T_1}=\mathcal O(1).
\label{eq:z1j-simultaneous-limit}
\end{equation}
For the axis-minimized jettiness variables on a three-parton configuration,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:njettiness-nested-pair-bound} gives the kinematic restriction
\begin{equation}
0\le z\le\frac23.
\label{eq:z1j-t2-t1-bound}
\end{equation}
Thus the physically relevant finite-$z$ region is compact, but it need not be strongly ordered.

A qualitative difference from Drell--Yan is that preserving the colour-singlet momentum does not completely
fix the limiting $\Ph_1$ point. At one-parton Born level, transverse momentum conservation gives
\begin{equation}
\vec p_{T,J}=-\vec q_T,
\qquad
p_{T,J}=q_T,
\label{eq:z1j-born-transverse-balance}
\end{equation}
while the longitudinal jet coordinate remains independent at fixed $q^\mu$. Locally one may therefore think
of the limiting one-jet Born manifold as being characterized by $q^\mu$ together with one additional
longitudinal coordinate, which may equivalently be represented by the jet rapidity or, in a suitable region,
by $\mathcal T_0$. Schematically, for a beam-thrust-like definition,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_0(\Ph_1)
\sim
q_T\,e^{-|y_J-Y_Z|},
\label{eq:z1j-t0-born-coordinate}
\end{equation}
up to the precise convention used for the resolution measure. Hence preservation of $q^\mu$ alone does not
imply preservation of the complete one-jet Born configuration.

This point is directly relevant for a $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ projection that preserves the vector-boson
four-momentum and the lower resolution variable $\mathcal T_1$ at leading power, but does not preserve $\mathcal T_0$ exactly.
The latter fact by itself does \emph{not} imply a failure of uniform nested infrared locality. The decisive
quantity is the scaling of
\begin{equation}
\Delta\mathcal T_0
=
\mathcal T_0(\Ph_3)
-
\mathcal T_0\!\left(\Pi_2(\Ph_3)\right)
\label{eq:z1j-delta-t0}
\end{equation}
along the finite-$z$ paths of Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-simultaneous-limit}. A nested-local map may perfectly well
satisfy
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta\mathcal T_0}{Q}
\sim
\left(\frac{\mathcal T_1}{Q}\right)^q
h_0(z,\omega),
\qquad q>0,
\label{eq:z1j-t0-nested-local-scaling}
\end{equation}
with $h_0$ finite over the allowed range $0\le z\le2/3$. By contrast, a nonzero finite limit
\begin{equation}
\lim_{\mathcal T_1\to0}
\frac{\Delta\mathcal T_0}{Q}
=h_0(z,\omega)\neq0
\qquad
\text{at fixed }z>0
\label{eq:z1j-t0-nested-failure}
\end{equation}
would demonstrate that exact and projected configurations approach different points of the limiting
$\Ph_1$ manifold.

The geometric reason why this question is not answered by exact preservation of $\mathcal T_1$ is simple.
The condition $\mathcal T_1\to0$ measures the approach to the one-jet manifold, and is therefore naturally
viewed as controlling a direction normal to that manifold. Every point of $\Ph_1$ has $\mathcal T_1=0$.
Preserving $\mathcal T_1$ consequently does not by itself prevent a displacement tangential to $\Ph_1$,
for example in the jet rapidity or $\mathcal T_0$ direction. Uniform nested infrared locality requires that
such tangential displacements vanish as the common unresolved scale is taken to zero.

{\bfseries We should look carefully in our mapping and determine whether this property is present or not.}

Jet observables introduce a second effect that is logically distinct from the locality of the projection map.
The $\mathcal T_1$ resolution variable is defined through its own exclusive clustering or minimization,
whereas the analysis commonly uses inclusive anti-$k_T$ jets. The anti-$k_T$ measurement is only piecewise
smooth in phase space: the clustering history changes across boundaries where two candidate distances become
equal, for example $d_{ij}=d_{kB}$, while the identity of the leading jet changes across
\begin{equation}
p_{T,j_a}=p_{T,j_b}.
\label{eq:z1j-leading-jet-boundary}
\end{equation}
These are codimension-one boundaries, since one equality separates neighboring phase-space regions with
different clustering or jet ordering. IRC safety guarantees the correct soft and collinear limit, but not
uniform differentiability of the measurement across such boundaries.

A particularly relevant boundary is the analysis requirement $p_{T,j_1}>p_T^{\min}$. For the projection-induced
shift $\Delta p_{T,j_1}=p_{T,j_1}(\Ph_3)-p_{T,j_1}(\widehat\Ph_2)$ one has distributionally
\begin{equation}
\Theta\!\left(\widehat p_{T,j_1}-p_T^{\min}\right)
-
\Theta\!\left(p_{T,j_1}-p_T^{\min}\right)
\simeq
-\Delta p_{T,j_1}\,
\delta\!\left(p_{T,j_1}-p_T^{\min}\right).
\label{eq:z1j-pt-threshold-migration}
\end{equation}
Thus a parametrically small displacement can generate a sizeable relative correction when the jet spectrum
is steep or the event lies close to a clustering, ordering or acceptance boundary. This enhancement does not
by itself signal a failure of nested locality: for a smooth underlying distribution the phase-space layer
that changes classification still shrinks with the size of the displacement.

There is nevertheless an important asymptotic protection for the leading-jet transverse momentum if the
$\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ map preserves $q^\mu$. In the strict one-jet limit both exact and projected
configurations must satisfy Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-born-transverse-balance}, independently of the jet rapidity.
Therefore, provided both configurations genuinely approach a one-jet limit,
\begin{equation}
\Delta p_{T,j_1}\longrightarrow0
\qquad
(\mathcal T_1\to0,\ z\text{ finite}),
\label{eq:z1j-leading-pt-protected}
\end{equation}
even if the limiting jet rapidities differ. A persistent nonzero plateau in $\Delta p_{T,j_1}$ would thus
signal a stronger problem than the mere non-preservation of $\mathcal T_0$. By contrast, a plateau in
$\Delta y_{j_1}$ or in $\Delta\mathcal T_0/Q$ at fixed finite $z$ would directly diagnose a failure to
approach the same point on the one-jet Born manifold.


The decisive test of the nested-locality properties of the two mappings is local: apply both projections to
the same exact $\Ph_3$ configurations and study, in bins of
\begin{equation}
z=\frac{\mathcal T_2}{\mathcal T_1},
\label{eq:z1j-z-diagnostic}
\end{equation}
the approach to the lower unresolved limit of
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta\mathcal T_0}{Q},
\qquad
\Delta y_{j_1}^{\rm anti-k_T},
\qquad
\frac{\Delta p_{T,j_1}^{\rm anti-k_T}}{Q}.
\label{eq:z1j-local-diagnostics}
\end{equation}
A positive-power fall with $\mathcal T_1/Q$ establishes nested locality for the corresponding coordinate,
whereas a nonzero plateau at fixed finite $z$ signals its failure. For the fiducial jet cuts one should
separately monitor the signed classification migration
\begin{equation}
\Theta\!\left(\widehat p_{T,j_1}-p_T^{\min}\right)
-
\Theta\!\left(p_{T,j_1}-p_T^{\min}\right)
\label{eq:z1j-signed-threshold-migration}
\end{equation}
for the relevant values of $p_T^{\min}$.


The $Z+1$-jet example therefore illustrates why exact preservation of the chosen resolution variable is only
one part of a robust event projection. The map must also approach the correct point of the lower Born manifold
in all tangential coordinates relevant to the measurement, while the observable itself must be examined for
nonuniform response near jet-clustering and fiducial boundaries.

\section{Differential locality of the lower map}
\label{sec:lower-map-locality}

We now turn to the lower projection
\begin{equation}
\Pi_N:\Ph_{N+1}\to\widehat\Ph_N.
\label{eq:lower-map}
\end{equation}
Suppose the singular \(N\)-body prediction is kept differential in
\begin{equation}
x\equiv\rnm.
\label{eq:x-def}
\end{equation}
This requirement exists independently of whether a generation cut on \(x\) is imposed.

For local matching in the measured variable \(x\), one wants
\begin{equation}
x(\widehat\Ph_N)
=
x^{\rm LP}(\Ph_{N+1})
+
\Delta x,
\qquad
\frac{\Delta x}{x}\to0
\quad\text{in the unresolved limit}.
\label{eq:lp-locality-lower}
\end{equation}
Exact preservation,
\begin{equation}
x(\widehat\Ph_N)=x^{\rm LP}(\Ph_{N+1}),
\label{eq:exact-lower-preservation}
\end{equation}
is sufficient and particularly convenient, but LP-consistent preservation is the essential requirement for the LP construction.
If \(x\) is shifted by the map,
\begin{equation}
x(\widehat\Ph_N)=x^{\rm LP}+\Delta x,
\label{eq:shift-lower}
\end{equation}
the projection delta expands as
\begin{align}
\delta\!\left(x-x^{\rm LP}-\Delta x\right)
={}&
\delta\!\left(x-x^{\rm LP}\right)
-
\Delta x\,
\partial_x
\delta\!\left(x-x^{\rm LP}\right)
+\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^2).
\label{eq:delta-expansion}
\end{align}
The first term is the LP contribution.
The second is a migration term and, after integration over the unresolved emission, generates derivatives of the singular lower-multiplicity spectrum.

\section{Toy model for migration in \texorpdfstring{\(r_{N-1}\)}{rN-1}}
\label{sec:lower-map-toy}

Take \(x=\rnm\) and assume the singular unresolved \(N+1\)-body contribution below \(\rn^{\cut}\) behaves as
\begin{equation}
d\sigma_{N+1}^{\rm sing}
\sim
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}\,
F(x)\,dx.
\label{eq:toy-singular}
\end{equation}
Let the map assign
\begin{equation}
x_{\rm proj}=x+\Delta x(\rn).
\label{eq:toy-map}
\end{equation}
Then the projected contribution is
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma^{\rm proj}}{dx}
\sim
\int_0^{\rn^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}\,
F\!\left(x-\Delta x(\rn)\right),
\label{eq:toy-projected}
\end{equation}
whereas the LP subtraction/resummed-expanded contribution contains
\begin{equation}
\int_0^{\rn^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}\,
F(x).
\label{eq:toy-lp}
\end{equation}
The residual map-dependent term is therefore
\begin{equation}
\Delta\!\left(\frac{d\sigma}{dx}\right)
\sim
\int_0^{\rn^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}
\left[
F\!\left(x-\Delta x(\rn)\right)-F(x)
\right].
\label{eq:toy-residual}
\end{equation}
For small \(\Delta x\),
\begin{equation}
F(x-\Delta x)-F(x)
=
-\Delta x\,F'(x)+\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^2),
\label{eq:toy-expand-F}
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Delta\!\left(\frac{d\sigma}{dx}\right)
\sim
-F'(x)
\int_0^{\rn^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}\,
\Delta x(\rn)
}.
\label{eq:toy-master}
\end{equation}

A useful nested scaling is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta x}{x}
\sim
c(\zeta)
\left(\frac{\rn}{x}\right)^p,
\qquad p>0,
\label{eq:toy-nested-scaling}
\end{equation}
whenever \(\rn\) and \(x\) admit such a homogeneous comparison.
Equation~\eqref{eq:toy-master} then yields
\begin{equation}
\Delta\!\left(\frac{d\sigma}{dx}\right)
\sim
-\frac{1}{p}\,
xF'(x)
\left(\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{x}\right)^p.
\label{eq:toy-general-scaling}
\end{equation}

For a singular spectrum
\begin{equation}
F(x)\sim\frac{1}{x},
\label{eq:toy-1overx}
\end{equation}
one obtains
\begin{equation}
\frac{
\Delta(d\sigma/dx)
}{
d\sigma/dx
}
\sim
\left(\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{x}\right)^p.
\label{eq:toy-relative}
\end{equation}
The projection effect therefore vanishes at fixed \(x\) when \(\rn^{\cut}\to0\), but the convergence is not uniform as \(x\to0\).
Near an active lower cut \(x\sim\rnm^{\cut}\), the relevant ratio becomes
\begin{equation}
\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{\rnm^{\cut}}.
\label{eq:toy-cut-ratio}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Pathological and slowly convergent maps}

A shift
\begin{equation}
\Delta x\sim c\,x
\label{eq:pathological-map}
\end{equation}
does not vanish at fixed \(x\) as \(\rn\to0\), and therefore does not define an IR-local projection for the variable \(x\).
It leads to logarithmic sensitivity,
\begin{equation}
\Delta\!\left(\frac{d\sigma}{dx}\right)
\sim
-c\,xF'(x)\ln \rn^{\cut},
\label{eq:pathological-log}
\end{equation}
but this case should be regarded as a deliberately pathological example, not as an acceptable IR-safe map.

By contrast, a map with a slower but still vanishing shift, e.g.
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta x}{X_{\rm char}}
\sim
c\left(\frac{\rn}{X_{\rm char}}\right)^{1/2},
\label{eq:sqrt-map}
\end{equation}
is IR local but converges more slowly than a linear power correction.

\section{Optional Born-level fiducial or generation boundaries}
\label{sec:born-boundaries}

If \(\ThetaB=1\), there is no additional lower-boundary migration issue.
If instead the process is defined in a restricted Born-like region,
\begin{equation}
\ThetaB(\Ph_M)
=
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm^{(M)}(\Ph_M)-\rnm^{\cut}
\right),
\label{eq:active-born-boundary}
\end{equation}
then the local NLO subtraction between \(N\) and \(N+1\) must also treat this boundary consistently.

For the lower map \(\Pi_N\), the real and counterterm contributions contain the acceptances
\begin{align}
&
R_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+1})
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm^{(N+1)}(\Ph_{N+1})-\rnm^{\cut}
\right),
\nonumber\\
&
C_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+1})
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm(\widehat\Ph_N)-\rnm^{\cut}
\right).
\label{eq:real-counter-born-cut}
\end{align}
Their difference contains the boundary mismatch
\begin{equation}
\Delta\Theta_B
=
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm(\widehat\Ph_N)-\rnm^{\cut}
\right)
-
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm^{(N+1)}(\Ph_{N+1})-\rnm^{\cut}
\right).
\label{eq:delta-thetaB}
\end{equation}
Defining
\begin{equation}
\Delta\rnm
=
\rnm^{(N+1)}(\Ph_{N+1})
-
\rnm(\widehat\Ph_N),
\label{eq:delta-rnm}
\end{equation}
one finds near the boundary
\begin{equation}
\Delta\Theta_B
\simeq
-\Delta\rnm\,
\delta\!\left(
\rnm(\widehat\Ph_N)-\rnm^{\cut}
\right).
\label{eq:boundary-expansion}
\end{equation}
This is a fiducial boundary correction localized at \(\rnm=\rnm^{\cut}\).

Exact preservation,
\begin{equation}
\rnm(\widehat\Ph_N)
=
\rnm^{(N+1)}(\Ph_{N+1}),
\label{eq:exact-born-boundary-preservation}
\end{equation}
eliminates this migration.
A weaker condition sufficient to avoid crossing the chosen boundary is
\begin{equation}
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm(\widehat\Ph_N)-\rnm^{\cut}
\right)
=
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm^{(N+1)}(\Ph_{N+1})-\rnm^{\cut}
\right)
\label{eq:theta-boundary-preservation}
\end{equation}
in the relevant singular region.

If the underlying Born process is itself singular as \(\rnm\to0\), the coefficient of the boundary correction may be enhanced because the cut can lie near a region where the Born spectrum is large.
If the Born process is finite, the same migration mechanism exists whenever the fiducial boundary is imposed, but without this additional singular enhancement.

\section{Consistency conditions for the nested event definition}
\label{sec:nested-event-consistency}

For
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{N+1}:\Ph_{N+2}\to\widehat\Ph_{N+1},
\label{eq:higher-map-repeat}
\end{equation}
the most important nested-locality condition is preservation of the already-defined \(\rn\) boundary,
\begin{equation}
\rn(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})
=
\rn(\Ph_{N+2}),
\label{eq:rn-preservation-higher-map}
\end{equation}
or at least preservation to the accuracy required by the construction.
Otherwise the \(\rnp<\rnp^{\cut}\) projection can migrate events across the lower multiplicity boundary \(\rn=\rn^{\cut}\).

If an additional lower Born-level fiducial boundary is active, the higher map should also preserve its classification,
\begin{equation}
\ThetaB(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})
=
\ThetaB(\Ph_{N+2}),
\label{eq:higher-map-born-boundary}
\end{equation}
again at least in the singular region relevant for the projection.

This suggests the general principle:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\text{a nested projection should preserve all lower resolution/fiducial boundaries
that are already active in the event definition}
}.
\label{eq:nested-principle}
\end{equation}
In addition, it should preserve at LP the Born-like coordinates in which the corresponding lower-multiplicity singular prediction is kept differential. For comparable neighboring resolution scales, the uniform nested infrared locality condition of Sec.~\ref{subsec:uniform-nested-locality} ensures that the map continues to approach the same lower-multiplicity kinematics in the simultaneous unresolved limit.


The conditions on the lower map $\Pi_N$ are complementary rather than redundant.  As discussed in
Secs.~\ref{sec:lower-map-locality}--\ref{sec:born-boundaries}, $\Pi_N$ must be local in any lower variable in
which the singular prediction is kept differential, and it must preserve the classification of any active
Born-level generation or fiducial boundary.  The complete event definition therefore contains two distinct
locality layers: the nested behavior of $\Pi_{N+1}$ in the $N+2\to N+1\to N$ limit and the differential and
boundary locality of $\Pi_N$ in the $N+1\to N\to N-1$ limit.  Only after these local properties have been
specified is it useful to integrate over $\rn$ and discuss their net effect on an NNLO prediction.

\section{From local projection effects to NNLO differential predictions}
\label{sec:nnlo-differential-predictions}

Sections~\ref{sec:higher-singular-spectrum}--\ref{sec:nested-event-consistency} characterized the local
singular structure and the locality requirements of the nested maps.  We now perform the logically separate
step of integrating over the lower resolution variable.  Schematically, the contribution of the higher
projection to a distribution differential only in $X_i$ is obtained from
\begin{equation}
\left.\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}\frac{d\sigma}{dX_i}\right|_{\rn>\rn^{\cut}}
=
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^{\rn^{\rm max}} d\rn\,
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}.
\label{eq:integrated-higher-projection-definition}
\end{equation}
This integration can convert a local neighboring-scale suppression into a dependence on the technical cuts,
but it must not be confused with the singularity analysis of the fixed-$\rn$ spectrum carried out above.

\subsection{Higher $\Ph_{N+2}\to\widehat\Ph_{N+1}$ LHE projection}
\label{subsec:higher-projection-nnlo}

\subsubsection{Strongly ordered hierarchy}

To see explicitly how the neighboring-cut ratio arises after integrating over the lower resolution variable,
consider first only the power-counting part of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rnp-contamination-rn}, keeping the distribution
differential in \(X_i\). For \(p>0\),
\begin{align}
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^Q
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}
\left(\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}\right)^p
&=
(\rnp^{\cut})^p
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^Q
\frac{d\rn}{\rn^{p+1}}
\nonumber\\
&=
\frac{1}{p}
\left[
\left(\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn^{\cut}}\right)^p
-
\left(\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^p
\right].
\label{eq:integrated-power-counting}
\end{align}
Thus, when \(Q\gg\rn^{\cut}\gg\rnp^{\cut}\), the integral is parametrically dominated by its lower endpoint.
Restoring the migration derivative and the logarithmic structure, the correction integrated only over
\(\rn\) has the schematic scaling
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{dX_i}
\equiv
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^Q d\rn\,
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
\sim
\frac{1}{p}
\left(
\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn^{\cut}}
\right)^p
\sum_{m,k}
\partial_{X_i}
\left[
X_{i,\rm char}\,
\widetilde C_{mk}(\mathbf X)
\ln^m\!\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{Q}
\ln^k\!\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn^{\cut}}
\right].
\label{eq:integrated-ratio-scaling}
\end{equation}
The coefficient functions \(\widetilde C_{mk}(\mathbf X)\) are not new independent ingredients. They denote
linear combinations of the coefficients \(C_{m'k'}(\mathbf X)\) appearing before the \(\rn\) integration in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rnp-contamination-rn}--\eqref{eq:p1-scaling}. The integration reshuffles the powers of
\(\ln(\rn/Q)\) and \(\ln(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn)\), so the coefficients multiplying the logarithms evaluated at
\(\rn=\rn^{\cut}\) are in general different linear combinations of the original \(C_{m'k'}\).

The second term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:integrated-power-counting}, proportional to
\((\rnp^{\cut}/Q)^p\), is parametrically smaller in this hierarchy. Equation~\eqref{eq:integrated-ratio-scaling}
therefore states that the \emph{integration over \(\rn\)} converts the local suppression
\((\rnp^{\cut}/\rn)^p\) into a dependence controlled by the ratio of the two technical cuts,
\((\rnp^{\cut}/\rn^{\cut})^p\). It does not imply a nonzero fully inclusive correction. If \(X_i\) is
subsequently integrated over its complete range, the migration derivative becomes a boundary term and
cancels, provided no fiducial boundary or other non-preserved measurement prevents that cancellation.

\subsubsection{Comparable cuts and uniform nested scaling}

The result in Eq.~\eqref{eq:integrated-ratio-scaling} must not be extrapolated to a situation in which the
two technical cuts are comparable. For a generic pair of resolution variables we do not assume any universal
kinematic upper bound on the ratio \(\rnp/\rn\). If, for example,
\begin{equation}
\rnp^{\cut}>\rn^{\cut},
\label{eq:inverted-cuts}
\end{equation}
it is useful to make the purely algebraic decomposition
\begin{equation}
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^Q d\rn
=
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^{\rnp^{\cut}}d\rn
+
\int_{\rnp^{\cut}}^Qd\rn.
\label{eq:split-rn-inverted-cuts}
\end{equation}
In the second region the higher technical cut itself enforces
\(\rnp/\rn<\rnp^{\cut}/\rn<1\), and sufficiently far above
\(\rnp^{\cut}\) one recovers the strongly ordered situation of
Sec.~\ref{subsec:strongly-ordered-higher-projection}. In the first region, by contrast, the condition
\(\rnp<\rnp^{\cut}\) alone does not force \(\rnp/\rn\) to be small. The same issue can occur for comparable
cuts \(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn^{\cut}=\mathcal O(1)\), even if the higher cut is somewhat smaller, because the region
near the lower endpoint need not be parametrically strongly ordered.

Equation~\eqref{eq:split-rn-inverted-cuts} should not be interpreted as a universal separation between a
non-strongly-ordered and a strongly ordered domain. It only illustrates what follows from the technical cuts
alone. Particular choices of resolution variables can impose additional kinematic restrictions on the
allowed values of \(\rnp\) at fixed \(\rn\). In that case the actual projected region is
\begin{equation}
0\le \rnp <
\min\!\left\{
\rnp^{\cut},\,\rnp^{\rm kin,max}(\rn,\mathbf X,\ldots)
\right\},
\label{eq:generic-kinematic-cap}
\end{equation}
and the point at which the technical cut becomes active is determined by the equality of the two entries in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:generic-kinematic-cap}, not necessarily by \(\rn=\rnp^{\cut}\).

It follows in particular that inserting \(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn^{\cut}=\mathcal O(1)\) into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:integrated-ratio-scaling} is not a prediction of an \(\mathcal O(1)\) projection effect. It is
only a signal that the expansion in the neighboring-scale ratio has been pushed outside its domain of
validity. The behavior of the projection map in the simultaneous unresolved limit has to be examined
separately, together with any kinematic restrictions specific to the chosen resolution variables.

For a generic pair of resolution variables, and in the absence of an additional kinematic cap that becomes
active before \(\rn\sim\rnp^{\cut}\), the contribution from the interval displayed in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:split-rn-inverted-cuts} has the illustrative power counting
\begin{align}
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^{\rnp^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}
\left(\frac{\rn}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
&=
\frac{1}{q_i}
\left[
\left(\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
-
\left(\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
\right].
\label{eq:uniform-nested-integrated-region}
\end{align}
If the cuts are lowered together at a fixed ratio
\begin{equation}
\rnp^{\cut}=\kappa\,\rn^{\cut},
\qquad \kappa=\mathcal O(1),
\label{eq:fixed-kappa-cuts}
\end{equation}
this gives
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{\rm sim}
\sim
\left(\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
\frac{\kappa^{q_i}-1}{q_i}
\times\text{logs and finite coefficients},
\label{eq:fixed-kappa-uniform-nested-scaling}
\end{equation}
for this interval when \(\kappa>1\). The formula is not universal: if the chosen resolution variables impose
a stronger kinematic restriction, the integration range and therefore the finite coefficient multiplying the
common-scale suppression must be modified accordingly. The robust conclusion is only that, for a uniformly
nested-local map, a finite cut ratio does not by itself imply an order-one projection error when both absolute
cuts are taken to zero together.

\subsubsection{Axis-minimized $N$-jettiness}

Using the kinematic bounds derived in Sec.~\ref{subsec:uniform-nested-locality}, the corresponding integrated
region is obtained only after the fixed-$\mathcal T_0$ spectrum has been understood.  In particular,

If \(\mathcal T_0^{\cut}<\mathcal T_1^{\cut}/c_M\), the interval in which the kinematic bound rather than
the technical \(\mathcal T_1\) cut sets the full allowed \(z\) range is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_0^{\cut}
<\mathcal T_0
<\frac{\mathcal T_1^{\cut}}{c_M}.
\label{eq:t1-t0-saturated-interval}
\end{equation}
For a power-like nested-local mismatch, the common-scale part of its integral over this interval behaves as
\begin{align}
\int_{\mathcal T_0^{\cut}}^{\mathcal T_1^{\cut}/c_M}
\frac{d\mathcal T_0}{\mathcal T_0}
\left(\frac{\mathcal T_0}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
&=
\frac{1}{q_i}
\left[
\left(\frac{\mathcal T_1^{\cut}}{c_M Q}\right)^{q_i}
-
\left(\frac{\mathcal T_0^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
\right],
\label{eq:t1-t0-uniform-integrated-region}
\end{align}
up to logarithms and finite coefficients from the integration over \(z\) in its allowed range. At fixed
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_1^{\cut}
=\kappa\,\mathcal T_0^{\cut},
\label{eq:t1-t0-fixed-kappa}
\end{equation}
this saturated interval exists for \(\kappa>c_M\), and the common-scale factor becomes
\begin{equation}
\left(\frac{\mathcal T_0^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
\frac{(\kappa/c_M)^{q_i}-1}{q_i},
\label{eq:t1-t0-fixed-kappa-scaling}
\end{equation}
up to logarithms and finite coefficients. Hence a large ratio of the technical cuts cannot make the physical
ratio \(\mathcal T_1/\mathcal T_0\) arbitrarily large at fixed multiplicity; it simply saturates at \(c_M\).

\subsubsection{Drell--Yan consequence}

The local Drell--Yan analysis in Sec.~\ref{subsubsec:dy-illustration} can now be integrated over
$\mathcal T_0$.  In the saturated region,

If \(\mathcal T_1^{\cut}\) is held fixed while \(\mathcal T_0^{\cut}\) is made much smaller, the higher cut
becomes kinematically inactive near the lower endpoint and the fully allowed interval
\(0\le\mathcal T_1/\mathcal T_0\le1/2\) is sampled there. Lowering \(\mathcal T_0^{\cut}\) further therefore
does not generate a growing cut-ratio enhancement. The transition to a cut-limited region occurs only around
\(\mathcal T_0\sim2\mathcal T_1^{\cut}\); correspondingly, for a power-like nested-local mismatch the
residual integrated scale associated with the saturated region is parametrically set by
\((2\mathcal T_1^{\cut}/Q)^{q_i}\), up to logarithms and finite coefficients.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{$Z+1$-jet}{Z+1-jet} consequence}

The local map and jet-response diagnostics were discussed in Sec.~\ref{subsubsec:z1jet-illustration}.
For the actual NNLO LHE comparison, the numerical hierarchy of the technical cuts must be treated using their
actual definitions. In the
setup considered here the lower cut is dynamical,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_1^{\cut}
=10^{-5}\,m_T^Z,
\qquad
m_T^Z=\sqrt{M_Z^2+q_T^2},
\label{eq:z1j-dynamic-t1cut}
\end{equation}
whereas the higher cut is taken to be fixed,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_2^{\cut}=10^{-3}\ {\rm GeV}.
\label{eq:z1j-fixed-t2cut}
\end{equation}
The neighboring-cut ratio is therefore event dependent,
\begin{equation}
\rho(\Ph)
\equiv
\frac{\mathcal T_2^{\cut}}{\mathcal T_1^{\cut}}
=
\frac{100\ {\rm GeV}}{m_T^Z},
\label{eq:z1j-cut-ratio}
\end{equation}
and is generically of order unity for electroweak transverse masses. In particular, the numerical values
$10^{-5}$ and $10^{-3}$ should not be interpreted as two directly comparable dimensionless cuts differing
by two orders of magnitude.

Combining Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-t2-t1-bound} with the fixed $\mathcal T_2$ cut shows that the higher technical cut
is kinematically inactive whenever
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_1
<
\frac32\,\mathcal T_2^{\cut}
=
1.5\times10^{-3}\ {\rm GeV},
\label{eq:z1j-t2cut-inactive}
\end{equation}
so that the full finite-$z$ interval $0\le z\le2/3$ is projected there. The corresponding saturated interval
above the lower technical boundary exists only if
\begin{equation}
10^{-5}m_T^Z
<1.5\times10^{-3}\ {\rm GeV},
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
m_T^Z<150\ {\rm GeV}.
\label{eq:z1j-saturated-condition}
\end{equation}
For $m_T^Z\sim100$ GeV, for example, the interval extends only from roughly
$10^{-3}$ GeV to $1.5\times10^{-3}$ GeV. Hence the current setup does not expose a logarithmically large
finite-$z$ region spanning the two decades that a naive comparison of $10^{-5}$ and $10^{-3}$ would suggest.
At larger $m_T^Z$ the ratio in Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-cut-ratio} decreases and the higher cut enforces a progressively
stronger restriction on $z$ already near the lower boundary.

The event sample is defined with the generation cut
\begin{equation}
q_T>q_T^{\rm gen}=10^{-3}\ {\rm GeV}.
\label{eq:z1j-generation-cut}
\end{equation}
Both the FKS and the $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ mappings considered here preserve $q_T$, and therefore preserve
this process-defining boundary exactly. The observed LHE differences cannot consequently be attributed to
migration across the generation cut.

The direct comparison of mappings is particularly informative. For
$\mathcal T_2^{\cut}=10^{-3}\ {\rm GeV}$ the LHE differences obtained with the FKS mapping are much smaller than
those obtained with the $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ mapping for observables involving the hardest anti-$k_T$ jet,
including analyses with $p_{T,j}^{\min}=10$, $50$ and $100$ GeV. For both mappings the differences grow when
$\mathcal T_2^{\cut}$ is increased, becoming much larger for $\mathcal T_2^{\cut}=1\ {\rm GeV}$, but the
$\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ residual remains substantially larger even at the smallest cut. Since the two mappings
are exposed to the same anti-$k_T$ clustering and threshold boundaries and preserve the same generation cut,
this shows that the large residual is map dependent. The jet measurement can amplify the migration, but cannot
by itself be its primary origin.

The observed hierarchy between the two mappings does not by itself establish a formal failure of uniform
nested infrared locality.  The $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ map could instead be nested-local with a larger
coefficient or a slower approach to the simultaneous unresolved limit than FKS.  The event-by-event scaling
test of Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-local-diagnostics}, performed at fixed finite $z$, is the appropriate way to
distinguish these possibilities.

A complementary cut study can distinguish formal from merely numerical problems. Keeping the dynamical
$\mathcal T_1^{\cut}=10^{-5}m_T^Z$ fixed, one may lower the higher cut through, for example,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_2^{\cut}
=
10^{-3},\ 5\times10^{-4},\ 2\times10^{-4},\ 10^{-4}\ {\rm GeV}.
\label{eq:z1j-t2cut-scan}
\end{equation}
If the LHE discrepancy decreases with a positive power of $\mathcal T_2^{\cut}$, the projection remains
formally convergent even if its coefficient is numerically large. An even cleaner nested test is to lower the
two cuts together at fixed ratio,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_2^{\cut}
=\kappa\,\mathcal T_1^{\cut}
=\kappa\,10^{-5}m_T^Z,
\label{eq:z1j-correlated-cut-scan}
\end{equation}
with fixed $\kappa$. A residual that survives this joint scaling would point to a failure of the LHE event
representation in the simultaneous unresolved limit, whereas a vanishing residual with a large finite-cut
coefficient would indicate slow but formally acceptable convergence.

\subsection{Lower \texorpdfstring{$N+1\to N$}{N+1 to N} projection, NNLO power corrections, and P2B}


The situation below \(\rn^{\cut}\) is conceptually different from the \(\rnp^{\cut}\) discussion.
The NNLO correction to multiplicity \(N\) is approximated using the LP factorization theorem in \(\rn\),
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\Sigma_N^{\rm NNLO}}{d\Ph_N}(\rn^{\cut})
\longrightarrow
\frac{d\Sigma_{N,\rm LP}^{\rm NNLO}}{d\Ph_N}(\rn^{\cut}).
\label{eq:nnlo-lp-replacement}
\end{equation}
The genuine dynamical power correction is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm dyn}^{(\rn)}
=
\frac{d\Sigma_N^{\rm NNLO}}{d\Ph_N}(\rn^{\cut})
-
\frac{d\Sigma_{N,\rm LP}^{\rm NNLO}}{d\Ph_N}(\rn^{\cut}),
\label{eq:dynamic-pc}
\end{equation}
with a generic scaling
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm dyn}^{(\rn)}
\sim
\left(\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^q
\sum_k
a_k(\Ph_N)
\ln^k\!\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{Q}.
\label{eq:dynamic-pc-scaling}
\end{equation}

There is in addition a distinct kinematic effect from replacing the exact higher-multiplicity measurement
by its projection onto \(\Ph_N\). At fixed order this is the place where fiducial power corrections can
be restored using a projection-to-Born (P2B) construction. Schematically, suppressing the local-subtraction
structure and the additional real-emission terms, the relevant correction has the form
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm FPC}^{(\rn)}[\Mcal]
\sim
\int d\Ph_{N+1}\,
w(\Ph_{N+1})
\left[
\Mcal(\Ph_{N+1})-
\Mcal\!\left(\Pi_N(\Ph_{N+1})\right)
\right].
\label{eq:p2b-fpc-correlated}
\end{equation}
The crucial point is that the two terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p2b-fpc-correlated} are not two independent
events: they are a \emph{correlated pair} generated from the same higher-multiplicity phase-space point
and carrying compensating weights. Their cancellation is therefore local in the underlying event and can
be implemented naturally in a fixed-order calculation with an internal analyzer, where both measurements
are filled together.

This creates a fundamental difficulty for a standard event-generator representation. A conventional LHEF
sample is a list of independent events. If the exact and projected pieces in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:p2b-fpc-correlated} are written as separate LHE events, the correlation on which the P2B
cancellation relies is lost. In particular, after subsequent showering the two events evolve independently,
so the fixed-order cancellation cannot in general be maintained. Thus a standard P2B FPC restoration is
not directly compatible with an ordinary LHEF event sample unless the event format and the downstream
analysis are extended to retain correlated event groups.

This is one reason why the properties of the projection map are more restrictive for event generation
than for a pure fixed-order calculation. In a fixed-order analyzer a projection mismatch can be corrected
by keeping the exact and projected contributions correlated. In an LHE event generator one instead wants
the map itself to preserve, as accurately as possible, the Born-like variables and active boundaries that
must survive in each individual event.

Schematically, the lower-cut error structure can be written as
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{(\rn)}
=
\Delta_{\rm dyn}^{(\rn)}
+
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rn)},
\label{eq:lower-error-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where \(\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rn)}\) is precisely the kinematic mismatch that a fixed-order P2B/FPC
construction such as Eq.~\eqref{eq:p2b-fpc-correlated} is designed to restore. By contrast,
\(\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rnp-proj-correction} is absent when using the exact
fixed-order analyzer and appears only when the \(N+2\) configuration is replaced by an \(N+1\) LHE event.

\section{Summary of mapping requirements}

The conclusions can be organized as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact higher-multiplicity NLO dynamics.}
The NLO \(N+1\) contribution is computed with local subtraction and does not use an LP approximation in \(\rnp\).

\item \textbf{Fixed-order analysis and LHE event generation are different objects.}
With the internal analyzer, the \(N+2\) contribution is analyzed on its exact kinematics and no FPC
restoration is needed for the \(N+2\to N+1\) step. The \(\Pi_{N+1}\) projection enters when constructing
\(N+1\)-parton LHE events below \(\rnp^{\cut}\).

\item \textbf{Projection effects can remain singular in lower variables.}
A kinematic difference suppressed in \(\rnp/\rn\) may still multiply the singular \(1/\rn\) spectrum
in the LHE event definition.

\item \textbf{Strong-ordering ratios do not control comparable-cut configurations.}
The local strong-ordering expansion of Sec.~\ref{subsec:strongly-ordered-higher-projection} and its
integrated consequence in Sec.~\ref{subsec:higher-projection-nnlo} require a genuine hierarchy. For comparable or inverted cuts, substituting an order-one ratio into the
strongly ordered formula is not a valid estimate of the projection error.

\item \textbf{Generic resolution variables need not obey a neighboring-ratio bound.}
The uniform nested-locality discussion is formulated without assuming a universal upper limit on
\(\rnp/\rn\). If a particular pair of resolution variables has an additional kinematic restriction, that
restriction must be imposed separately when determining the physically allowed projected region.

\item \textbf{Axis-minimized \(N\)-jettiness obeys monotonicity and a multiplicity-dependent bound.}
At fixed \(\Ph_M\), axis minimization implies \(\mathcal T_{K+1}\le\mathcal T_K\), and more strongly
\[
\frac{\mathcal T_{K+1}(\Ph_M)}{\mathcal T_K(\Ph_M)}
\le\frac{M-1}{M}.
\]
Equation~(61) of arXiv:2312.06496 is the \(K=0\) case. On \(\Ph_{N+2}\) this gives
\(\mathcal T_{N+1}/\mathcal T_N\le(N+1)/(N+2)\), while for Drell--Yan double-real kinematics
\(\mathcal T_1/\mathcal T_0\le1/2\). These quantitative statements rely on the axis-minimized definition;
no analogous numerical bound is assumed for generic resolution variables.

\item \textbf{Uniform nested infrared locality is a property of the projection map.}
A higher map has uniform nested infrared locality if exact and projected configurations approach the same
underlying lower-multiplicity point uniformly as \(\rn/Q\to0\) at finite values of \(\rnp/\rn\) throughout
the kinematically allowed domain. Once this is true, ordinary IRC safety implies that any smooth
lower-multiplicity observable has a vanishing exact--projected mismatch in the simultaneous unresolved limit.
The rate of convergence, however, is not fixed by IRC safety.

\item \textbf{Fixed-order NNLO accuracy does not require uniform nested locality of an auxiliary subtraction map.}
A map may be one-step IR local as \(\rnp\to0\) at fixed resolved \(\rn\), and hence define a valid local NLO
subtraction, while approaching a different lower-multiplicity point when \(\rn\) and \(\rnp\) vanish together.
If the same map is used consistently in the local and integrated counterterms, this auxiliary map dependence
cancels and the higher-multiplicity NLO contribution remains exact. Uniform nested locality becomes a physical
requirement only when the projected configuration is retained as the event representative rather than being
used solely inside an add--subtract construction.

\item \textbf{Observable smoothness is a separate source of nonuniformity.}
Even a uniformly vanishing kinematic displacement can generate a large local difference near a sharp
kinematic feature. The Drell--Yan lepton transverse-momentum distribution near the Sudakov shoulder
\(p_{T,\ell}\simeq M_{\ell\ell}/2\) provides a concrete example.

\item \textbf{Differential locality is independent of generation cuts.}
If the singular \(N\)-body result is differential in \(\rnm\), then \(\Pi_N\) should preserve \(\rnm\) at least to LP accuracy even when no cut on \(\rnm\) is imposed.

\item \textbf{Active lower boundaries impose extra conditions.}
If a cut such as \(\rnm>\rnm^{\cut}\) is part of the process definition, the map should in addition avoid migration across that boundary.

\item \textbf{P2B fiducial corrections belong to the lower \(N+1\to N\) fixed-order projection.}
They require exact and projected contributions to remain correlated. This is natural in a fixed-order
analyzer but is not representable as a standard set of independent LHE events without additional machinery
to preserve those correlations.

\item \textbf{Exact preservation is sufficient but not always necessary at fixed order.}
At fixed order, a residual projection mismatch can in principle be restored through correlated P2B/FPC
terms. For event generation, where such correlated restoration is not available in a standard LHEF sample,
exact or very accurate preservation of the relevant variables is much more important.

\item \textbf{Nested convergence is controlled by neighboring ratios only in ordered regions.}
When the resolutions are strongly ordered the natural parameters include
\[
\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn},
\qquad
\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{\rnm}.
\]
Near finite neighboring ratios, absolute suppression in the common unresolved scale and uniform nested
infrared locality of the map become the relevant criteria. For axis-minimized \(N\)-jettiness the physical
neighboring ratio is additionally bounded by Eq.~\eqref{eq:njettiness-adjacent-bound}.

\item \textbf{The genuine LP truncation in the setup discussed here}
is the NNLO lower-multiplicity approximation in \(\rn\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:nnlo-lp-replacement}.
\end{enumerate}
