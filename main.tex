\section{Purpose and scope}

This note collects in a unified form a number of related observations concerning the definition of
exclusive event samples in GENEVA when several nested resolution variables are used.
The main aim is to distinguish clearly between
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item genuine leading-power (LP) approximations in the lower-multiplicity NNLO contribution,
\item kinematic or fiducial power corrections induced by phase-space projections,
\item locality requirements on the projection maps for distributions differential in lower resolution variables,
\item and additional constraints induced by optional Born-level fiducial or generation cuts.
\end{enumerate}

The discussion is formulated for a lowest explicitly generated multiplicity \(N\), together with three
nested resolution variables,
\begin{equation}
\rnm(\Ph_N),\qquad
\rn(\Ph_{N+1}),\qquad
\rnp(\Ph_{N+2}),
\label{eq:nested-variables}
\end{equation}
with the unresolved limits
\begin{equation}
\rnm\to0:\ N\to N-1,\qquad
\rn\to0:\ N+1\to N,\qquad
\rnp\to0:\ N+2\to N+1.
\label{eq:unresolved-limits}
\end{equation}

The Born process at multiplicity \(N\) may be either finite or singular.
If it is finite, no lower generation cut is required.
If it is singular, or if one deliberately restricts the Born-like phase space, one may impose
\begin{equation}
\rnm(\Ph_N)>\rnm^{\cut}.
\label{eq:born-cut}
\end{equation}
It is useful to encode this optional restriction in a generic Born-level acceptance factor,
\begin{equation}
\ThetaB(\Ph_M)=
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{no lower Born-level restriction},\\[2mm]
\Theta\!\left(\rnm^{(M)}(\Ph_M)-\rnm^{\cut}\right),
& \text{if the restriction \eqref{eq:born-cut} is imposed},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:thetaB}
\end{equation}
where \(\rnm^{(M)}\) denotes the extension of the lower resolution variable to multiplicity \(M\).

At NNLO one then introduces the technical resolution cuts
\begin{equation}
\rn^{\cut},\qquad \rnp^{\cut}.
\label{eq:technical-cuts}
\end{equation}
The roles of the three cuts, when all are present, are conceptually different:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
cut & role \\
\midrule
\(\rnm^{\cut}\) & optional Born-level fiducial/generation cut \\
\(\rn^{\cut}\) & NNLO slicing/resolution cut between \(N\) and \(N+1\) \\
\(\rnp^{\cut}\) & higher-multiplicity separation inside the NLO \(N+1\) contribution \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{Measurement functions and projection maps}

For a generic observable we write
\begin{equation}
\Mcal(\Ph_M)
=
\ThetaB(\Ph_M)\,
\Mcal_{\rm phys}(\Ph_M),
\label{eq:measurement-full}
\end{equation}
with, for example,
\begin{equation}
\Mcal_{\rm phys}(\Ph_M)
=
\prod_{a=1}^{n_X}
\delta\!\left(X_a-X_a(\Ph_M)\right)
\,
\Theta_{\rm fid}^{\rm phys}(\Ph_M).
\label{eq:measurement-physical}
\end{equation}
The variables \(X_a\) may be Born-like and completely inclusive over additional QCD radiation,
while still being changed by the projection maps.

We shall use
\begin{equation}
P_N:\Ph_{N+1}\to\widehat\Ph_N,
\qquad
P_{N+1}:\Ph_{N+2}\to\widehat\Ph_{N+1},
\label{eq:projection-maps}
\end{equation}
for the two successive projections.

Two logically distinct notions of map consistency will be important:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{differential locality}: preservation, at least at LP, of variables in which the singular lower-multiplicity prediction is kept differential;
\item \emph{fiducial-boundary preservation}: avoidance of migration across any lower-multiplicity cut that is part of the process definition.
\end{enumerate}
The first requirement can be relevant even when no generation cut exists; the second is conditional on such a boundary being present.

\section{Exact NLO higher-multiplicity contribution}

For \(\rn>\rn^{\cut}\), the NNLO construction contains the NLO prediction for multiplicity \(N+1\).
With a local subtraction term \(C_{N+2}\),
\begin{align}
\sigma_{N+1}^{\rm NLO}[\Mcal]
={}&
\int d\Ph_{N+1}\,
\left[
B_{N+1}+V_{N+1}+I_{N+1}
\right](\Ph_{N+1})
\Mcal(\Ph_{N+1})
\nonumber\\
&+
\int d\Ph_{N+2}\,
\Bigl[
R_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})\Mcal(\Ph_{N+2})
-
C_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})\Mcal(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})
\Bigr].
\label{eq:nlo-nplus1}
\end{align}

The important point is that this is a dynamically exact NLO calculation.
There is no replacement
\begin{equation}
R_{N+2}\longrightarrow R_{N+2}^{\rm LP}(\rnp)
\label{eq:no-lp-rnp}
\end{equation}
in this contribution.
Hence, in the setup considered here, there is no genuine dynamical LP truncation associated with
\(\rnp^{\cut}\).

\section{\texorpdfstring{\(r_{N+1}^{\rm cut}\)}{rN+1cut}: projection effects only}

The phase-space identity
\begin{equation}
1=
\Theta\!\left(\rnp<\rnp^{\cut}\right)
+
\Theta\!\left(\rnp>\rnp^{\cut}\right)
\label{eq:rnp-partition}
\end{equation}
is exact.
If both regions are analyzed with exact \(\Ph_{N+2}\) kinematics, the final NLO result is independent of
\(\rnp^{\cut}\).

A residual dependence appears when configurations below the cut are represented by projected kinematics,
\begin{equation}
\Ph_{N+2}\to\widehat\Ph_{N+1}=P_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+2}),
\label{eq:rnp-projection}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
\Mcal(\Ph_{N+2})\to\Mcal(\widehat\Ph_{N+1}).
\label{eq:rnp-measurement-replacement}
\end{equation}
The corresponding projection correction is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}[\Mcal]
=
\int d\Ph_{N+2}\,
R_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})
\Theta\!\left(\rnp(\Ph_{N+2})<\rnp^{\cut}\right)
\left[
\Mcal(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})-\Mcal(\Ph_{N+2})
\right].
\label{eq:rnp-proj-correction}
\end{equation}
This is a kinematic/fiducial power correction, not a dynamical LP correction.

One should distinguish the correction itself, \(\Delta_{\rm proj}\), from any \emph{residual}
projection error left after an explicit FPC restoration has been included.

\section{Preserved and violated observables}

Suppose a variable \(X\) is exactly preserved by \(P_{N+1}\),
\begin{equation}
X(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})=X(\Ph_{N+2}).
\label{eq:x-preserved}
\end{equation}
Then for a distribution depending only on preserved variables and on variables integrated over their full ranges,
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{dX}=0.
\label{eq:x-preserved-zero}
\end{equation}

In general, however, preserving one Born-like variable does not imply preservation of all the others.
Consider, for example,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX},
\label{eq:rn-x-spectrum}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\rn(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})=\rn(\Ph_{N+2}),
\qquad
X(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})\neq X(\Ph_{N+2}).
\label{eq:rn-preserved-x-not}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX}
={}&
\int d\Ph_{N+2}\,
R_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})
\Theta\!\left(\rnp<\rnp^{\cut}\right)
\delta\!\left(\rn-\rn(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\nonumber\\
&\times
\left[
\delta\!\left(X-X(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})\right)
-
\delta\!\left(X-X(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\right].
\label{eq:rn-x-proj}
\end{align}

Thus, being inclusive over QCD radiation does not by itself imply projection independence.
The relevant criterion is invariance of the \emph{full measurement function}.

If all variables violated by the map are integrated over their complete ranges, while \(\rn\) is preserved,
their migration cancels in the pure \(d\sigma/d\rn\) distribution.
Any fiducial cut or differential dependence on those variables spoils this cancellation.

\section{How a higher cut can contaminate a lower singular spectrum}

In the strongly ordered region
\begin{equation}
\rnp\ll\rn\ll Q,
\label{eq:strong-ordering}
\end{equation}
the double-real cross section has schematically the singular structure
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma_{N+2}}
{d\rn\,d\rnp\,d\zeta}
\sim
\frac{1}{\rn}\frac{1}{\rnp}
F\!\left(
\ln\frac{\rn}{Q},
\ln\frac{\rnp}{\rn},
\zeta
\right),
\label{eq:double-singular}
\end{equation}
where \(\zeta\) denotes the remaining Born-like and splitting variables.

For a variable violated by the map define
\begin{equation}
\Delta X
=
X(\Ph_{N+2})-X(\widehat\Ph_{N+1}).
\label{eq:delta-x-higher}
\end{equation}
If the map approaches the exact kinematics smoothly, a generic nested scaling is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta X}{X_{\rm char}}
\sim
c_X(\zeta)
\left(\frac{\rnp}{\rn}\right)^p,
\qquad p>0,
\label{eq:delta-x-scaling}
\end{equation}
where \(p\) is nonuniversal and depends on the resolution variables and recoil prescription.

Expanding the measurement function gives parametrically
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX}
\sim
\frac{1}{\rn}
\left(\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}\right)^p
\sum_k
D_k(X)
\ln^k\!\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}.
\label{eq:rnp-contamination-rn}
\end{equation}
Hence,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\text{power suppressed in the higher unresolved limit}
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\text{nonsingular in the lower resolution variable}
}.
\label{eq:key-nonuniformity}
\end{equation}

For \(p=1\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:rnp-contamination-rn} behaves as
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX}
\sim
\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn^2}
\label{eq:p1-scaling}
\end{equation}
up to logarithms and observable-dependent coefficients.
This expression is meaningful only in the ordered regime
\(\rnp^{\cut}\ll\rn\); it is not a uniform expansion down to arbitrarily small \(\rn\) at fixed
\(\rnp^{\cut}\).

Integrating the ordered estimate over \(\rn>\rn^{\cut}\), one obtains parametrically
\begin{equation}
\Delta\sigma_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\sim
\left(
\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn^{\cut}}
\right)^p
\label{eq:integrated-ratio-scaling}
\end{equation}
up to logarithms and details of the map.
This is the origin of a potentially enhanced nested-cut dependence.

\section{Differential locality of the lower map}

We now turn to the lower projection
\begin{equation}
P_N:\Ph_{N+1}\to\widehat\Ph_N.
\label{eq:lower-map}
\end{equation}
Suppose the singular \(N\)-body prediction is kept differential in
\begin{equation}
x\equiv\rnm.
\label{eq:x-def}
\end{equation}
This requirement exists independently of whether a generation cut on \(x\) is imposed.

For local matching in the measured variable \(x\), one wants
\begin{equation}
x(\widehat\Ph_N)
=
x^{\rm LP}(\Ph_{N+1})
+
\Delta x,
\qquad
\frac{\Delta x}{x}\to0
\quad\text{in the unresolved limit}.
\label{eq:lp-locality-lower}
\end{equation}
Exact preservation,
\begin{equation}
x(\widehat\Ph_N)=x^{\rm LP}(\Ph_{N+1}),
\label{eq:exact-lower-preservation}
\end{equation}
is sufficient and particularly convenient, but LP-consistent preservation is the essential requirement for the LP construction.

If \(x\) is shifted by the map,
\begin{equation}
x(\widehat\Ph_N)=x^{\rm LP}+\Delta x,
\label{eq:shift-lower}
\end{equation}
the projection delta expands as
\begin{align}
\delta\!\left(x-x^{\rm LP}-\Delta x\right)
={}&
\delta\!\left(x-x^{\rm LP}\right)
-
\Delta x\,
\partial_x
\delta\!\left(x-x^{\rm LP}\right)
+\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^2).
\label{eq:delta-expansion}
\end{align}
The first term is the LP contribution.
The second is a migration term and, after integration over the unresolved emission, generates derivatives of the singular lower-multiplicity spectrum.

\section{Toy model for migration in \texorpdfstring{\(r_{N-1}\)}{rN-1}}

Take \(x=\rnm\) and assume the singular unresolved \(N+1\)-body contribution below \(\rn^{\cut}\) behaves as
\begin{equation}
d\sigma_{N+1}^{\rm sing}
\sim
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}\,
F(x)\,dx.
\label{eq:toy-singular}
\end{equation}
Let the map assign
\begin{equation}
x_{\rm proj}=x+\Delta x(\rn).
\label{eq:toy-map}
\end{equation}
Then the projected contribution is
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma^{\rm proj}}{dx}
\sim
\int_0^{\rn^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}\,
F\!\left(x-\Delta x(\rn)\right),
\label{eq:toy-projected}
\end{equation}
whereas the LP subtraction/resummed-expanded contribution contains
\begin{equation}
\int_0^{\rn^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}\,
F(x).
\label{eq:toy-lp}
\end{equation}
The residual map-dependent term is therefore
\begin{equation}
\Delta\!\left(\frac{d\sigma}{dx}\right)
\sim
\int_0^{\rn^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}
\left[
F\!\left(x-\Delta x(\rn)\right)-F(x)
\right].
\label{eq:toy-residual}
\end{equation}
For small \(\Delta x\),
\begin{equation}
F(x-\Delta x)-F(x)
=
-\Delta x\,F'(x)+\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^2),
\label{eq:toy-expand-F}
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Delta\!\left(\frac{d\sigma}{dx}\right)
\sim
-F'(x)
\int_0^{\rn^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}\,
\Delta x(\rn)
}.
\label{eq:toy-master}
\end{equation}

A useful nested scaling is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta x}{x}
\sim
c(\zeta)
\left(\frac{\rn}{x}\right)^p,
\qquad p>0,
\label{eq:toy-nested-scaling}
\end{equation}
whenever \(\rn\) and \(x\) admit such a homogeneous comparison.
Equation~\eqref{eq:toy-master} then yields
\begin{equation}
\Delta\!\left(\frac{d\sigma}{dx}\right)
\sim
-\frac{1}{p}\,
xF'(x)
\left(\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{x}\right)^p.
\label{eq:toy-general-scaling}
\end{equation}

For a singular spectrum
\begin{equation}
F(x)\sim\frac{1}{x},
\label{eq:toy-1overx}
\end{equation}
one obtains
\begin{equation}
\frac{
\Delta(d\sigma/dx)
}{
d\sigma/dx
}
\sim
\left(\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{x}\right)^p.
\label{eq:toy-relative}
\end{equation}
The projection effect therefore vanishes at fixed \(x\) when \(\rn^{\cut}\to0\), but the convergence is not uniform as \(x\to0\).
Near an active lower cut \(x\sim\rnm^{\cut}\), the relevant ratio becomes
\begin{equation}
\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{\rnm^{\cut}}.
\label{eq:toy-cut-ratio}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Pathological and slowly convergent maps}

A shift
\begin{equation}
\Delta x\sim c\,x
\label{eq:pathological-map}
\end{equation}
does not vanish at fixed \(x\) as \(\rn\to0\), and therefore does not define an IR-local projection for the variable \(x\).
It leads to logarithmic sensitivity,
\begin{equation}
\Delta\!\left(\frac{d\sigma}{dx}\right)
\sim
-c\,xF'(x)\ln \rn^{\cut},
\label{eq:pathological-log}
\end{equation}
but this case should be regarded as a deliberately pathological example, not as an acceptable IR-safe map.

By contrast, a map with a slower but still vanishing shift, e.g.
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta x}{X_{\rm char}}
\sim
c\left(\frac{\rn}{X_{\rm char}}\right)^{1/2},
\label{eq:sqrt-map}
\end{equation}
is IR local but converges more slowly than a linear power correction.

\section{Optional Born-level fiducial or generation boundaries}

If \(\ThetaB=1\), there is no additional lower-boundary migration issue.
If instead the process is defined in a restricted Born-like region,
\begin{equation}
\ThetaB(\Ph_M)
=
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm^{(M)}(\Ph_M)-\rnm^{\cut}
\right),
\label{eq:active-born-boundary}
\end{equation}
then the local NLO subtraction between \(N\) and \(N+1\) must also treat this boundary consistently.

For the lower map \(P_N\), the real and counterterm contributions contain the acceptances
\begin{align}
&
R_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+1})
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm^{(N+1)}(\Ph_{N+1})-\rnm^{\cut}
\right),
\nonumber\\
&
C_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+1})
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm(\widehat\Ph_N)-\rnm^{\cut}
\right).
\label{eq:real-counter-born-cut}
\end{align}
Their difference contains the boundary mismatch
\begin{equation}
\Delta\Theta_B
=
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm(\widehat\Ph_N)-\rnm^{\cut}
\right)
-
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm^{(N+1)}(\Ph_{N+1})-\rnm^{\cut}
\right).
\label{eq:delta-thetaB}
\end{equation}
Defining
\begin{equation}
\Delta\rnm
=
\rnm^{(N+1)}(\Ph_{N+1})
-
\rnm(\widehat\Ph_N),
\label{eq:delta-rnm}
\end{equation}
one finds near the boundary
\begin{equation}
\Delta\Theta_B
\simeq
-\Delta\rnm\,
\delta\!\left(
\rnm(\widehat\Ph_N)-\rnm^{\cut}
\right).
\label{eq:boundary-expansion}
\end{equation}
This is a fiducial boundary correction localized at \(\rnm=\rnm^{\cut}\).

Exact preservation,
\begin{equation}
\rnm(\widehat\Ph_N)
=
\rnm^{(N+1)}(\Ph_{N+1}),
\label{eq:exact-born-boundary-preservation}
\end{equation}
eliminates this migration.
A weaker condition sufficient to avoid crossing the chosen boundary is
\begin{equation}
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm(\widehat\Ph_N)-\rnm^{\cut}
\right)
=
\Theta\!\left(
\rnm^{(N+1)}(\Ph_{N+1})-\rnm^{\cut}
\right)
\label{eq:theta-boundary-preservation}
\end{equation}
in the relevant singular region.

If the underlying Born process is itself singular as \(\rnm\to0\), the coefficient of the boundary correction may be enhanced because the cut can lie near a region where the Born spectrum is large.
If the Born process is finite, the same migration mechanism exists whenever the fiducial boundary is imposed, but without this additional singular enhancement.

\section{Nested consistency of the higher map}

For
\begin{equation}
P_{N+1}:\Ph_{N+2}\to\widehat\Ph_{N+1},
\label{eq:higher-map-repeat}
\end{equation}
the most important nested-locality condition is preservation of the already-defined \(\rn\) boundary,
\begin{equation}
\rn(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})
=
\rn(\Ph_{N+2}),
\label{eq:rn-preservation-higher-map}
\end{equation}
or at least preservation to the accuracy required by the construction.
Otherwise the \(\rnp<\rnp^{\cut}\) projection can migrate events across the lower multiplicity boundary \(\rn=\rn^{\cut}\).

If an additional lower Born-level fiducial boundary is active, the higher map should also preserve its classification,
\begin{equation}
\ThetaB(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})
=
\ThetaB(\Ph_{N+2}),
\label{eq:higher-map-born-boundary}
\end{equation}
again at least in the singular region relevant for the projection.

This suggests the general principle:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\text{a nested projection should preserve all lower resolution/fiducial boundaries
that are already active in the event definition}
}.
\label{eq:nested-principle}
\end{equation}
In addition, it should preserve at LP the Born-like coordinates in which the corresponding lower-multiplicity singular prediction is kept differential.

\section{Genuine LP approximation in the lower-multiplicity NNLO contribution}

The situation below \(\rn^{\cut}\) is conceptually different from the \(\rnp^{\cut}\) discussion.
The NNLO correction to multiplicity \(N\) is approximated using the LP factorization theorem in \(\rn\),
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\Sigma_N^{\rm NNLO}}{d\Ph_N}(\rn^{\cut})
\longrightarrow
\frac{d\Sigma_{N,\rm LP}^{\rm NNLO}}{d\Ph_N}(\rn^{\cut}).
\label{eq:nnlo-lp-replacement}
\end{equation}
The genuine dynamical power correction is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm dyn}^{(\rn)}
=
\frac{d\Sigma_N^{\rm NNLO}}{d\Ph_N}(\rn^{\cut})
-
\frac{d\Sigma_{N,\rm LP}^{\rm NNLO}}{d\Ph_N}(\rn^{\cut}),
\label{eq:dynamic-pc}
\end{equation}
with a generic scaling
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm dyn}^{(\rn)}
\sim
\left(\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^q
\sum_k
a_k(\Ph_N)
\ln^k\!\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{Q}.
\label{eq:dynamic-pc-scaling}
\end{equation}
This is distinct from projection/fiducial corrections generated by
\(\Ph_{N+1}\to\widehat\Ph_N\) or \(\Ph_{N+2}\to\widehat\Ph_{N+1}\).

Schematically,
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{(\rn)}
=
\Delta_{\rm dyn}^{(\rn)}
+
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rn)},
\qquad
\Delta^{(\rnp)}
=
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\label{eq:error-decomposition}
\end{equation}
for the setup considered here, where the higher-multiplicity NLO calculation is exact.

\section{Summary of mapping requirements}

The conclusions can be organized as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact higher-multiplicity NLO dynamics.}
The NLO \(N+1\) contribution is computed with local subtraction and does not use an LP approximation in \(\rnp\).

\item \textbf{Projection effects can remain singular in lower variables.}
A correction suppressed in \(\rnp/\rn\) may still multiply the singular \(1/\rn\) spectrum.

\item \textbf{Differential locality is independent of generation cuts.}
If the singular \(N\)-body result is differential in \(\rnm\), then \(P_N\) should preserve \(\rnm\) at least to LP accuracy even when no cut on \(\rnm\) is imposed.

\item \textbf{Active lower boundaries impose extra conditions.}
If a cut such as \(\rnm>\rnm^{\cut}\) is part of the process definition, the map should in addition avoid migration across that boundary.

\item \textbf{Exact preservation is sufficient but not always necessary.}
Exact preservation removes the corresponding projection correction identically.
LP-consistent preservation leaves only a subleading correction, which may still require explicit FPC restoration.

\item \textbf{Nested convergence is controlled by ratios of neighboring resolution scales.}
In ordered regions the relevant parameters are naturally of the form
\[
\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn},
\qquad
\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{\rnm},
\]
and near lower active cuts by
\[
\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn^{\cut}},
\qquad
\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{\rnm^{\cut}}.
\]

\item \textbf{The only genuine LP truncation in the setup discussed here}
is the NNLO lower-multiplicity approximation in \(\rn\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:nnlo-lp-replacement}.
\end{enumerate}
