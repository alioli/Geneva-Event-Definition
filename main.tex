\section{Purpose and scope}

This note collects in a unified form a number of related observations concerning the definition of
exclusive event samples in GENEVA when several nested resolution variables are used.
The main aim is to distinguish clearly between
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item dynamical approximations generated by slicing cuts in pure fixed-order calculations, including the common case in which only the leading-power (LP) terms are retained and systematically improved cases in which NLP, NNLP, or still higher power-suppressed terms are also included,
\item kinematic effects induced specifically by replacing exact configurations with projected LHE events,
\item locality requirements needed when one sliced fixed-order calculation is embedded inside another,
\item kinematic and fiducial power corrections that can be restored at fixed order through projection-to-Born constructions,
\item dynamical truncation remainders associated with the terms not retained in the below-cut dynamical approximation, which cannot be restored by projection-to-Born,
\item and additional constraints induced by optional Born-level fiducial or generation cuts.
\end{enumerate}

The discussion is formulated for a lowest explicitly generated multiplicity \(N\), together with three
nested resolution variables,
\begin{equation}
\rnm(\Ph_N),\qquad
\rn(\Ph_{N+1}),\qquad
\rnp(\Ph_{N+2}),
\label{eq:nested-variables}
\end{equation}
with the unresolved limits
\begin{equation}
\rnm\to0:\ N\to N-1,\qquad
\rn\to0:\ N+1\to N,\qquad
\rnp\to0:\ N+2\to N+1.
\label{eq:unresolved-limits}
\end{equation}

The Born process at multiplicity \(N\) may be either finite or singular.
If it is finite, no lower generation cut is required.
If it is singular, or if one deliberately restricts the Born-like phase space, one may impose
\begin{equation}
\rnm(\Ph_N)>\rnm^{\cut}.
\label{eq:born-cut}
\end{equation}
It is useful to encode this optional restriction in a generic Born-level acceptance factor,
\begin{equation}
\ThetaB(\Ph_M)=
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{no lower Born-level restriction},\\[2mm]
\Theta\!\left(\rnm(\Ph_M)-\rnm^{\cut}\right),
& \text{if the restriction \eqref{eq:born-cut} is imposed},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:thetaB}
\end{equation}

At NNLO one then introduces the technical resolution cuts
\begin{equation}
\rn^{\cut},\qquad \rnp^{\cut}.
\label{eq:technical-cuts}
\end{equation}
The roles of the three cuts, when all are present, are conceptually different:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
cut & role \\
\midrule
\(\rnm^{\cut}\) & optional Born-level fiducial/generation cut \\
\(\rn^{\cut}\) & NNLO slicing/resolution cut between \(N\) and \(N+1\) \\
\(\rnp^{\cut}\) & higher-multiplicity separation inside the NLO \(N+1\) contribution \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{Higher-multiplicity NLO contribution and LHE projections}

\subsection{Measurement functions and projection maps}

For a generic observable we write
\begin{equation}
\Mcal(\Ph_M)
=
\ThetaB(\Ph_M)\,
\Mcal_{\rm phys}(\Ph_M),
\label{eq:measurement-full}
\end{equation}
with, for example,
\begin{equation}
\Mcal_{\rm phys}(\Ph_M)
=
\prod_{a=1}^{n_X}
\delta\!\left(X_a-X_a(\Ph_M)\right)
\,
\Theta_{\rm fid}^{\rm phys}(\Ph_M).
\label{eq:measurement-physical}
\end{equation}
The variables \(X_a\), collected into the vector
\(\mathbf X=(X_1,\ldots,X_{n_X})\), may be Born-like and completely inclusive over additional QCD radiation,
while still being changed by the projection maps.

We shall use
\begin{equation}
\Pi_N:\Ph_{N+1}\to\widehat\Ph_N,
\qquad
\Pi_{N+1}:\Ph_{N+2}\to\widehat\Ph_{N+1},
\label{eq:projection-maps}
\end{equation}
for the two successive projections.

\subsection{Exact NLO higher-multiplicity contribution}

For \(\rn>\rn^{\cut}\), the NNLO construction contains the NLO prediction for multiplicity \(N+1\).
With a local subtraction term \(C_{N+2}\),
\begin{align}
\sigma_{N+1}^{\rm NLO}[\Mcal]
={}&
\int d\Ph_{N+1}\,
\left[
B_{N+1}+V_{N+1}+I_{N+1}
\right](\Ph_{N+1})
\Mcal(\Ph_{N+1})
\nonumber\\
&+
\int d\Ph_{N+2}\,
\Bigl[
R_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})\Mcal(\Ph_{N+2})
-
C_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})\Mcal(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})
\Bigr].
\label{eq:nlo-nplus1}
\end{align}

The important point is that this is a dynamically exact NLO calculation.
In particular, there is no replacement
\begin{equation}
R_{N+2}\longrightarrow R_{N+2}^{\rm LP}(\rnp)
\label{eq:no-lp-rnp}
\end{equation}
in this contribution. Equation~\eqref{eq:no-lp-rnp} is only an illustrative LP example of the more general
statement: the higher separation \(\rnp^{\cut}\) does not replace the exact real-emission dynamics by any
truncated power-expanded approximation. Hence there is no dynamical truncation remainder associated with
\(\rnp^{\cut}\) in the setup considered here.

\subsection{Fixed-order analysis versus LHE event generation dependence on \texorpdfstring{\(r_{N+1}^{\rm cut}\)}{rN+1cut}}

The phase-space identity
\begin{equation}
1=
\Theta\!\left(\rnp<\rnp^{\cut}\right)
+
\Theta\!\left(\rnp>\rnp^{\cut}\right)
\label{eq:rnp-partition}
\end{equation}
is exact.
If both regions are analyzed with exact \(\Ph_{N+2}\) kinematics, the final NLO result is independent of
\(\rnp^{\cut}\).
In GENEVA this is precisely what happens in a fixed-order calculation with the
\emph{internal analyzer}: every contribution is filled using its exact partonic kinematics.
Equation~\eqref{eq:rnp-partition} is then only an internal partition of phase space and the dependence on
\(\rnp^{\cut}\) cancels, up to numerical integration errors. No fiducial-power-correction restoration is
needed for the \(N+2\to N+1\) step in this setup, because no kinematic information has been discarded.
This is the appropriate setup for validating the underlying fixed-order calculation.

The situation relevant for MC event generation is different. When events are generated for output to
the LHEF file, configurations below \(\rnp^{\cut}\) are represented as \((N+1)\)-parton events rather
than as resolved \((N+2)\)-parton configurations. Their kinematics are therefore projected according to
\begin{equation}
\Ph_{N+2}\to\widehat\Ph_{N+1}=\Pi_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+2}),
\label{eq:rnp-projection}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
\Mcal(\Ph_{N+2})\to\Mcal(\widehat\Ph_{N+1}).
\label{eq:rnp-measurement-replacement}
\end{equation}
The difference between the LHE event definition and the exact fixed-order result is therefore
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}[\Mcal]
=
\int d\Ph_{N+2}\,
R_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})
\Theta\!\left(\rnp(\Ph_{N+2})<\rnp^{\cut}\right)
\left[
\Mcal(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})-\Mcal(\Ph_{N+2})
\right].
\label{eq:rnp-proj-correction}
\end{equation}
This is an observable-dependent kinematic effect induced by the LHE projection. It should not be
confused with the fiducial power corrections appearing in a projection-to-Born construction for the
lower \(N+1\to N\) projection at fixed-order NNLO.

\subsection{Observables preserved and violated by the NLO projection}

Suppose a particular Born-like variable \(X_i\) is exactly preserved by \(\Pi_{N+1}\),
\begin{equation}
X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})=X_i(\Ph_{N+2}).
\label{eq:x-preserved}
\end{equation}
Then for a distribution depending only on preserved variables and on variables integrated over their full ranges,
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{dX_i}=0.
\label{eq:x-preserved-zero}
\end{equation}

In general, however, preserving one Born-like variable does not imply preservation of all the others.
When focusing on one specific component \(X_i\) of \(\mathbf X\), we denote the remaining Born-like variables
collectively by \(\mathbf X_{\neq i}\). Consider, for example,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i},
\label{eq:rn-x-spectrum}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\rn(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})=\rn(\Ph_{N+2}),
\qquad
X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})\neq X_i(\Ph_{N+2}).
\label{eq:rn-preserved-x-not}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
={}&
\int d\Ph_{N+2}\,
R_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})
\Theta\!\left(\rnp<\rnp^{\cut}\right)
\delta\!\left(\rn-\rn(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\nonumber\\
&\times
\left[
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})\right)
-
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\right].
\label{eq:rn-x-proj}
\end{align}

The cancellation after integrating over the \emph{full} range of \(X_i\) is immediate. If the integration
range contains both the exact and projected values of \(X_i\) for every phase-space point, then
\begin{equation}
\int_{\rm full} dX_i\,
\left[
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})\right)
-
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\right]
=1-1=0.
\label{eq:x-full-range-cancellation}
\end{equation}
Hence, provided \(\rn\) is itself preserved by the projection,
\begin{equation}
\int_{\rm full} dX_i\,
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
=0.
\label{eq:x-integrated-zero}
\end{equation}
The interpretation is simple: the projection can move an event from one value of \(X_i\) to another, but
it does not change the total weight. Once all values of \(X_i\) are summed inclusively, this migration is
invisible.

This cancellation is lost as soon as the integration over \(X_i\) is not fully inclusive. To make this
explicit, introduce an acceptance function \(A_{X_i}(X_i)\), equal to one in the accepted fiducial region and
zero outside. Integrating Eq.~\eqref{eq:rn-x-proj} with this acceptance gives
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma^{\rm fid}}{d\rn}
={}&
\int d\Ph_{N+2}\,
R_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})
\Theta\!\left(\rnp<\rnp^{\cut}\right)
\delta\!\left(\rn-\rn(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\nonumber\\
&\times
\left[
A_{X_i}\!\left(X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})\right)
-
A_{X_i}\!\left(X_i(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\right].
\label{eq:x-fiducial-migration}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:x-fiducial-migration} is exact and makes no assumption about the size of the migration.
For a binary fiducial acceptance, the contribution is nonzero precisely for configurations for which the
exact and projected kinematics lie on opposite sides of an acceptance boundary. Define the migration in
\(X_i\) as
\begin{equation}
\Delta X_i
=
X_i(\Ph_{N+2})-X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1}),
\label{eq:delta-x-fiducial}
\end{equation}
If \(\Delta X_i\) is small on the relevant local kinematic scales, the generic acceptance difference can be
expanded as
\begin{align}
&A_{X_i}\!\left(X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})\right)
-A_{X_i}\!\left(X_i(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\nonumber\\
&\qquad\simeq
-\Delta X_i\,
A_{X_i}'\!\left(X_i(\Ph_{N+2})\right).
\label{eq:x-acceptance-linearization}
\end{align}
For a smooth acceptance weight, \(A_{X_i}'\) is an ordinary derivative. For a hard lower cut it is understood
distributionally,
\[
A_{X_i}(X_i)=\Theta(X_i-X_{i,\rm cut}),
\qquad
A_{X_i}'(X_i)=\delta(X_i-X_{i,\rm cut}).
\]
Only in this small-migration approximation is the hard-cut contribution localized at the boundary. Without
that approximation, Eq.~\eqref{eq:x-fiducial-migration} describes a finite migration layer containing all
configurations whose exact and projected values lie on opposite sides of the cut.

More generally, if several components of \(\mathbf X\) are changed by the projection, integrating over the
full range of one of them removes only the migration associated with that component. Any remaining differential
measurement or fiducial cut on another non-preserved component can still produce a nonzero projection
effect. The actual criterion for cancellation is therefore invariance of the \emph{full measurement
function}, not merely inclusiveness with respect to QCD radiation. Likewise, if \(\rn\) itself is not
preserved, integrating over \(X_i\) does not eliminate migration in the \(\rn\) distribution.

\section{Impact of the higher-multiplicity \texorpdfstring{$r_{N+1}^{\rm cut}$}{rN+1cut} projection on the lower-multiplicity \texorpdfstring{$r_N$}{rN} singular spectrum}
\label{sec:higher-singular-spectrum}

Throughout this section the lower resolution variable $\rn$ is kept differential.  The object of interest is
therefore the projection-induced modification of the singular spectrum
$d\sigma/(d\rn\,dX_i)$ at fixed $\rn$.  No integration over $\rn$ is used here to infer the size of a final
NNLO distribution differential only in $X_i$.  That separate step, and the resulting dependence on the
technical cuts after the $\rn$ integration, are discussed only in
Sec.~\ref{sec:nnlo-differential-predictions}.


The purpose of this section is to understand a potentially important feature of the LHE projection discussed
above. Although the difference between the exact \((N+2)\)-parton kinematics and the projected
\((N+1)\)-parton kinematics vanishes in the \(\rnp\to0\) limit, this does not by itself imply that the
resulting correction is nonsingular as a function of the lower resolution variable \(\rn\).

\subsection{Strongly ordered nested limit}
\label{subsec:strongly-ordered-higher-projection}

The relevant region for the analysis in this subsection is the nested unresolved limit
\begin{equation}
\rnp\ll\rn\ll Q,
\label{eq:strong-ordering}
\end{equation}
where the two emissions are strongly ordered in resolution scale. Here \(Q\) denotes a generic hard scale
characterizing the underlying \((N-1)\)-parton process and remaining finite in the nested unresolved limits.
Depending on the process, it may be an invariant mass, a hard transverse momentum, or another characteristic
short-distance scale. Its precise definition is immaterial for the argument below; it simply provides the
hard reference scale with respect to which \(\rn\) is parametrically small.

In this region the singular part of the \((N+2)\)-parton cross section can be viewed, at leading power, as
the lower-multiplicity hard spectrum, differential in the Born-like variables \(\mathbf X\), multiplied by
two successive unresolved-emission factors: one associated with the \(N+1\to N\) limit and one with the
further \(N+2\to N+1\) limit.

For resolution variables with the usual soft/collinear scaling, a single unresolved emission produces a
logarithmic measure of the form \(d r_i/r_i\), together with splitting kernels, angular variables, colour
factors and couplings. Equivalently, at fixed order the singular terms generated by one unresolved limit
have the familiar schematic form \(\ln^m(r_i/Q_i)/r_i\). Iterating two strongly ordered unresolved limits
therefore produces a leading singular structure containing one inverse power of each resolution variable.
We focus on the migration of one particular component \(X_i\) of \(\mathbf X\), while
\(\mathbf X_{\neq i}\) denotes all the remaining Born-like variables. We use
\(\Ph_{\rm rad}^{\rm rest}\) for the residual radiation variables after \(\rn\) and \(\rnp\) have been
displayed explicitly. In particular, \(d\Ph_{\rm rad}^{\rm rest}\) does not include \(d\rnp\); it contains
the remaining energy fractions, angles, recoil variables, and any other coordinates needed to parametrize
the corresponding splittings, with the associated Jacobian absorbed into \(F\). This gives schematically
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma_{N+2}}
{d\rn\,d\rnp\,dX_i\,d\mathbf X_{\neq i}\,d\Ph_{\rm rad}^{\rm rest}}
\sim
\frac{1}{\rn}\frac{1}{\rnp}
F\!\left(
\mathbf X,\Ph_{\rm rad}^{\rm rest},
\ln\frac{\rn}{Q},
\ln\frac{\rnp}{\rn}
\right).
\label{eq:double-singular}
\end{equation}
The first factor \(1/\rn\) is associated with resolving the \((N+1)\)-parton configuration relative to the
underlying \(N\)-parton state, while \(1/\rnp\) is the additional singularity associated with the more
unresolved \(N+2\to N+1\) emission. The logarithms have been written in terms of the two hierarchical ratios
\(\rn/Q\) and \(\rnp/\rn\), which directly expose the nested ordering. One could equivalently use
\(\rnp/Q\), since its logarithm is a linear combination of these two logarithms.

Equation~\eqref{eq:double-singular} is meant as a schematic leading-power form, not as a universal statement
for arbitrary definitions of the resolution variables. The precise powers and logarithms depend on their
canonical scaling and on the detailed factorization theorem. What matters for the argument below is that the
higher unresolved singular measure in \(\rnp\) multiplies a spectrum that is already singular in the lower
resolution variable \(\rn\).

The projection mismatch \(\Delta X_i\) has already been defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta-x-fiducial}. To
characterize its size, introduce a nonsingular characteristic scale \(X_{i,\rm char}\) associated with the
particular Born-like variable \(X_i\) whose migration is being studied. Its role is only to render the relative
shift dimensionless. We denote this normalized migration by
\[
\Delta_{x_i}
\equiv
\frac{\Delta X_i}{X_{i,\rm char}}.
\]
The lowercase subscript labels the normalized version of the shift in \(X_i\); it does not introduce a new
kinematic variable \(x_i\). The characteristic scale \(X_{i,\rm char}\) is a fixed local reference scale,
introduced only to normalize the migration. It may depend parametrically on the reference Born kinematics,
including the reference value of \(X_i\), only provided that it remains uniformly finite and bounded away
from zero throughout the region under consideration. In particular, choices such as
\(X_{i,\rm char}=|X_i|\) are not admissible in a region where \(X_i\) can vanish.

If the map approaches the exact kinematics smoothly, the relative migration can then have a generic nested
scaling
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{x_i}
\sim
c_i(\mathbf X,\Ph_{\rm rad}^{\rm rest})
\left(\frac{\rnp}{\rn}\right)^p,
\qquad p>0,
\label{eq:delta-x-scaling}
\end{equation}
where \(c_i\) is a nonsingular dimensionless coefficient and \(p\) is nonuniversal and depends on the
resolution variables and recoil prescription. Equation~\eqref{eq:delta-x-scaling} should therefore be
understood only as a parametrization of how rapidly the projection becomes local in \(X_i\) as the second
emission becomes unresolved relative to the first one; it does not assume that the absolute shift
\(\Delta X_i\) is set by \(\rnp\) itself. In particular, Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta-x-scaling} isolates the dependence
on the strong-ordering ratio \(\rnp/\rn\); it does not by itself determine whether the coefficient multiplying
this ratio receives an additional suppression when \(\rn/Q\to0\). That separate question will be addressed
in Sec.~\ref{subsec:uniform-nested-locality}.

The origin of the migration derivative can be seen directly by expanding the measurement delta. Since
\(X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})=X_i(\Ph_{N+2})-\Delta X_i\),
\[
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\widehat\Ph_{N+1})\right)
-
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\simeq
\Delta X_i\,
\left.
\frac{\partial}{\partial X_i}
\right|_{\rn,\mathbf X_{\neq i}}
\delta\!\left(X_i-X_i(\Ph_{N+2})\right).
\]
Here \(\mathbf X_{\neq i}\) denotes all Born variables other than \(X_i\). The derivative is taken at fixed
\(\rn\) and \(\mathbf X_{\neq i}\). The reference scale \(X_{i,\rm char}\) is fixed at the underlying Born
point before applying this expansion and is therefore not acted on by the derivative. Similarly,
\(\ln(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn)\) is independent of \(X_i\) and is pulled outside the derivative, whereas
\(\ln[\rn/Q(\mathbf X)]\) remains inside because the physical hard scale can depend on the Born kinematics.

Combining the measurement expansion with Eqs.~\eqref{eq:double-singular} and \eqref{eq:delta-x-scaling}, and
integrating the higher unresolved region up to \(\rnp^{\cut}\), gives parametrically
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
\sim
\frac{1}{\rn}
\left(\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}\right)^p
\sum_{m,k}
\ln^k\!\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}
X_{i,\rm char}\,
\left.
\frac{\partial}{\partial X_i}
\right|_{\rn,\mathbf X_{\neq i}}
\left[
C_{mk}(\mathbf X)\,
\ln^m\!\frac{\rn}{Q(\mathbf X)}
\right].
\label{eq:rnp-contamination-rn}
\end{equation}
Here the coefficients \(C_{mk}(\mathbf X)\) contain the remaining nonsingular Born-kinematic dependence,
the coefficient \(c_i\) of the projection mismatch, and the integrations over the radiation variables.
The characteristic migration scale \(X_{i,\rm char}\) is kept explicitly outside the derivative generated by
the displacement in \(X_i\), while the logarithms display separately the two hierarchies
\(\rn/Q(\mathbf X)\) and \(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn\).

\medskip
\noindent
\fbox{%
\parbox{0.94\linewidth}{%
\textbf{Key point.}
Power suppression with respect to the \emph{higher} unresolved limit does not imply that the
corresponding projection effect is nonsingular in the \emph{lower} resolution variable.
The reason is that the projection mismatch multiplies a spectrum that is already singular in
\(\rn\). Thus, even if the relative mismatch vanishes as
\((\rnp/\rn)^p\) for \(\rnp/\rn\to0\), after integrating the higher unresolved region up to
\(\rnp^{\cut}\) it produces an absolute correction scaling parametrically as
\(\rn^{-1}(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn)^p\), multiplied by the explicit migration derivative, logarithms, and
nonsingular Born-kinematic coefficients.
The correction is therefore suppressed relative to the leading singular spectrum at fixed
\(\rn\), but its convergence is nonuniform as \(\rn\) becomes small.
}%
}
\medskip

For \(p=1\), the origin of the additional inverse power of \(\rn\) can be seen directly. Suppressing only
the logarithmic variation and the Born-dependent migration factor for this intermediate power-counting step,
\[
\frac{1}{\rn}
\int_0^{\rnp^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rnp}{\rnp}
\left(\frac{\rnp}{\rn}\right)
=
\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn^2}.
\]
Restoring all factors, the \(p=1\) specialization of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rnp-contamination-rn} is therefore
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
&\sim
\frac{1}{\rn}
\left(\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}\right)
\sum_{m,k}
\ln^k\!\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}
X_{i,\rm char}\,
\left.
\frac{\partial}{\partial X_i}
\right|_{\rn,\mathbf X_{\neq i}}
\left[
C_{mk}(\mathbf X)\,
\ln^m\!\frac{\rn}{Q(\mathbf X)}
\right]
\nonumber\\
&=
\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn^2}
\sum_{m,k}
\ln^k\!\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}
X_{i,\rm char}\,
\left.
\frac{\partial}{\partial X_i}
\right|_{\rn,\mathbf X_{\neq i}}
\left[
C_{mk}(\mathbf X)\,
\ln^m\!\frac{\rn}{Q(\mathbf X)}
\right].
\label{eq:p1-scaling}
\end{align}
The derivative in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p1-scaling} therefore acts on the Born-dependent coefficient and on the
lower-resolution logarithm through any Born dependence of \(Q(\mathbf X)\), but not on
\(X_{i,\rm char}\) or the explicit higher-cut logarithm. The migration derivative still originates from the
shifted measurement function, whereas the logarithms originate from the singular radiative dynamics.

Equation~\eqref{eq:p1-scaling} should not be interpreted as a genuine \(1/\rn^2\) singularity that can be
extrapolated to \(\rn\to0\) at fixed \(\rnp^{\cut}\). It is an asymptotic estimate valid only in the strongly
ordered region \mbox{$Q\gg\rn\gg\rnp^{\cut}$}.
In that region the underlying lower-resolution spectrum supplies
the factor \(1/\rn\), while the integration over the higher unresolved emission together with the linear
projection mismatch supplies the additional relative factor \(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn\). Thus at any fixed
\(\rn>0\) the correction vanishes when \(\rnp^{\cut}\to0\), but the convergence is nonuniform in \(\rn\):
the relevant expansion parameter is \(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn\), not \(\rnp^{\cut}/Q\). Once
\(\rn\) becomes parametrically comparable to \(\rnp^{\cut}\), the strong-ordering expansion used to derive
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rnp-contamination-rn}--\eqref{eq:p1-scaling} breaks down.

The hard scale \(Q\) therefore remains important through the logarithmic structure of the underlying
\(\rn\) spectrum, while the additional power suppression induced by the projection is controlled by the ratio
of the two neighboring resolution scales, \(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn\).


\subsection{Comparable or inverted cuts and uniform nested infrared locality}
\label{subsec:uniform-nested-locality}

The strong-ordering estimate of Sec.~\ref{subsec:strongly-ordered-higher-projection} is a statement at
fixed $\rn$ and cannot be extrapolated to configurations with a finite neighboring ratio
$z=\rnp/\rn$.  At fixed $\rn$, if $\rn\gg\rnp^{\cut}$ the higher technical cut enforces
$z\ll1$, whereas for $\rn$ comparable to or smaller than $\rnp^{\cut}$ it need not do so.  Particular
resolution variables can impose additional kinematic restrictions on the finite allowed range of $z$.
The singular spectrum in this region must therefore be analyzed directly in the simultaneous unresolved
limit, independently of the later integration over $\rn$.

\subsubsection{Simultaneous limit and uniform nested infrared locality}

To describe the simultaneous region, introduce
\begin{equation}
\epsilon=\frac{\rn}{Q(\mathbf X)},
\qquad
z=\frac{\rnp}{\rn},
\label{eq:epsilon-z-def}
\end{equation}
and consider a family of \((N+2)\)-parton configurations
\(\Ph_{N+2}(\epsilon,z,\omega)\), where the underlying \(N\)-body coordinates are held fixed and
\(\omega\) denotes dimensionless splitting, angular and recoil variables. The simultaneous unresolved
limit is
\begin{equation}
\epsilon\to0,
\qquad
z=\mathcal O(1),
\label{eq:simultaneous-unresolved}
\end{equation}
where \(z\) is understood to remain finite inside the kinematically allowed domain of the particular
resolution variables. Thus \(\rn\) and \(\rnp\) approach zero together rather than hierarchically.

Before imposing any locality property on the projection map, a convenient schematic form of the singular
measure in this region is
\begin{equation}
d\sigma_{N+2}^{\rm sing}
\sim
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}\,
d\mu(z,\omega)\,
\mathcal F\!\left(
\mathbf X,z,\omega,
\ln\epsilon
\right),
\label{eq:simultaneous-singular-measure}
\end{equation}
where \(d\mu(z,\omega)\) contains the remaining unresolved-emission measure and reduces to a logarithmic
\(dz/z\)-type measure in the strongly ordered \(z\to0\) limit. No expansion in \(z\) is made when \(z\) is
finite. The normalized migration \(\Delta_{x_i}\) can depend on \(\epsilon\), \(z\), and \(\omega\).
Combining Eq.~\eqref{eq:simultaneous-singular-measure} with the measurement-delta expansion that generates
the migration derivative gives the general simultaneous-region formula
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
&\sim
\frac{1}{\rn}
X_{i,\rm char}\,
\left.
\frac{\partial}{\partial X_i}
\right|_{\rn,\mathbf X_{\neq i}}
\left[
\mathcal I_i(\epsilon,\mathbf X;z_{\max})
\right],
\\
\mathcal I_i(\epsilon,\mathbf X;z_{\max})
&\equiv
\int_0^{z_{\max}}
d\mu(z,\omega)\,
\Delta_{x_i}(\epsilon,z,\omega)\,
\mathcal F(\mathbf X,z,\omega,\ln\epsilon).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:general-simultaneous-migration}
\end{equation}
Here \(z_{\max}\) is set jointly by the higher technical cut and the kinematic range. Equation~\eqref{eq:general-simultaneous-migration}
makes no nested-locality assumption and is the appropriate starting point for any map. If \(\Delta_{x_i}\) has a
nonzero limit over a region of nonvanishing radiation measure, \(\mathcal I_i\) need not contain a positive
power of \(\epsilon\), and the migration can retain the leading \(1/\rn\) singular power. Pointwise convergence
at every fixed \(z\) is not sufficient if it is nonuniform: a shrinking region in \(z\) can retain an
\(\mathcal O(1)\) mismatch, preventing the \(\epsilon\to0\) limit from being interchanged with the radiation
integral. Such a region must be isolated and power counted separately. A particular observable can still be
protected by an accidental preservation or cancellation, but this does not provide a generic
Born-differential event definition.

To obtain map-level control of \(\mathcal I_i\), it is useful to distinguish a property of the projection map
from a property of the measured observable. Ordinary one-step infrared locality of \(\Pi_{N+1}\) requires
that, at fixed nonzero \(\rn\),
removing the additional unresolved emission sends the projected configuration to the same \((N+1)\)-body
infrared limit as the exact configuration. This controls \(\rnp\to0\) at fixed \(\rn\), but does not by
itself guarantee uniform behavior when the projected \((N+1)\)-body state simultaneously approaches its own
\(N\)-body unresolved limit.

We shall say that \(\Pi_{N+1}\) has \emph{uniform nested infrared locality} if, whenever an exact
\((N+2)\)-parton configuration approaches an underlying \(N\)-parton configuration in the simultaneous
limit of Eq.~\eqref{eq:simultaneous-unresolved}, the projected \((N+1)\)-parton configuration approaches the
\emph{same} underlying \(N\)-parton point, uniformly for finite kinematically allowed values of \(z\).
Equivalently, for every finite set of smooth IRC-safe coordinates
\(\mathbf Y=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_s)\) that characterize the relevant lower-multiplicity kinematics, with finite
nonsingular characteristic scales \(Y_{a,\rm char}\), define
\[
\Delta_{y_a}(\epsilon,z,\omega)
\equiv
\frac{
Y_a(\Ph_{N+2}(\epsilon,z,\omega))
-Y_a(\Pi_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+2}(\epsilon,z,\omega)))
}{Y_{a,\rm char}}.
\]
The map must then satisfy
\begin{equation}
\lim_{\epsilon\to0}
\sup_{0\le z\le z_*}
\max_a
\left|\Delta_{y_a}(\epsilon,z,\omega)\right|
=0,
\label{eq:uniform-nested-locality-definition}
\end{equation}
for every fixed \(z_*\) whose interval lies inside the kinematically allowed simultaneous-unresolved region,
and for fixed \(\omega\) away from additional singular boundaries. No universal numerical value of
\(z_*\) is assumed here. The quantification over lower-multiplicity coordinates is what makes
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform-nested-locality-definition} a property of the map rather than of one chosen observable.
The uniformity in \(z\) is essential: a map that is local only for \(z\to0\) but approaches a different
lower-multiplicity point along some allowed path with finite \(z\) does not satisfy uniform nested infrared
locality.

The qualifier \emph{nested} refers to the fact that the \(N+2\to N+1\) projection must remain local not only
with respect to its own unresolved emission, but also while the projected \((N+1)\)-parton state itself
approaches the next lower \(N\)-parton limit. The qualifier \emph{uniform} stresses that this agreement must
persist for finite allowed values of \(z\), rather than only after taking \(z\to0\) first. Operationally, the
two operations ``remove the higher unresolved emission'' and ``approach the lower unresolved configuration''
must lead to the same underlying \(N\)-body kinematics.

Once the map has uniform nested infrared locality, ordinary IRC safety of a particular observable \(X_i\) is
enough to imply
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{x_i}
\longrightarrow0
\qquad
(\epsilon\to0,\ z\text{ finite and allowed}).
\label{eq:irc-safe-consequence-uniform-map}
\end{equation}
Thus the vanishing of the normalized migration \(\Delta_{x_i}\), or equivalently of \(\Delta X_i\) for
finite nonzero \(X_{i,\rm char}\), should not be regarded as an additional independent requirement on every
IRC-safe observable. What IRC safety alone does \emph{not} guarantee is
that an arbitrary projection map approaches the same lower-multiplicity point, nor does it determine the
rate at which the difference in Eq.~\eqref{eq:irc-safe-consequence-uniform-map} vanishes.

When this approach is power-like, the latter rate can be parametrized as
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{x_i}(\epsilon,z,\omega)
=
\epsilon^{q_i}
 h_i(z,\omega)
+\mathcal O(\epsilon^{q_i+\delta_i}),
\qquad q_i>0,
\label{eq:uniform-nested-locality-power}
\end{equation}
with \(h_i\) finite for finite allowed \(z\), and with the expansion understood uniformly over the integrated
\(z\) domain. The exponent \(q_i\) is not fixed by IRC safety. It depends on
the recoil prescription, on the resolution variables and on how the particular observable responds to the
small kinematic displacement. The approach need not even be a pure power; Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform-nested-locality-power}
is a convenient parametrization when a power expansion exists. Substituting this behavior into the general
integral in Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-simultaneous-migration}, and then performing the integration over \(z\) and
the remaining radiation variables, gives a function \(G_i\). At any fixed perturbative order, it is convenient
to separate its explicit logarithms of the common lower scale by writing
\begin{equation}
G_i\!\left(\mathbf X,\ln\frac{\rn}{Q(\mathbf X)};z_{\max}\right)
=
\sum_m
\ln^m\!\frac{\rn}{Q(\mathbf X)}\,
G_{i,m}(\mathbf X;z_{\max}),
\label{eq:Gi-log-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where the coefficients \(G_{i,m}\) contain the integration over \(z\) and the remaining radiation variables,
including \(h_i\). They may themselves contain finite logarithms associated with the \(z\) integration or
with a Born-dependent endpoint \(z_{\max}\); such dependence is part of the coefficient and is differentiated
when it depends on \(X_i\). They need only remain finite in the power-counting sense, but must not contain
additional inverse powers of \(\epsilon\); such powers would signal a radiation region not isolated in the
assumed scaling. The powers of \(\ln[\rn/Q(\mathbf X)]\) also remain inside the \(X_i\) derivative.

Inserting Eqs.~\eqref{eq:uniform-nested-locality-power} and \eqref{eq:Gi-log-decomposition} into the general
formula \eqref{eq:general-simultaneous-migration} then gives
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
\sim
\frac{1}{\rn}
\sum_m
X_{i,\rm char}\,
\left.
\frac{\partial}{\partial X_i}
\right|_{\rn,\mathbf X_{\neq i}}
\left[
\left(\frac{\rn}{Q(\mathbf X)}\right)^{q_i}
G_{i,m}(\mathbf X;z_{\max})\,
\ln^m\!\frac{\rn}{Q(\mathbf X)}
\right].
\label{eq:uniform-nested-locality-spectrum}
\end{equation}
The common-scale suppression and its explicit large logarithms remain inside the migration derivative in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform-nested-locality-spectrum}. They can become numerically large and can spoil the usefulness
of a fixed-order expansion if they are not resummed, but they do not by themselves change the power
classification of the \(\rn\) singularity. For every finite logarithmic power \(m\) and every \(q_i>0\),
\[
\left(\frac{\rn}{Q(\mathbf X)}\right)^{q_i}
\ln^m\!\frac{\rn}{Q(\mathbf X)}
\longrightarrow0
\qquad (\rn/Q(\mathbf X)\to0).
\]
Thus the logarithms can enhance and modify the logarithmic structure of the correction, but they cannot
compensate the positive-power suppression implied by uniform nested locality. Parametrically a term in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform-nested-locality-spectrum} behaves as
\(\rn^{q_i-1}\ln^m[\rn/Q(\mathbf X)]\): for \(0<q_i<1\) it can still diverge as \(\rn\to0\), but only
through an integrable singularity weaker than the leading \(1/\rn\) spectrum; for \(q_i=1\) at most a
logarithmic singularity remains; and for \(q_i>1\) the spectrum-level correction vanishes up to logarithms.

In the overlap with the strongly ordered region, consistency with Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta-x-scaling} requires
\begin{equation}
h_i(z,\omega)\sim z^{p_i}
\qquad (z\to0),
\label{eq:uniform-nested-strong-overlap}
\end{equation}
up to logarithms and nonsingular coefficients. This condition is not needed to derive
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform-nested-locality-spectrum}; it connects the simultaneous-region result to the
strongly ordered estimate. The latter controls the \(z\)-dependence, while
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform-nested-locality-power} supplies the independent suppression in the common unresolved
scale \(\epsilon\).


\subsubsection{Kinematic restriction for minimized \(N\)-jettiness}

For an axis-minimized definition of \(N\)-jettiness, increasing the number of final-state jet axes enlarges
the space over which the observable is minimized. At fixed final-state parton multiplicity \(M\), this
immediately implies the monotonicity relation
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_{K+1}(\Ph_M)
\le
\mathcal T_K(\Ph_M),
\label{eq:njettiness-monotonicity}
\end{equation}
for every admissible \(K\). A stronger quantitative bound follows from the same minimization property.
Let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_K(\Ph_M)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{M} t_i^{(K)},
\qquad
t_i^{(K)}\ge0,
\label{eq:njettiness-parton-contributions}
\end{equation}
be the decomposition into the nonnegative parton contributions evaluated for a set of axes that minimizes
\(\mathcal T_K\). At least one contribution satisfies
\(t_{\max}^{(K)}\ge \mathcal T_K/M\). In the \((K+1)\)-axis minimization one may keep all the minimizing
\(K\) axes and add one extra axis aligned with the parton that gives \(t_{\max}^{(K)}\). Its contribution
then vanishes, while the minimization over an enlarged set of directions cannot increase the contributions
of the other partons. Hence, provided the axis minimization admits this alignment,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal T_{K+1}(\Ph_M)
\le
\frac{M-1}{M}\,
\mathcal T_K(\Ph_M)
}.
\label{eq:njettiness-adjacent-bound}
\end{equation}
More generally, adding \(s\) axes aligned with the \(s\) largest contributions to the minimizing
\(K\)-axis configuration gives
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_{K+s}(\Ph_M)
\le
\frac{M-s}{M}\,
\mathcal T_K(\Ph_M),
\label{eq:njettiness-multiaxis-bound}
\end{equation}
whenever the corresponding \((K+s)\)-axis minimization is admissible.

Equation~(61) of arXiv:2312.06496 is the \(K=0\), \(s=1\) specialization,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathcal T_1(\Ph_M)}{\mathcal T_0(\Ph_M)}
\le
c_M,
\qquad
c_M\equiv\frac{M-1}{M}.
\label{eq:t1-t0-multiplicity-bound}
\end{equation}
Thus \(c_2=1/2\) on a two-parton configuration and \(c_3=2/3\) on a three-parton configuration. For the
neighboring pair relevant to a generic nested step on \(\Ph_{N+2}\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:njettiness-adjacent-bound}
corresponds to \(K=N\), \(M=N+2\), and therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathcal T_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+2})}
{\mathcal T_N(\Ph_{N+2})}
\le
\frac{N+1}{N+2}.
\label{eq:njettiness-nested-pair-bound}
\end{equation}
These quantitative bounds rely on the axis-minimized definition of \(N\)-jettiness and are not assumed for
generic resolution variables.

At fixed multiplicity \(M\), the region below a technical \(\mathcal T_1\) cut is therefore
\begin{equation}
0\le\mathcal T_1
<
\min\!\left\{
\mathcal T_1^{\cut},\,
c_M\,\mathcal T_0
\right\},
\label{eq:t1-t0-effective-upper-bound}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
z_{\max}
=
\min\!\left\{
\frac{\mathcal T_1^{\cut}}{\mathcal T_0},\,
c_M
\right\},
\qquad
z\equiv\frac{\mathcal T_1}{\mathcal T_0}.
\label{eq:t1-t0-zmax}
\end{equation}
The higher technical cut is kinematically inactive whenever
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_0
<
\frac{\mathcal T_1^{\cut}}{c_M},
\label{eq:t1-cut-inactive}
\end{equation}
and becomes the limiting upper bound only above this value.

Equation~\eqref{eq:t1-cut-inactive} is useful because it identifies the scale at which the role of the
higher-multiplicity technical cut changes qualitatively. For
\(\mathcal T_0<\mathcal T_1^{\cut}/c_M\), the kinematic bound
\(\mathcal T_1\le c_M\mathcal T_0\) is stronger than the imposed cut
\(\mathcal T_1<\mathcal T_1^{\cut}\). Hence every physically allowed configuration at that value of
\(\mathcal T_0\) automatically lies below the higher cut. The latter is therefore completely inactive, and
the projected region covers the full allowed finite-ratio interval
\[
0\le z=\frac{\mathcal T_1}{\mathcal T_0}\le c_M.
\]
In particular, making the nominal ratio
\(\mathcal T_1^{\cut}/\mathcal T_0\) larger does not open an ever larger hierarchy in \(z\): once the
kinematic bound is saturated, the physically accessible ratio simply remains bounded by \(c_M\).

Conversely, for
\(\mathcal T_0>\mathcal T_1^{\cut}/c_M\), the technical cut becomes the restrictive condition and
\[
z_{\max}=\frac{\mathcal T_1^{\cut}}{\mathcal T_0}<c_M.
\]
As \(\mathcal T_0\) is increased further above \(\mathcal T_1^{\cut}\), this ratio becomes parametrically
small and the strongly ordered description becomes appropriate. Equation~\eqref{eq:t1-cut-inactive}
therefore identifies the transition between a lower-\(\mathcal T_0\) region in which finite-\(z\)
configurations are fully present and an upper region in which the higher cut itself enforces strong ordering.
It also tells us immediately whether a ``saturated'' finite-\(z\) interval exists above the lower technical
boundary: this happens only if
\(\mathcal T_0^{\cut}<\mathcal T_1^{\cut}/c_M\).
This observation is what allows the subsequent \(\mathcal T_0\) integration to be separated into regions
with genuinely different power counting.


\subsection{Observable response to projection-induced migrations}
\label{subsec:observable-response-projection}

The size of the migration in phase space is only one part of the problem. The response of the observable can
introduce a second, logically distinct nonuniformity. At fixed $\rn$, let
\begin{equation}
S_i(\rn,X_i)=\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
\label{eq:observable-spectrum-S}
\end{equation}
denote the relevant smooth singular spectrum in a region where a first-order migration expansion is meaningful,
and define its local variation scale in $X_i$ by
\begin{equation}
L_i(\rn,X_i)
=
\left|
\partial_{X_i}\ln S_i(\rn,X_i)
\right|^{-1}
=
\left|
\frac{S_i(\rn,X_i)}{\partial_{X_i}S_i(\rn,X_i)}
\right|.
\label{eq:local-variation-scale}
\end{equation}
Then a small displacement produces parametrically
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{s_i}
\equiv
\frac{\Delta S_i}{S_i}
\sim
\frac{\Delta X_i}{L_i(\rn,X_i)}
=
\Delta_{x_i}
\frac{X_{i,\rm char}}{L_i(\rn,X_i)}
\label{eq:relative-observable-sensitivity}
\end{equation}
when \(|\Delta X_i|\ll L_i\). Although both \(\Delta_{x_i}\) and \(\Delta_{s_i}\) are dimensionless, they
describe different objects. The former is the normalized kinematic displacement generated by the map; the
latter is the induced fractional response of the spectrum and also depends on its local variation scale.
They are therefore not interchangeable, and a small \(\Delta_{x_i}\) does not by itself guarantee a small
\(\Delta_{s_i}\). In a smooth region \(L_i\) is of the order of a hard or characteristic
kinematic scale, so uniform nested infrared locality of the map together with IRC safety of the observable
translates into a small projection effect. Near a kinematic boundary, Jacobian feature or Sudakov shoulder,
however, \(L_i\) can become parametrically much smaller than the hard scale. A displacement that is small in
absolute terms can then generate a large local change in the distribution. When \(|\Delta X_i|\) becomes
comparable to the distance from the nonanalytic point, the derivative expansion itself is no longer uniform
and the effect is better viewed as migration across the feature rather than as a small derivative correction.

The preceding discussion deliberately stops at the singular spectrum differential in $\rn$.
It concerns a problem that is specific to the \emph{LHE event definition}: below $\rnp^{\cut}$ an exact
$\Ph_{N+2}$ configuration is discarded and the projected $\widehat\Ph_{N+1}$ configuration is retained as the
physical event representative. In a pure fixed-order calculation the same higher-multiplicity partition is
only an internal organization of an exact NLO contribution and does not generate this residual projection
effect. We now integrate the fixed-$\rn$ result to obtain its consequences for NNLO LHE observables. The
separate question of dynamical truncation and kinematic power corrections in a pure fixed-order NNLO calculation,
and of using such a sliced NNLO result inside an $\mathrm{N}^3\mathrm{LO}$ slicing calculation, is postponed to
Sec.~\ref{sec:fixed-order-nested-slicing}.

\section{From the lower singular spectrum to NNLO LHE observables}
\label{sec:nnlo-differential-predictions}

This section completes the LHE-specific problem studied in Sec.~\ref{sec:higher-singular-spectrum}. The
higher cut $\rnp^{\cut}$ does not truncate or approximate the fixed-order dynamics: it merely partitions the
dynamically exact NLO $(N+1)$ contribution. A dependence on it appears here only because, for the LHE sample,
the measurement on exact $\Ph_{N+2}$ kinematics is replaced below the cut by the measurement on the projected
$\widehat\Ph_{N+1}$ event. The resulting effect is therefore a \emph{kinematic event-definition effect}.
It is absent if the same NNLO calculation is analyzed purely at fixed order with exact partonic kinematics.

The local analysis of Sec.~\ref{sec:higher-singular-spectrum} kept $\rn$ differential. We now perform the
logically separate integration over the resolved region,
\begin{equation}
\left.\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}\frac{d\sigma}{dX_i}\right|_{\rn>\rn^{\cut}}
=
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^{\rn^{\rm max}} d\rn\,
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}.
\label{eq:integrated-higher-projection-definition}
\end{equation}
This integration converts the local behavior of the singular $\rn$ spectrum into a finite-cut dependence of
NNLO LHE distributions. It should not be confused with the lower-region approximation used in a sliced
fixed-order NNLO calculation. That second problem contains both a dynamical truncation remainder and
kinematic projection power corrections and is discussed only in Sec.~\ref{sec:fixed-order-nested-slicing}.

\subsection{Higher $\Ph_{N+2}\to\widehat\Ph_{N+1}$ LHE projection}
\label{subsec:higher-projection-nnlo}

\subsubsection{Strongly ordered hierarchy}

To see explicitly how the neighboring-cut ratio arises after integrating over the lower resolution variable,
consider first only the power-counting part of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rnp-contamination-rn}, keeping the distribution
differential in \(X_i\). For \(p>0\),
\begin{align}
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^Q
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}
\left(\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn}\right)^p
&=
(\rnp^{\cut})^p
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^Q
\frac{d\rn}{\rn^{p+1}}
\nonumber\\
&=
\frac{1}{p}
\left[
\left(\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn^{\cut}}\right)^p
-
\left(\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^p
\right].
\label{eq:integrated-power-counting}
\end{align}
Thus, when \(Q\gg\rn^{\cut}\gg\rnp^{\cut}\), the integral is parametrically dominated by its lower endpoint.
Restoring the migration derivative and the logarithmic structure, the correction integrated only over
\(\rn\) has the schematic scaling
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{dX_i}
\equiv
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^Q d\rn\,
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{(\rnp)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rn\,dX_i}
\sim
\frac{1}{p}
\left(
\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn^{\cut}}
\right)^p
\sum_{m,k}
\ln^k\!\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{\rn^{\cut}}
X_{i,\rm char}\,
\left.
\frac{\partial}{\partial X_i}
\right|_{\rn^{\cut},\mathbf X_{\neq i}}
\left[
\widetilde C_{mk}(\mathbf X)\,
\ln^m\!\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{Q(\mathbf X)}
\right].
\label{eq:integrated-ratio-scaling}
\end{equation}
The coefficient functions \(\widetilde C_{mk}(\mathbf X)\) are not new independent ingredients. They denote
linear combinations of the coefficients \(C_{m'k'}(\mathbf X)\) appearing before the \(\rn\) integration in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rnp-contamination-rn}--\eqref{eq:p1-scaling}. The integration reshuffles the powers of
\(\ln(\rn/Q)\) and \(\ln(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn)\), so the coefficients multiplying the logarithms evaluated at
\(\rn=\rn^{\cut}\) are in general different linear combinations of the original \(C_{m'k'}\). As in the
fixed-\(\rn\) formulas, the higher-cut logarithm is held fixed under the \(X_i\) derivative, while the
lower-resolution logarithm remains inside because \(Q=Q(\mathbf X)\).

The second term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:integrated-power-counting}, proportional to
\((\rnp^{\cut}/Q)^p\), is parametrically smaller in this hierarchy. Equation~\eqref{eq:integrated-ratio-scaling}
therefore states that the \emph{integration over \(\rn\)} converts the local suppression
\((\rnp^{\cut}/\rn)^p\) into a dependence controlled by the ratio of the two technical cuts,
\((\rnp^{\cut}/\rn^{\cut})^p\). It does not imply a nonzero fully inclusive correction. If \(X_i\) is
subsequently integrated over its complete range, the migration derivative becomes a boundary term and
cancels, provided no fiducial boundary or other non-preserved measurement prevents that cancellation.

\subsubsection{Comparable cuts and uniform nested scaling}

The result in Eq.~\eqref{eq:integrated-ratio-scaling} must not be extrapolated to a situation in which the
two technical cuts are comparable. For a generic pair of resolution variables we do not assume any universal
kinematic upper bound on the ratio \(\rnp/\rn\). If, for example,
\begin{equation}
\rnp^{\cut}>\rn^{\cut},
\label{eq:inverted-cuts}
\end{equation}
it is useful to make the purely algebraic decomposition
\begin{equation}
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^Q d\rn
=
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^{\rnp^{\cut}}d\rn
+
\int_{\rnp^{\cut}}^Qd\rn.
\label{eq:split-rn-inverted-cuts}
\end{equation}
In the second region the higher technical cut itself enforces
\(\rnp/\rn<\rnp^{\cut}/\rn<1\), and sufficiently far above
\(\rnp^{\cut}\) one recovers the strongly ordered situation of
Sec.~\ref{subsec:strongly-ordered-higher-projection}. In the first region, by contrast, the condition
\(\rnp<\rnp^{\cut}\) alone does not force \(\rnp/\rn\) to be small. The same issue can occur for comparable
cuts \(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn^{\cut}=\mathcal O(1)\), even if the higher cut is somewhat smaller, because the region
near the lower endpoint need not be parametrically strongly ordered.

Equation~\eqref{eq:split-rn-inverted-cuts} should not be interpreted as a universal separation between a
non-strongly-ordered and a strongly ordered domain. It only illustrates what follows from the technical cuts
alone. Particular choices of resolution variables can impose additional kinematic restrictions on the
allowed values of \(\rnp\) at fixed \(\rn\). In that case the actual projected region is
\begin{equation}
0\le \rnp <
\min\!\left\{
\rnp^{\cut},\,\rnp^{\rm kin,max}(\rn,\mathbf X,\ldots)
\right\},
\label{eq:generic-kinematic-cap}
\end{equation}
and the point at which the technical cut becomes active is determined by the equality of the two entries in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:generic-kinematic-cap}, not necessarily by \(\rn=\rnp^{\cut}\).

It follows in particular that inserting \(\rnp^{\cut}/\rn^{\cut}=\mathcal O(1)\) into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:integrated-ratio-scaling} is not a prediction of an \(\mathcal O(1)\) projection effect. It is
only a signal that the expansion in the neighboring-scale ratio has been pushed outside its domain of
validity. The behavior of the projection map in the simultaneous unresolved limit has to be examined
separately, together with any kinematic restrictions specific to the chosen resolution variables.

For a generic pair of resolution variables, and in the absence of an additional kinematic cap that becomes
active before \(\rn\sim\rnp^{\cut}\), the contribution from the interval displayed in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:split-rn-inverted-cuts} has the illustrative power counting
\begin{align}
\int_{\rn^{\cut}}^{\rnp^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}
\left(\frac{\rn}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
&=
\frac{1}{q_i}
\left[
\left(\frac{\rnp^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
-
\left(\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
\right].
\label{eq:uniform-nested-integrated-region}
\end{align}
If the cuts are lowered together at a fixed ratio
\begin{equation}
\rnp^{\cut}=\kappa\,\rn^{\cut},
\qquad \kappa=\mathcal O(1),
\label{eq:fixed-kappa-cuts}
\end{equation}
this gives
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm proj}^{\rm sim}
\sim
\left(\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
\frac{\kappa^{q_i}-1}{q_i}
\times\text{logs and finite coefficients},
\label{eq:fixed-kappa-uniform-nested-scaling}
\end{equation}
for this interval when \(\kappa>1\). The formula is not universal: if the chosen resolution variables impose
a stronger kinematic restriction, the integration range and therefore the finite coefficient multiplying the
common-scale suppression must be modified accordingly. The robust conclusion is only that, for a uniformly
nested-local map, a finite cut ratio does not by itself imply an order-one projection error when both absolute
cuts are taken to zero together.

\subsubsection{Axis-minimized $N$-jettiness}

Using the kinematic bounds derived in Sec.~\ref{subsec:uniform-nested-locality}, the corresponding integrated
region is obtained only after the fixed-$\mathcal T_0$ spectrum has been understood. In particular,

If \(\mathcal T_0^{\cut}<\mathcal T_1^{\cut}/c_M\), the interval in which the kinematic bound rather than
the technical \(\mathcal T_1\) cut sets the full allowed \(z\) range is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_0^{\cut}
<\mathcal T_0
<\frac{\mathcal T_1^{\cut}}{c_M}.
\label{eq:t1-t0-saturated-interval}
\end{equation}
For a power-like nested-local mismatch, the common-scale part of its integral over this interval behaves as
\begin{align}
\int_{\mathcal T_0^{\cut}}^{\mathcal T_1^{\cut}/c_M}
\frac{d\mathcal T_0}{\mathcal T_0}
\left(\frac{\mathcal T_0}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
&=
\frac{1}{q_i}
\left[
\left(\frac{\mathcal T_1^{\cut}}{c_M Q}\right)^{q_i}
-
\left(\frac{\mathcal T_0^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
\right],
\label{eq:t1-t0-uniform-integrated-region}
\end{align}
up to logarithms and finite coefficients from the integration over \(z\) in its allowed range. At fixed
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_1^{\cut}
=\kappa\,\mathcal T_0^{\cut},
\label{eq:t1-t0-fixed-kappa}
\end{equation}
this saturated interval exists for \(\kappa>c_M\), and the common-scale factor becomes
\begin{equation}
\left(\frac{\mathcal T_0^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^{q_i}
\frac{(\kappa/c_M)^{q_i}-1}{q_i},
\label{eq:t1-t0-fixed-kappa-scaling}
\end{equation}
up to logarithms and finite coefficients. Hence a large ratio of the technical cuts cannot make the physical
ratio \(\mathcal T_1/\mathcal T_0\) arbitrarily large at fixed multiplicity; it simply saturates at \(c_M\).

\subsection{Process-specific NNLO observables}

\subsubsection{Drell--Yan illustration}
\label{subsubsec:dy-illustration}

For Drell--Yan double-real kinematics, the relevant configuration is \(\Ph_2\), and therefore
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_1(\Ph_2)
\le\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal T_0(\Ph_2),
\qquad
0\le z=\frac{\mathcal T_1}{\mathcal T_0}\le\frac12.
\label{eq:dy-t1-t0-bound}
\end{equation}
The higher-cut transition consequently occurs at
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_0=2\,\mathcal T_1^{\cut}.
\label{eq:dy-t1cut-transition}
\end{equation}


Drell--Yan production provides a useful illustration. Taking
\begin{equation}
\rn=\mathcal T_0,
\qquad
\rnp=\mathcal T_1,
\qquad
Q\sim M_{\ell\ell},
\label{eq:dy-resolution-identification}
\end{equation}
the vector-boson rapidity is preserved by the projection in both the FKS and $\mathcal T_0^{\mathrm FR}$-preserving mappings and therefore has no migration of the type discussed
here. On the other end, the transverse momenta of the decay leptons are IRC safe but are not preserved by either mapping. For the local singular spectrum at fixed \(\mathcal T_0\), two features are important. First,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dy-t1-t0-bound} guarantees that the physical ratio \(\mathcal T_1/\mathcal T_0\) never exceeds
\(1/2\) on the \(\Ph_2\) configurations relevant to the higher projection, independently of the ratio of the
two technical cuts. Second, since both maps have uniform nested infrared locality, the exact and projected states
approach the same Born configuration as \(\mathcal T_0\to0\) for all finite allowed values
\(0\le\mathcal T_1/\mathcal T_0\le1/2\). IRC safety then implies that the absolute displacement of the lepton
transverse momentum vanishes in this common unresolved limit.


The important exception is the region
\begin{equation}
p_{T,\ell}\simeq \frac{M_{\ell\ell}}{2},
\label{eq:dy-sudakov-shoulder}
\end{equation}
which corresponds to the Born kinematic endpoint and develops a Sudakov shoulder once QCD radiation is
included. There the spectrum varies rapidly and its fixed-order derivatives are enhanced. Consequently the
local variation scale in Eq.~\eqref{eq:local-variation-scale} becomes small. A projection-induced shift
\(\Delta p_{T,\ell}\) can therefore be much smaller than \(M_{\ell\ell}\) and still be comparable to the
scale over which the spectrum changes. Large local discrepancies near the shoulder are thus compatible with
a map satisfying uniform nested infrared locality: they need not signal a failure of the nested unresolved
limit, but rather a nonuniform response of the measurement to a small kinematic migration. Very close to the
shoulder, where
\begin{equation}
\left|p_{T,\ell}-\frac{M_{\ell\ell}}{2}\right|
\lesssim
|\Delta p_{T,\ell}|,
\label{eq:shoulder-migration-region}
\end{equation}
one should not expect the linearized measurement expansion to be quantitatively reliable; the exact
migration across the shoulder must be retained.

In summary, these two sources of nonuniformity should therefore be kept conceptually separate. Strong ordering can fail
because neighboring resolution scales remain at a finite ratio inside their allowed kinematic domain, in
which case uniform nested infrared locality controls the simultaneous unresolved limit. Independently, even
an IRC-safe observable can have a nonuniform differential response to a uniformly vanishing displacement when
its spectrum contains a sharp kinematic feature. For the \(\mathcal T_1/\mathcal T_0\) pair there is in
addition the multiplicity-dependent kinematic bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:t1-t0-multiplicity-bound}. The
Drell--Yan behavior described above is consistent with the combination of this kinematic restriction and
nested locality away from the shoulder, while the largest local projection differences in the lepton
transverse-momentum distribution can still arise from the nonuniform response near the shoulder.

If \(\mathcal T_1^{\cut}\) is held fixed while \(\mathcal T_0^{\cut}\) is made much smaller, the higher cut
becomes kinematically inactive near the lower endpoint and the fully allowed interval
\(0\le\mathcal T_1/\mathcal T_0\le1/2\) is sampled there. Lowering \(\mathcal T_0^{\cut}\) further therefore
does not generate a growing cut-ratio enhancement. The transition to a cut-limited region occurs only around
\(\mathcal T_0\sim2\mathcal T_1^{\cut}\); correspondingly, for a power-like nested-local mismatch the
residual integrated scale associated with the saturated region is parametrically set by
\((2\mathcal T_1^{\cut}/Q)^{q_i}\), up to logarithms and finite coefficients.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{$Z+1$-jet}{Z+1-jet} illustration}
\label{subsubsec:z1jet-illustration}

The colour-singlet-plus-jet case provides a more stringent test of the higher projection than Drell--Yan.
For the NNLO $Z+1$-jet calculation the relevant multiplicities and neighboring resolution variables are
\begin{equation}
\Ph_1\leftrightarrow Z+1\text{ parton},\qquad
\Ph_2\leftrightarrow Z+2\text{ partons},\qquad
\Ph_3\leftrightarrow Z+3\text{ partons},
\label{eq:z1j-multiplicities}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\rnm=q_T,\qquad
\rn=\mathcal T_1,\qquad
\rnp=\mathcal T_2.
\label{eq:z1j-resolution-identification}
\end{equation}
The LHE replacement relevant here is therefore
\begin{equation}
\Ph_3\longrightarrow
\widehat\Ph_2=\Pi_2(\Ph_3)
\qquad
\text{for }\mathcal T_2<\mathcal T_2^{\cut}.
\label{eq:z1j-higher-projection}
\end{equation}
The simultaneous lower unresolved limit is conveniently described by
\begin{equation}
\epsilon=\frac{\mathcal T_1}{Q}\to0,
\qquad
z=\frac{\mathcal T_2}{\mathcal T_1}=\mathcal O(1).
\label{eq:z1j-simultaneous-limit}
\end{equation}
For the axis-minimized jettiness variables on a three-parton configuration,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:njettiness-nested-pair-bound} gives the kinematic restriction
\begin{equation}
0\le z\le\frac23.
\label{eq:z1j-t2-t1-bound}
\end{equation}
Thus the physically relevant finite-$z$ region is compact, but it need not be strongly ordered.

In the setup considered here $q_T$ is the actual lower process-defining variable. The quantity
$\mathcal T_0$ introduced below is not used as a generation cut; it is only a useful diagnostic coordinate
for identifying the point approached on the limiting one-jet Born manifold.

A qualitative difference from Drell--Yan is that preserving the colour-singlet momentum does not completely
fix the limiting $\Ph_1$ point. At one-parton Born level, transverse momentum conservation gives
\begin{equation}
\vec p_{T,J}=-\vec q_T,
\qquad
p_{T,J}=q_T,
\label{eq:z1j-born-transverse-balance}
\end{equation}
while the longitudinal jet coordinate remains independent at fixed $q^\mu$. Locally one may therefore think
of the limiting one-jet Born manifold as being characterized by $q^\mu$ together with one additional
longitudinal coordinate, which may equivalently be represented by the jet rapidity or, in a suitable region,
by $\mathcal T_0$. Schematically, for a beam-thrust-like definition,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_0(\Ph_1)
\sim
q_T\,e^{-|y_J-Y_Z|},
\label{eq:z1j-t0-born-coordinate}
\end{equation}
up to the precise convention used for the resolution measure. Hence preservation of $q^\mu$ alone does not
imply preservation of the complete one-jet Born configuration.

This point is directly relevant for a $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ projection that preserves the vector-boson
four-momentum and the lower resolution variable $\mathcal T_1$ by construction, but does not in general preserve $\mathcal T_0$ exactly.
The latter fact by itself does \emph{not} imply a failure of uniform nested infrared locality. The decisive
quantity is the scaling of
\begin{equation}
\Delta\mathcal T_0
=
\mathcal T_0(\Ph_3)
-
\mathcal T_0\!\left(\Pi_2(\Ph_3)\right)
\label{eq:z1j-delta-t0}
\end{equation}
along the finite-$z$ paths of Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-simultaneous-limit}. Using \(Q\) as its characteristic scale,
denote the corresponding normalized migration by
\[
\Delta_{t_0}
\equiv
\frac{\Delta\mathcal T_0}{Q}.
\]
A nested-local map may perfectly well satisfy
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{t_0}
\sim
\left(\frac{\mathcal T_1}{Q}\right)^q
h_0(z,\omega),
\qquad q>0,
\label{eq:z1j-t0-nested-local-scaling}
\end{equation}
with $h_0$ finite over the allowed range $0\le z\le2/3$. By contrast, a nonzero finite limit
\begin{equation}
\lim_{\mathcal T_1\to0}
\Delta_{t_0}
=h_0(z,\omega)\neq0
\qquad
\text{at fixed }z>0
\label{eq:z1j-t0-nested-failure}
\end{equation}
would demonstrate that exact and projected configurations approach different points of the limiting
$\Ph_1$ manifold.

The geometric reason why this question is not answered by exact preservation of $\mathcal T_1$ is simple.
The condition $\mathcal T_1\to0$ measures the approach to the one-jet manifold, and is therefore naturally
viewed as controlling a direction normal to that manifold. Every point of $\Ph_1$ has $\mathcal T_1=0$.
Preserving $\mathcal T_1$ consequently does not by itself prevent a displacement tangential to $\Ph_1$,
for example in the jet rapidity or $\mathcal T_0$ direction. Uniform nested infrared locality requires that
such tangential displacements vanish as the common unresolved scale is taken to zero.

We return to this locality test after presenting the numerical comparison of the two mappings below.

Jet observables introduce a second effect that is logically distinct from the locality of the projection map.
The $\mathcal T_1$ resolution variable is defined through its own exclusive clustering or minimization,
whereas the analysis commonly uses inclusive anti-$k_T$ jets. The anti-$k_T$ measurement is only piecewise
smooth in phase space: the clustering history changes across boundaries where two candidate distances become
equal, for example $d_{ij}=d_{kB}$, while the identity of the leading jet changes across
\begin{equation}
p_{T,j_a}=p_{T,j_b}.
\label{eq:z1j-leading-jet-boundary}
\end{equation}
These are codimension-one boundaries, since one equality separates neighboring phase-space regions with
different clustering or jet ordering. IRC safety guarantees the correct soft and collinear limit, but not
uniform differentiability of the measurement across such boundaries.

A particularly relevant boundary is the analysis requirement $p_{T,j_1}>p_T^{\min}$. For the projection-induced
shift $\Delta p_{T,j_1}=p_{T,j_1}(\Ph_3)-p_{T,j_1}(\widehat\Ph_2)$ one has distributionally
\begin{equation}
\Theta\!\left(\widehat p_{T,j_1}-p_T^{\min}\right)
-
\Theta\!\left(p_{T,j_1}-p_T^{\min}\right)
\simeq
-\Delta p_{T,j_1}\,
\delta\!\left(p_{T,j_1}-p_T^{\min}\right).
\label{eq:z1j-pt-threshold-migration}
\end{equation}
Thus a parametrically small displacement can generate a sizeable relative correction when the jet spectrum
is steep or the event lies close to a clustering, ordering or acceptance boundary. This enhancement does not
by itself signal a failure of nested locality: for a smooth underlying distribution the phase-space layer
that changes classification still shrinks with the size of the displacement.

For the local diagnostics below, use the same normalized-shift convention,
\[
\Delta_{p_{T,j_1}}^{\rm anti-k_T}
\equiv
\frac{\Delta p_{T,j_1}^{\rm anti-k_T}}{Q},
\qquad
\Delta_{y_{j_1}}^{\rm anti-k_T}
\equiv
\Delta y_{j_1}^{\rm anti-k_T},
\]
where the rapidity shift is already dimensionless.

There is nevertheless an important asymptotic protection for the leading-jet transverse momentum if the
$\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ map preserves $q^\mu$. In the strict one-jet limit both exact and projected
configurations must satisfy Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-born-transverse-balance}, independently of the jet rapidity.
Therefore, provided both configurations genuinely approach a one-jet limit,
\begin{equation}
\Delta p_{T,j_1}\longrightarrow0
\qquad
(\mathcal T_1\to0,\ z\text{ finite}),
\label{eq:z1j-leading-pt-protected}
\end{equation}
even if the limiting jet rapidities differ. A persistent nonzero plateau in $\Delta p_{T,j_1}$ would thus
signal a stronger problem than the mere non-preservation of $\mathcal T_0$. By contrast, a plateau in
$\Delta_{y_{j_1}}^{\rm anti-k_T}$ or in $\Delta_{t_0}$ at fixed finite $z$ would directly diagnose a failure to
approach the same point on the one-jet Born manifold.

The decisive test of the nested-locality properties of the two mappings is local: apply both projections to
the same exact $\Ph_3$ configurations and study, in bins of
\begin{equation}
z=\frac{\mathcal T_2}{\mathcal T_1},
\label{eq:z1j-z-diagnostic}
\end{equation}
the approach to the lower unresolved limit of
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{t_0},
\qquad
\Delta_{y_{j_1}}^{\rm anti-k_T},
\qquad
\Delta_{p_{T,j_1}}^{\rm anti-k_T}.
\label{eq:z1j-local-diagnostics}
\end{equation}
A positive-power fall with $\mathcal T_1/Q$ establishes nested locality for the corresponding coordinate,
whereas a nonzero plateau at fixed finite $z$ signals its failure. For the fiducial jet cuts one should
separately monitor the signed classification migration
\begin{equation}
\Theta\!\left(\widehat p_{T,j_1}-p_T^{\min}\right)
-
\Theta\!\left(p_{T,j_1}-p_T^{\min}\right)
\label{eq:z1j-signed-threshold-migration}
\end{equation}
for the relevant values of $p_T^{\min}$.

The $Z+1$-jet example therefore illustrates why exact preservation of the chosen resolution variable is only
one part of a robust event projection. The map must also approach the correct point of the lower Born manifold
in all tangential coordinates relevant to the measurement, while the observable itself must be examined for
nonuniform response near jet-clustering and fiducial boundaries.

For the actual NNLO LHE comparison, the numerical hierarchy of the technical cuts must be treated using their
actual definitions. In the
setup considered here the lower cut is dynamical,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_1^{\cut}
=10^{-5}\,m_T^Z,
\qquad
m_T^Z=\sqrt{M_Z^2+q_T^2},
\label{eq:z1j-dynamic-t1cut}
\end{equation}
whereas the higher cut is taken to be fixed,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_2^{\cut}=10^{-3}\ {\rm GeV}.
\label{eq:z1j-fixed-t2cut}
\end{equation}
The neighboring-cut ratio is therefore event dependent,
\begin{equation}
\rho(\Ph)
\equiv
\frac{\mathcal T_2^{\cut}}{\mathcal T_1^{\cut}}
=
\frac{100\ {\rm GeV}}{m_T^Z},
\label{eq:z1j-cut-ratio}
\end{equation}
and is generically of order unity for electroweak transverse masses. In particular, the numerical values
$10^{-5}$ and $10^{-3}$ should not be interpreted as two directly comparable dimensionless cuts differing
by two orders of magnitude.

Combining Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-t2-t1-bound} with the fixed $\mathcal T_2$ cut shows that the higher technical cut
is kinematically inactive whenever
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_1
<
\frac32\,\mathcal T_2^{\cut}
=
1.5\times10^{-3}\ {\rm GeV},
\label{eq:z1j-t2cut-inactive}
\end{equation}
so that the full finite-$z$ interval $0\le z\le2/3$ is projected there. The corresponding saturated interval
above the lower technical boundary exists only if
\begin{equation}
10^{-5}m_T^Z
<1.5\times10^{-3}\ {\rm GeV},
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
m_T^Z<150\ {\rm GeV}.
\label{eq:z1j-saturated-condition}
\end{equation}
For $m_T^Z\sim100$ GeV, for example, the interval extends only from roughly
$10^{-3}$ GeV to $1.5\times10^{-3}$ GeV. Hence the current setup does not expose a logarithmically large
finite-$z$ region spanning the two decades that a naive comparison of $10^{-5}$ and $10^{-3}$ would suggest.
At larger $m_T^Z$ the ratio in Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-cut-ratio} decreases and the higher cut enforces a progressively
stronger restriction on $z$ already near the lower boundary.

The event sample is defined with the generation cut
\begin{equation}
q_T>q_T^{\rm gen}=10^{-3}\ {\rm GeV}.
\label{eq:z1j-generation-cut}
\end{equation}
Both the FKS and the $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ mappings considered here preserve $q_T$, and therefore preserve
this process-defining boundary exactly. The observed LHE differences cannot consequently be attributed to
migration across the generation cut.

The direct comparison of mappings is particularly informative. For
$\mathcal T_2^{\cut}=10^{-3}\ {\rm GeV}$ the LHE differences obtained with the FKS mapping are much smaller than
those obtained with the $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ mapping for observables involving the hardest anti-$k_T$ jet,
including analyses with $p_{T,j}^{\min}=10$, $50$ and $100$ GeV. For both mappings the differences grow when
$\mathcal T_2^{\cut}$ is increased, becoming much larger for $\mathcal T_2^{\cut}=1\ {\rm GeV}$, but the
$\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ residual remains substantially larger even at the smallest cut. Since the two mappings
are exposed to the same anti-$k_T$ clustering and threshold boundaries and preserve the same generation cut,
this shows that the large residual is map dependent. The jet measurement can amplify the migration, but cannot
by itself be its primary origin.

The numerical hierarchy alone would not establish a formal failure of uniform nested infrared locality.
However, the explicit mapping formulas allow this question to be settled analytically, as follows.

\paragraph{Analytic diagnosis of the mapping dependence.}
The relevant distinction is between ordinary one-step locality, which tests $\mathcal T_2\to0$ at fixed
nonzero $\mathcal T_1$, and the uniform nested condition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform-nested-locality-definition},
which takes $\mathcal T_1/Q\to0$ at fixed finite $z=\mathcal T_2/\mathcal T_1$.  The standard FKS mapping and
the $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ mapping are both one-step infrared local.  They differ, however, in how the
recoil behaves when the lower and higher unresolved limits are approached simultaneously.

The difference can be exposed explicitly in the ISR$\times$FSR sector of the $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ map.
In the colour-singlet frame define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_{12}
\equiv
\hat p_{12}^0-\lvert\hat{\boldsymbol p}_{12}\rvert,
\qquad
R_{12}
\equiv
\hat p_{12}^0+\lvert\hat{\boldsymbol p}_{12}\rvert.
\label{eq:z1j-t1fr-t12-R12}
\end{equation}
The explicit ISR$\times$FSR construction has
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_2=\hat p_3^{\pm},
\qquad
\mathcal T_1=\mathcal T_{12}+\mathcal T_2,
\label{eq:z1j-t1fr-isrfsr-jettiness}
\end{equation}
and imposes on the projected configuration
\begin{equation}
\bar q=q,
\qquad
\bar p_{12}^{\,2}=p_{12}^{\,2},
\qquad
\bar{\mathcal T}_{12}=\mathcal T_1,
\label{eq:z1j-t1fr-isrfsr-map}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\mathcal T}_{12}=\hat{\bar p}_{12}^0-\lvert\hat{\boldsymbol{\bar p}}_{12}\rvert$.
Since
\begin{equation}
p_{12}^2=\mathcal T_{12}R_{12},
\qquad
\bar p_{12}^{\,2}=\bar{\mathcal T}_{12}\bar R_{12},
\label{eq:z1j-t1fr-mass-factorization}
\end{equation}
and at fixed $z$
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_{12}
=\mathcal T_1-\mathcal T_2
=(1-z)\mathcal T_1,
\label{eq:z1j-t1fr-t12-fixed-z}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-t1fr-isrfsr-map} implies the exact relation
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\bar R_{12}=(1-z)R_{12}
}.
\label{eq:z1j-t1fr-R12-relation}
\end{equation}
This is the crucial result.  In the simultaneous unresolved limit the cluster $p_{12}$ approaches the hard
one-jet momentum, so $R_{12}\to R_{12}^{(0)}=\mathcal O(Q)$ rather than zero.  Hence, along any allowed path
with fixed nonzero $z$,
\begin{equation}
\bar R_{12}-R_{12}
\longrightarrow
-zR_{12}^{(0)}
=\mathcal O(Q),
\label{eq:z1j-t1fr-hard-mismatch}
\end{equation}
and the projected and exact configurations approach different points of the one-jet Born manifold.  The
ISR$\times$FSR sector therefore violates uniform nested infrared locality.  This does not contradict its
ordinary infrared locality: if the strongly ordered limit $z\to0$ is taken first,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-t1fr-R12-relation} gives $\bar R_{12}/R_{12}\to1$.

This failure can be made directly observable with the smooth Born coordinate given by the jet rapidity $y_J$.
Exact preservation of $q^\mu$ fixes the same $Y_Z$ and $q_T$ in the exact and projected configurations.  In
the common massless one-jet limit, the common transverse factor therefore cancels in the ratio
\(\bar R_J/R_J\), and Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-t1fr-R12-relation} gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\cosh(\bar y_J-Y_Z)}{\cosh(y_J-Y_Z)}
\longrightarrow
1-z
\qquad (\mathcal T_1/Q\to0,\ z\text{ fixed}).
\label{eq:z1j-t1fr-rapidity-relation}
\end{equation}
On the rapidity branch continuously connected to the exact configuration as $z\to0$, this implies the direct
limiting shift
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Delta y_J
\equiv
y_J-\bar y_J
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{sgn}(y_J-Y_Z)
\left[
|y_J-Y_Z|
-
\operatorname{arcosh}\!\left((1-z)\cosh|y_J-Y_Z|\right)
\right]
}.
\label{eq:z1j-t1fr-rapidity-shift}
\end{equation}
The mapping exists only where the projected rapidity is real, namely where
$(1-z)\cosh|y_J-Y_Z|\ge1$.  This condition leaves a finite allowed region, and for every fixed nonzero $z$
in that region Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-t1fr-rapidity-shift} gives a finite nonzero shift directly in $y_J$.  A
single such family is sufficient to disprove uniform nested locality.  By contrast, when the strongly ordered
limit $z\to0$ is taken first, the bracket vanishes and $\Delta y_J\to0$, consistently with ordinary one-step
locality.

This also explains why the failure can be invisible in some observables.  The $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ map
preserves $q^\mu$ exactly in the explicit sectors considered here, so $q_T$, $Y_Z$ and the colour-singlet
invariant mass are blind to the nonlocal tangential recoil.  Moreover, the leading-jet transverse momentum
is asymptotically protected by $p_{T,J}=q_T$ in the one-jet limit.  By contrast, $y_J$, the jet energy and
$\mathcal T_0$ can retain a finite displacement.  A map can therefore fail the map-level criterion of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform-nested-locality-definition} while still being perfectly local for a restricted set of
observables.

Inspection of the other explicit $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ sectors does not reveal an analogous hard
$\mathcal O(Q)$ recoil.  In ISR$\times$ISR the finite changes are carried by momenta that themselves become
lower-unresolved; in FSR$\times$same-ISR and FSR$\times$different-ISR the light-cone components of the hard
FSR cluster are inherited directly while the remaining displacement is attached to the lower-unresolved
parton.  The FSR$\times$FSR sector is stronger still: the map identifies the projected hard parent with the
full pre-projection parent, $\hat{\bar p}_{12}=\hat p_{123}$.  Thus these sectors are compatible with uniform
nested locality in regular hard regions.  Nevertheless, the single ISR$\times$FSR counterexample is enough
to show that the $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ map does not possess the property globally.

The standard FKS construction behaves differently.  For an initial-state singular region the recoil of the
hard final state is generated by longitudinal and transverse boosts whose deviation from the identity is
controlled by the transverse momentum of the unresolved emission.  For a final-state singular region the
recoil system is likewise boosted so that the clustered parent becomes massless, and that boost tends to the
identity as the parent virtuality vanishes.  Consequently, when $\mathcal T_1/Q\to0$ and
$z=\mathcal T_2/\mathcal T_1$ is kept finite, the displacement of any smooth hard Born coordinate vanishes
with a positive power of the common unresolved scale, provided one stays away from additional singular or
phase-space boundaries.  In the regular regions relevant here, the FKS map therefore satisfies uniform nested
infrared locality.  Exact preservation of $\mathcal T_1$ is not required for this statement: FKS may change
$\mathcal T_1$ by an amount comparable to $\mathcal T_1$ itself while both exact and projected values vanish.
The resolution variable measures the distance normal to the Born manifold, whereas nested locality requires
the finite coordinates along that manifold to agree.

\medskip
\noindent
\fbox{%
\parbox{0.94\linewidth}{%
\textbf{Mapping conclusion for $Z+1$ jet.}
The $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ map is ordinarily local in the $\mathcal T_2\to0$ limit and becomes local again
in the strongly ordered $z\to0$ limit, but it is not uniformly nested-local because its ISR$\times$FSR sector
contains the finite hard recoil in Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-t1fr-R12-relation}.  The FKS map has no analogous finite
hard recoil and is uniformly nested-local in the regular hard regions relevant to the comparison.  Large
anti-$k_T$ effects must still be kept conceptually separate: clustering, ordering and $p_T$-threshold
boundaries can amplify a small displacement for either map, but such observable nonuniformity is not itself a
failure of map locality.
}%
}
\medskip

The explicit result above sharpens the interpretation of the numerical hierarchy.  The much smaller FKS
residual is consistent with a genuinely nested-local recoil, while the larger $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ effect
has an analytic source that survives at the map level when $\mathcal T_1$ and $\mathcal T_2$ approach zero
with finite ratio.  The event-by-event diagnostics in Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-local-diagnostics} remain valuable as
a direct numerical confirmation: the ISR$\times$FSR contribution should show a plateau in a sensitive smooth
coordinate such as $\Delta y_{j_1}$ or $\Delta\mathcal T_0/Q$ at fixed finite $z$, whereas FKS should show a
positive-power fall away from measurement boundaries.

A complementary cut study then has a more precise interpretation. Keeping the dynamical
$\mathcal T_1^{\cut}=10^{-5}m_T^Z$ fixed while lowering the higher cut through, for example,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_2^{\cut}
=
10^{-3},\ 5\times10^{-4},\ 2\times10^{-4},\ 10^{-4}\ {\rm GeV},
\label{eq:z1j-t2cut-scan}
\end{equation}
progressively enforces the strongly ordered regime and should therefore reduce the $\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$
problem.  Such a scan tests ordinary strong-ordering convergence, but by itself does not establish uniform
nested locality.  The cleaner test is to lower the two cuts together at fixed ratio,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_2^{\cut}
=\kappa\,\mathcal T_1^{\cut}
=\kappa\,10^{-5}m_T^Z,
\label{eq:z1j-correlated-cut-scan}
\end{equation}
with fixed $\kappa$.  For a uniformly nested-local map such as FKS, hard-coordinate mismatches vanish with the
common unresolved scale at fixed finite $\kappa$.  In the ISR$\times$FSR sector of the
$\mathcal T_1^{\rm FR}$ map, by contrast, Eq.~\eqref{eq:z1j-t1fr-R12-relation} shows that the local hard
mismatch has no positive-power suppression in the common scale at fixed finite $z$.  Integrated observables
can still exhibit cancellations or additional enhancement from jet boundaries, so the local event-by-event
test remains the cleanest diagnostic of the map property.

\section{Pure fixed-order slicing and nested sliced calculations}
\label{sec:fixed-order-nested-slicing}

The discussion now changes problem. Sections~\ref{sec:higher-singular-spectrum} and
\ref{sec:nnlo-differential-predictions} concerned the replacement of an exact configuration by a projected
\emph{physical LHE event}. In a pure fixed-order calculation projections play a different role: they are
auxiliary devices used to organize subtraction or to evaluate a truncated power-expanded approximation below
a slicing cut. If exact and projected terms are kept correlated, kinematic information lost by the projection
can in principle be restored. What cannot be restored in this way are dynamical terms that were not retained
in the below-cut approximation itself.

It is therefore useful to separate from the outset two conceptually different sources of finite-cut error,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Delta_{\rm slice}^{(\rn)}
=
\Delta_{\rm dyn,trunc}^{(\rn)}
+
\Delta_{\rm kin}^{(\rn)}
}
\label{eq:fixed-order-pc-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_{\rm dyn,trunc}^{(\rn)}$ is the \emph{dynamical truncation remainder}, namely the part of the
exact dynamics that is not included in the approximation retained below the cut, while
$\Delta_{\rm kin}^{(\rn)}$ is the error induced by evaluating the measurement on projected
lower-multiplicity kinematics. Projection-to-Born can restore the second contribution, but not the first.

\subsection{What a slicing cut does in a pure fixed-order NNLO calculation}
\label{subsec:fixed-order-nnlo-slicing}

Below the NNLO slicing cut $\rn^{\cut}$ one replaces the exact lower-multiplicity contribution by some
power-expanded dynamical approximation. We denote the approximation actually retained by
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\Sigma_{N,\rm dyn}^{\rm appr}}{d\Ph_N}(\rn^{\cut}).
\label{eq:nnlo-dynamical-approximation}
\end{equation}
In the most common implementation this is simply the LP factorization theorem,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\Sigma_{N,\rm dyn}^{\rm appr}}{d\Ph_N}
=
\frac{d\Sigma_{N,\rm LP}^{\rm NNLO}}{d\Ph_N},
\label{eq:nnlo-lp-special-case}
\end{equation}
but this is not part of the definition. More generally the approximation may also contain known NLP, NNLP,
or still higher power-suppressed dynamical contributions. Schematically one may think of
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{N,\rm dyn}^{\rm appr}
=
\Sigma_{N,\rm LP}
+\Sigma_{N,\rm NLP}
+\Sigma_{N,\rm NNLP}
+\cdots,
\label{eq:nnlo-dynamical-power-expansion}
\end{equation}
where only the terms actually known and retained are to be included in the right-hand side.

The appropriate dynamical error is therefore not intrinsically ``exact minus LP'', but rather
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm dyn,trunc}^{(\rn)}
=
\frac{d\Sigma_N^{\rm NNLO}}{d\Ph_N}(\rn^{\cut})
-
\frac{d\Sigma_{N,\rm dyn}^{\rm appr}}{d\Ph_N}(\rn^{\cut}).
\label{eq:dynamic-truncation-remainder}
\end{equation}
If all dynamical terms through a given power have been included, the remainder starts at the first omitted
power, together with its associated logarithmic structure. If that first omitted contribution scales with
power $q_{\rm miss}>0$, one expects schematically
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm dyn,trunc}^{(\rn)}
\sim
\left(\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{Q}\right)^{q_{\rm miss}}
\sum_k a_k(\Ph_N)\ln^k\!\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{Q}.
\label{eq:dynamic-truncation-scaling}
\end{equation}
In the common LP-only case, Eq.~\eqref{eq:dynamic-truncation-remainder} reduces to the usual difference between
the exact result and its LP approximation, and $q_{\rm miss}$ characterizes the leading omitted
power-suppressed dynamics, conventionally referred to as the NLP contribution. If complete NLP information
is also included, the remainder instead begins at NNLP, and analogously at higher orders. If only a subset of
the terms at a given nominal power is retained, $q_{\rm miss}$ should be understood as the smallest scaling
among the still omitted contributions.

This dynamical truncation remainder is present even for a completely inclusive measurement of the Born
variables. It is not caused by a recoil prescription or by a mismatch between exact and projected kinematics,
and no P2B correction can reconstruct it. Reducing it requires taking a smaller slicing cut, calculating
additional power-suppressed dynamical terms, or otherwise improving the below-cut dynamical approximation.

There is separately a kinematic power correction whenever exact higher-multiplicity kinematics are replaced
inside the measurement by a projection,
\begin{equation}
\Mcal(\Ph_{N+1})\longrightarrow
\Mcal\!\left(\Pi_N(\Ph_{N+1})\right).
\label{eq:fixed-order-lower-measurement-projection}
\end{equation}
This part is observable dependent and includes ordinary fiducial power corrections. Unlike
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dynamic-truncation-remainder}, it can be restored at fixed order by keeping the exact and
projected measurements correlated.

\subsection{Outer $\mathrm{N}^3\mathrm{LO}$ slicing and an embedded sliced NNLO ingredient}
\label{subsec:n3lo-embedding}

A pure fixed-order $\mathrm{N}^3\mathrm{LO}$ calculation sliced directly in $\rnm$ already has the same two
classes of finite-cut effects associated with its \emph{outer} cut,
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm slice}^{(\rnm)}
=
\Delta_{\rm dyn,trunc}^{(\rnm)}
+
\Delta_{\rm kin}^{(\rnm)}.
\label{eq:n3lo-outer-pc-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Here $\Delta_{\rm dyn,trunc}^{(\rnm)}$ is defined with respect to the dynamical approximation actually retained
below the outer cut. This approximation is very often LP only, but it may in principle include one or more
subleading-power terms. The remainder begins at the first omitted dynamical power. By contrast,
$\Delta_{\rm kin}^{(\rnm)}$ is caused by dropping exact kinematic dependence below the outer cut. At fixed
order a suitable P2B construction can restore the latter, including fiducial migrations, but it cannot restore
the former. This statement is independent of how the resolved NNLO ingredient is computed.

There is then a \emph{second}, nested issue if the NNLO ingredient required above the outer cut is itself
obtained from a slicing calculation. Consider an outer $\mathrm{N}^3\mathrm{LO}$ calculation sliced in
$\rnm$. Its resolved region requires an NNLO prediction for multiplicity $N$ that is differential in the
outer resolution variable and in the Born-like coordinates,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma_N^{\rm NNLO}}
{d\rnm\,d\mathbf X}.
\label{eq:nnlo-ingredient-for-n3lo}
\end{equation}
Suppose this NNLO ingredient is itself obtained with a slicing calculation in $\rn$. Then the relevant
internal approximation is the \emph{lower-multiplicity NNLO cut} $\rn^{\cut}$. It is this cut that can
contaminate the singular spectrum in $\rnm$ needed by the outer calculation.

By contrast, an additional $\rnp^{\cut}$ used only to organize the exact NLO $(N+1)$ contribution inside the
embedded NNLO calculation is not a new fixed-order slicing approximation. When both sides of that partition
are treated with exact kinematics and the local and integrated subtraction terms are combined consistently,
its dependence cancels. Thus, for a pure fixed-order nested calculation, the potentially problematic
neighboring hierarchy is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\rn^{\cut}\quad\hbox{versus}\quad \rnm,
}
\label{eq:relevant-inner-outer-hierarchy}
\end{equation}
not $\rnp^{\cut}$ versus $\rn$. The latter becomes a physical issue only when the projected
$\widehat\Ph_{N+1}$ configuration is retained as an LHE event, as in Secs.~\ref{sec:higher-singular-spectrum}
and \ref{sec:nnlo-differential-predictions}.

For the outer slicing to inherit the correct singular structure, the sliced NNLO ingredient must reproduce
Eq.~\eqref{eq:nnlo-ingredient-for-n3lo} with an error that is sufficiently suppressed as $\rnm\to0$. Both
parts of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixed-order-pc-decomposition} must therefore be examined in the nested limit. P2B can
remove the kinematic part; the dynamical truncation remainder remains and may itself become nonuniform if the
scale controlling the first omitted inner contribution is set by $\rnm$ rather than by a fixed hard scale.

\subsection{Toy model for the locality of the embedded subtraction}
\label{subsec:nested-slicing-toy-model}

The original locality issue is most transparent in a simple toy model. In the lower unresolved region of
the embedded NNLO calculation, take the singular contribution schematically as
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma_{N+1}^{\rm sing}}
{d\rn\,d\rnm\,d\mathbf X}
\sim
\frac{1}{\rn}
F\!\left(\rnm,\mathbf X,
\ln\frac{\rn}{Q},\ldots\right).
\label{eq:nested-toy-singular}
\end{equation}
The outer calculation needs the dependence on $\rnm$ and $\mathbf X$ to remain correct. Define the
exact-minus-projected displacements
\begin{align}
\Delta\rnm(\Ph_{N+1})
&=
\rnm(\Ph_{N+1})
-
\rnm\!\left(\Pi_N(\Ph_{N+1})\right),
\label{eq:delta-rnm}
\\
\Delta X_i(\Ph_{N+1})
&=
X_i(\Ph_{N+1})
-
X_i\!\left(\Pi_N(\Ph_{N+1})\right).
\label{eq:delta-x-lower-map}
\end{align}
Linearizing the measurement mismatch gives two distinct migration operators. After integrating the inner
unresolved region, the kinematic contribution has the schematic form
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\rm kin}^{(\rn)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rnm\,d\mathbf X}
\sim{}&
\partial_{\rnm}
\int_0^{\rn^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}
\left[F\,\Delta\rnm\right]
\nonumber\\
&+
\sum_i
\partial_{X_i}
\int_0^{\rn^{\cut}}
\frac{d\rn}{\rn}
\left[F\,\Delta X_i\right].
\label{eq:nested-toy-kinematic-migration}
\end{align}
The first line is the direct statement of locality of the inner subtraction with respect to the singular
outer variable $\rnm$. The second line shows that preserving the singular variable alone is not sufficient
for a fully differential prediction: migrations in Born-like variables also modify the coefficient of the
$\rnm$ singular spectrum. If an $X_i$ is integrated over its full range, its derivative term reduces to a
boundary contribution and can disappear; the $\rnm$ dependence, however, is intrinsic to the outer slicing
problem.

In a strongly ordered region
\begin{equation}
\rn\ll\rnm\ll Q,
\label{eq:lower-strong-ordering}
\end{equation}
let a generic kinematic displacement $Y_a$ obey
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{y_a}
\equiv
\frac{\Delta Y_a}{Y_{a,\rm char}}
\sim
\left(\frac{\rn}{\rnm}\right)^p,
\qquad p>0,
\label{eq:nested-toy-kinematic-scaling}
\end{equation}
where $Y_a$ stands for either $\rnm$ or one of the $X_i$. Then Eq.~\eqref{eq:nested-toy-kinematic-migration}
contains the neighboring-scale behavior
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm kin}^{(\rn)}
\sim
\left(\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{\rnm}\right)^p
\times\hbox{derivatives of the lower singular spectrum},
\label{eq:nested-toy-kinematic-ratio}
\end{equation}
up to logarithms and finite coefficients. Hence a cut that is harmless at fixed nonzero $\rnm$ can become
nonuniform as the outer singular limit is approached. When the outer calculation itself probes
$\rnm\sim\rnm^{\cut}$, the natural ordered expansion parameter becomes
$\rn^{\cut}/\rnm^{\cut}$.

The same neighboring-scale issue can occur in the dynamical truncation remainder, but with a different origin.
Let $d\sigma^{\rm dyn,appr}$ denote the inner singular dynamics that are actually retained below the cut. If
the first omitted contribution behaves locally as
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma^{\rm exact}-d\sigma^{\rm dyn,appr}}
{d\rn\,d\rnm\,d\mathbf X}
\sim
\frac{1}{\rn}
\left(\frac{\rn}{\rnm}\right)^{q_{\rm miss}}
D(\rnm,\mathbf X,\ldots),
\qquad q_{\rm miss}>0,
\label{eq:nested-toy-dynamic-local}
\end{equation}
then integration below $\rn^{\cut}$ gives parametrically
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm dyn,trunc}^{(\rn)}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\rnm\,d\mathbf X}
\sim
\left(\frac{\rn^{\cut}}{\rnm}\right)^{q_{\rm miss}}
D(\rnm,\mathbf X,\ldots),
\label{eq:nested-toy-dynamic-ratio}
\end{equation}
again up to logarithms. In an LP-only inner slicing calculation $q_{\rm miss}$ characterizes the first
subleading dynamical term. If additional powers are already included, it instead characterizes the first
remaining omitted contribution. This term is not a migration derivative and cannot be generated by changing
the measurement function. It is therefore outside the reach of P2B.

\subsection{What P2B restores, and what it cannot restore}
\label{subsec:p2b-scope}

At fixed order, the kinematic term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixed-order-pc-decomposition} can be restored through a
projection-to-Born construction. Schematically, suppressing the detailed subtraction structure,
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\rm P2B}^{(\rn)}[\Mcal]
\sim
\int d\Ph_{N+1}\,
w_{\rm exact}(\Ph_{N+1})
\left[
\Mcal(\Ph_{N+1})-
\Mcal\!\left(\Pi_N(\Ph_{N+1})\right)
\right].
\label{eq:p2b-fpc-correlated}
\end{equation}
The two measurements are evaluated on the same exact higher-multiplicity phase-space point and carry
correlated weights. This exactly targets the kinematic information lost by the projection: fiducial
migrations, recoil effects, and the derivative terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:nested-toy-kinematic-migration}.
Within the perturbative order for which the exact higher-multiplicity ingredient is available, these
kinematic power corrections can therefore be recovered at fixed order.

P2B does \emph{not} improve the dynamical approximation retained below $\rn^{\cut}$. It cannot supply the
missing term $D$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:nested-toy-dynamic-local}, and hence cannot remove
$\Delta_{\rm dyn,trunc}^{(\rn)}$. This statement is independent of whether the below-cut approximation is LP
only or already contains NLP, NNLP, or higher power-suppressed dynamics: P2B cannot generate the first omitted
dynamical term. After a P2B restoration, the residual slicing dependence associated with the below-cut
dynamics is therefore the dynamical truncation remainder. Controlling it requires a sufficiently small inner
cut or explicit information about the still omitted power-suppressed terms.

This distinction is especially important for an embedded NNLO calculation. Without P2B, both
$\Delta_{\rm kin}^{(\rn)}$ and $\Delta_{\rm dyn,trunc}^{(\rn)}$ must be sufficiently local in the outer
singular variable. With P2B, the kinematic part can be restored, but the dynamical truncation remainder must
still reproduce the required $\rnm$ singular spectrum to the accuracy needed by the outer
$\mathrm{N}^3\mathrm{LO}$ subtraction.
\subsection{Optional lower boundaries}
\label{sec:born-boundaries}

A process-defining lower boundary is an additional kinematic issue, not a new dynamical one. If, for
example,
\begin{equation}
\Theta_B(\Ph_M)=
\Theta\!\left(\rnm(\Ph_M)-\rnm^{\cut}\right),
\label{eq:active-born-boundary}
\end{equation}
then a lower projection can move an event across that boundary. For a small displacement,
\begin{equation}
\Theta\!\left(\rnm(\Pi_N\Ph_{N+1})-\rnm^{\cut}\right)
-
\Theta\!\left(\rnm(\Ph_{N+1})-\rnm^{\cut}\right)
\simeq
-\Delta\rnm\,
\delta\!\left(\rnm(\Ph_{N+1})-\rnm^{\cut}\right).
\label{eq:boundary-expansion}
\end{equation}
At fixed order this migration belongs to $\Delta_{\rm kin}$ and can be included in the correlated P2B
restoration. If no such lower boundary is part of the process definition, this term is absent; the separate
requirement of reproducing the differential $\rnm$ spectrum remains.

\subsection{Auxiliary subtraction maps versus physical event projections}
\label{subsec:fixed-order-does-not-require-uniform-nested-locality}

Uniform nested infrared locality is stronger than what is required of an auxiliary map used only inside the NLO part above the cut for a
fixed-order NNLO slicing calculation. A concrete counterexample can be constructed by starting from any ordinary one-step
IR-local map \(\Pi_{N+1}^{(0)}\), and composing it with a smooth recoil transformation
\(\mathcal R_\alpha\) acting on the projected \((N+1)\)-body state,
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{N+1}^{\rm bad}
=
\mathcal R_{\alpha(x,y)}\circ\Pi_{N+1}^{(0)},
\qquad
x=\frac{\rn}{Q},
\qquad
y=\frac{\rnp}{Q},
\qquad
\alpha(x,y)=\frac{y}{x+y}.
\label{eq:bad-map-definition}
\end{equation}
Assume that \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) preserves the on-shell and momentum-conservation constraints of the
\((N+1)\)-body phase space and changes one smooth lower-multiplicity coordinate \(Y\) according to
\begin{equation}
Y\!\left(\mathcal R_\alpha(\Ph_{N+1})\right)
=
Y(\Ph_{N+1})
+
\alpha\,Y_{\rm char}
+
\mathcal O(\alpha^2),
\label{eq:bad-map-recoil}
\end{equation}
with a finite nonsingular scale \(Y_{\rm char}\). At fixed nonzero \(x\),
\begin{equation}
y\to0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha(x,y)\to0,
\label{eq:bad-map-one-step-local}
\end{equation}
so \(\Pi_{N+1}^{\rm bad}\) has exactly the same one-step \(N+2\to N+1\) infrared limit as
\(\Pi_{N+1}^{(0)}\). It is therefore perfectly admissible from the point of view of local NLO subtraction,
provided the usual phase-space factorization and invertibility requirements are satisfied.

In the simultaneous unresolved limit, however, taking \(y=z x\) gives
\begin{equation}
\alpha(x,zx)=\frac{z}{1+z},
\label{eq:bad-map-simultaneous-alpha}
\end{equation}
which remains finite as \(x\to0\) for any fixed nonzero \(z\). Since lowercase $y$ already labels the
resolution ratio in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bad-map-definition}, we write the normalized $Y$-migration explicitly
here rather than denoting it by \(\Delta_y\):
\begin{equation}
\frac{
Y(\Ph_{N+2})
-Y\!\left(\Pi_{N+1}^{\rm bad}(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
}{Y_{\rm char}}
\longrightarrow
-\frac{z}{1+z}
\label{eq:bad-map-violates-nested-locality}
\end{equation}
up to the convention for the sign of the recoil. The map is one-step IR local but violates uniform nested
infrared locality maximally: exact and projected configurations approach different points of the limiting
\(N\)-body phase space along a finite-\(z\) path.

This does not spoil the fixed-order NLO calculation of the \((N+1)\)-body contribution that enters an NNLO
calculation. Schematically, for \(\rn>\rn^{\cut}\), one has
\begin{align}
\sigma_{N+1}^{\rm NLO}[\Ocal]
={}&
\int_{\rn>\rn^{\cut}}d\Ph_{N+2}
\Bigl[
R_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})\Ocal(\Ph_{N+2})
-
C_{N+2}(\Ph_{N+2})
\Ocal\!\left(\Pi_{N+1}^{\rm bad}(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\Bigr]
\nonumber\\
&+
\int_{\rn>\rn^{\cut}}d\Ph_{N+1}
\Bigl[V_{N+1}+I_{N+1}^{\rm bad}\Bigr](\Ph_{N+1})
\Ocal(\Ph_{N+1}),
\label{eq:bad-map-subtracted-nlo}
\end{align}
where \(I_{N+1}^{\rm bad}\) is obtained by integrating the same counterterm with the same map. The counterterm
has only been added and subtracted. For every fixed nonzero \(\rn^{\cut}\), ordinary one-step locality is
sufficient to make the real-minus-counterterm contribution locally integrable in the
\(\rnp\to0\) limit. The map dependence cancels between the subtracted real contribution and its integrated
counterpart, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:bad-map-subtracted-nlo} is the exact NLO result independently of the bad
simultaneous-limit behavior of \(\Pi_{N+1}^{\rm bad}\).

Embedding this exact NLO contribution in a sliced NNLO calculation therefore does not require the higher map
to be uniformly nested-local. The lower region \(\rn<\rn^{\cut}\) is supplied by the separate
lower-multiplicity NNLO contribution, while the higher-multiplicity NLO calculation only requires the
ordinary \(\rnp\to0\) cancellation at fixed resolved \(\rn>\rn^{\cut}\). Individual subtraction pieces may
develop increasingly large and numerically delicate cancellations as \(\rn^{\cut}\) is reduced, but this is
a subtraction-scheme issue rather than a loss of formal NNLO accuracy.

The situation changes when the projected \((N+1)\)-body configuration is used as the physical representative
of the event below \(\rnp^{\cut}\). In that case the replacement
\begin{equation}
\Ocal(\Ph_{N+2})
\longrightarrow
\Ocal\!\left(\Pi_{N+1}(\Ph_{N+2})\right)
\label{eq:physical-projection-replacement}
\end{equation}
is part of the event definition. There is no compensating integrated counterterm that restores the discarded
measurement on the exact \(\Ph_{N+2}\) kinematics. A failure of uniform nested locality therefore becomes a
genuine projection effect in the LHE event sample even though the same map could be entirely legitimate as
an auxiliary fixed-order subtraction map. This is the precise sense in which the mapping requirements for
GENEVA event generation are stronger than those required for formal fixed-order NNLO accuracy.

The counterexample above also explains why the higher $\rnp^{\cut}$ projection must not be imported into the
fixed-order nesting discussion. As long as $\Pi_{N+1}$ is used only inside the add--subtract organization of
the exact NLO $(N+1)$ contribution, its auxiliary map dependence cancels. The stronger simultaneous-limit
condition derived in Sec.~\ref{subsec:uniform-nested-locality} becomes physically relevant only when the
projected configuration is retained as an event, not when it is merely an internal subtraction coordinate.

\section{Summary: three distinct uses of cuts and projections}

The main conclusions are most clearly organized by separating three applications.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{NNLO LHE event definition.}
The higher separation $\rnp^{\cut}$ sits inside a dynamically exact NLO $(N+1)$ contribution. It generates
no fixed-order dynamical truncation. A residual dependence appears only when exact $\Ph_{N+2}$ kinematics
below the cut are replaced by a projected $\widehat\Ph_{N+1}$ configuration that is retained as the physical
LHE event. The relevant questions are then uniform nested locality of $\Pi_{N+1}$, migration of Born-like
variables $X_i$, and nonuniform observable response near shoulders, jet-clustering boundaries or fiducial cuts.

\item \textbf{Pure fixed-order NNLO slicing.}
The lower cut $\rn^{\cut}$ introduces a dynamical approximation below the cut, but that approximation need
not stop at LP. Its finite-cut error naturally splits as
\[
\Delta_{\rm slice}^{(\rn)}
=
\Delta_{\rm dyn,trunc}^{(\rn)}+
\Delta_{\rm kin}^{(\rn)}.
\]
The kinematic part is the loss of exact measurement dependence under the lower projection and can be restored
with a correlated P2B construction. The dynamical truncation remainder consists of the power-suppressed
dynamics not retained below the cut and cannot be reconstructed by P2B. In the usual LP-only slicing
calculation this reduces to the familiar exact-minus-LP remainder; if NLP, NNLP, or higher terms are included,
the remainder starts at the first still omitted contribution.

\item \textbf{Pure fixed-order $\mathrm{N}^3\mathrm{LO}$ slicing and a sliced NNLO ingredient.}
The outer $\rnm^{\cut}$ slicing has its own dynamical truncation remainder and kinematic power corrections,
exactly as at NNLO: P2B can restore the outer kinematic part but not the omitted dynamics. If the resolved
NNLO ingredient is itself sliced, an additional nested requirement appears. The internal cut that can
contaminate the lower singular spectrum is $\rn^{\cut}$, the lower-multiplicity cut of the embedded NNLO
calculation. In a strongly ordered nested region the relevant parameters can be $\rn^{\cut}/\rnm$ and, near
the outer boundary, $\rn^{\cut}/\rnm^{\cut}$. Kinematic contamination from the inner slicing can again be
removed by P2B; the dynamical truncation remainder remains and must be controlled by the inner cut or by
including further power-suppressed dynamics. The still higher $\rnp^{\cut}$ used only inside the exact NLO
component is not an additional fixed-order nesting error.
\end{enumerate}

The same projection map can therefore be perfectly adequate as an auxiliary fixed-order subtraction map and
yet be inadequate as a physical LHE event map. Conversely, restoring exact kinematics with P2B can remove
all projection-induced kinematic power corrections at fixed order without curing the dynamical truncation
remainder of the slicing expansion. In the common LP-only implementation this remainder is precisely what is
usually referred to as the genuine dynamical power correction. Keeping these distinctions explicit is
essential when several nested resolution variables are used in GENEVA.
